# Round 2, Growth: attack

## 1. Candidate A can be sold, but it doesn't spread
- **Cadence.** FWB runs twice a year, plus Queens. [FACT] R4 §3 counts about 40 slots a season and a ceiling of about 1,000 credited people a year. A's creative-side "loop" is *forced registration*: you sign up because the open call requires it. That is acquisition with no retention, and 26 weeks later the cohort is cold.
- **Organizers spread through sales, not through use.** A walk-on model never becomes an event organizer, so the only route to the next organizer is founders selling. [FACT] Money's own pricing ($20/slot, about $800 a season for FWB) shows what the anchor is worth. [ASSUMPTION] NYC has dozens of recurring selection events, not thousands. A is a sales-led tool business, which is The Dots' white-label endpoint (R1 §7) reached on purpose.
- **Fidelity.** A record that updates twice a year, through someone else's event, is not "living." A gives up the founders' goal and keeps the name.

## 2. Attacks on named delegates (my round-1 allies)
- **Founder:** *"≥40% of credited participants claim their profile within 14 days."* That measures a vanity act, clicking a link to see your own name, and says nothing about return. The test is *a non-founder creates a production unrelated to FWB*.
- **Competition:** *"5–10 indie magazines pushed off Kavyar's free tier."* [FACT] (R4 §2, Kavyar help) Those publishers charge non-refundable submission fees. An issuer that was price-sensitive enough to leave a $99/mo tool will not pay us. One that charges creatives $40 to be published turns "ISSUED" into pay-to-publish laundering, which is trust's R3 vector. It is the worst issuer pool proposed.
- **Trust:** *ID verification is "required to confirm anyone else's credit."* Putting a roughly $1 Persona check and a liveness selfie in front of the *accept* tap kills the invite loop at its first step. Gate *issuing* and *paid exposure* with ID. Let acceptance carry a plain provenance sentence ("Confirmed by @handle, not ID-checked") instead.
- **Creative:** *"invited people confirm with one tap and no account."* Right for acceptance, wrong for compounding: a confirmation tied to no account adds no node and nothing to return to. The tap must create a claimable stub (phone or handle) where the next invite lands.

## 4. Revised position
**I hold B as the engine. I change four things.**
1. **One object, not two products.** A FWB show is a *production with an issuer flag*. A's desk builds the sheet and FWB's "publish" makes it ISSUED. A becomes B's launch event, not a separate SaaS: the majority's best asset, a credible issuer with a real roster, now feeds a loop.
2. **I adopt C's freshness decay as the model's second-use trigger.** [BET] "Digitals taken 9 days ago" ages into "taken 4 months ago." Staleness prompts re-capture with no hirer traffic needed. Producers return for the next shoot; models return to refresh.
3. **The ID gate moves to issuers, issuer signatories and paid exposure,** not to acceptance (see the trust attack above).
4. **Sequencing changes because of my evidence gap.** Before writing code: 20 interviews plus shadowing 10 real NYC test or editorial shoots. Kill B if fewer than 6 of 10 shoots show a lead manually gathering roles and handles across 4 or more people. That is cheaper than building and finding out.

The first-100 plan and round-1 kill criteria stand.

**Conceded to money/vc:** B's revenue is weak (scored 3). The payer is the organizer either way: events at A's per-slot price, and [BET] a producer tier for anyone running 4 or more productions a month. Neither touches money between parties.
