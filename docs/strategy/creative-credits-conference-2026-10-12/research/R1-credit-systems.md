## 1. IMDb / IMDbPro

**What it is.** IMDb is the de facto public credit record for film and TV, owned by Amazon. IMDbPro is the paid layer for industry professionals and launched at Sundance in 2002 [FACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb).

**How credits are recorded.**
- Anyone with a free IMDb account can submit credits, subject to per-type eligibility rules. A title that is not yet listed goes through the New Title Submission Form first [FACT](https://help.imdb.com/article/contribution/filmography-credits/adding-filmography-credits/GAB5B33MBLEG73CT).
- Submission status and the reason for any rejection are visible to the submitter [FACT](https://help.imdb.com/article/contribution/filmography-credits/adding-filmography-credits/GAB5B33MBLEG73CT).
- **Two tiers.** Film and TV credits are "verified": edits go to IMDb's team for review. Commercials, theater, modeling, dance and similar work are "self-verified": they appear only on the person's IMDbPro page, and changes to self-verified data appear once the user clicks Save [FACT](https://help.imdb.com/article/imdbpro/manage-your-imdb-page/how-do-updates-on-my-resume-work/GMLWQXHRJL84G66W).
- **Verified affiliations.** Guild membership badges (SAG-AFTRA, ACTRA) are verified by member ID. Other organizations (WGC, AWG, LMGI) confirm membership to IMDbPro directly. Other bodies must join the IMDbPro Affiliate Program. Badges "are only displayed when IMDbPro has been able to verify a member's status" [FACT](https://help.imdb.com/article/imdbpro/manage-your-imdb-page/verified-affiliations-on-imdbpro/GA8B6VK2JXAAWNME). This is the closest existing analog to Pholio's ISSUED tier: an institution vouches in bulk through a partnership program.

**Verification weaknesses and credit padding.**
- An investigation found dozens of IMDb profiles with near-identical bios attached to big Indian films such as *KGF: Chapter 2*. They were added to build Google Knowledge Panels, alongside self-written press releases and fake streaming profiles. Edit histories are not public. A Bengali YouTube tutorial series teaches the method, and paid services reportedly charge more than ₹2,500 [FACT](https://peabee.substack.com/p/16-the-case-of-fake-imdb-credits). Quartz covered the same pattern and said people "either add themselves to movies' credits or use third-party services" [FACT, excerpt only, page returned 403](https://qz.com/imposters-game-bollywood-imdb-pages-to-fool-google-1849393865).
- In a federal fraud indictment, a producer gave an investor *another person's* IMDb profile as his own to inflate his credibility [FACT](https://www.irs.gov/compliance/criminal-investigation/movie-producer-arrested-on-federal-indictment-charging-him-with-12-million-schemes-to-defraud-victims-including-film-projects).
- Pay-to-be-cast productions charge actors $100–$500 per feature, a practice that is legal but criticized [FACT](https://11alive.com/article/news/local/some-independent-film-productions-asking-actors-to-pay-to-be-in-movie-but-leaders-say-thats-not-right/85-0f0bc60c-dd0c-48fd-ad30-f3462a0c2d43). Backstage reports about 280 attempts per period to post scam casting calls on its site [FACT](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/the-art-of-the-scam-how-grifters-operate-how-to-avoid-them-68015/).
- Allegations that STARmeter rankings were sold come from a small outlet and are unverified. I found no IMDb response [FACT that the allegation exists](https://cinemablend.com/new/IMDB-Accused-Rigging-Star-Meter-42058.html). Treat it as unproven.
- [INFERENCE] I found no source describing a direct "pay for an IMDb credit" scam targeting actors. The documented abuse is self-insertion plus SEO laundering, which works because the credit is *publicly indexed and trusted by Google*.

**Who pays and how much.**
- US IMDbPro costs $19.99/month or $149.99/year. A 5-seat group plan costs $79.95/month ($15.99 per seat). India pricing is about ₹745/month. Promotions take the annual price to about $95 [FACT](https://help.imdb.com/article/imdbpro/your-account/imdbpro-s-save-now-november-offer-terms-conditions/GARSGQ5WC93UCGVT), [FACT](https://pro.imdb.com/groupplan).
- **Who pays: both sides.** Talent pays to control their page, add self-verified credits and get representation listed. Buyers pay for contact information, which is gated: "Become a member to see contact information" [FACT](https://pro.imdb.com/name/nm0687896). They also pay for development tracking and connections.
- **Scale.** IMDb discloses no IMDbPro subscriber count, and I found none [FACT of absence]. An older case study said Pro unlocked contacts for more than 51,000 names and about 10,000 companies [FACT](https://www.mequoda.com/reviews-and-studies/publishing-case-studies/imdb-media-network-case-study).

**Why the industry pays (and why it worked).**
- [INFERENCE] IMDb did not *create* trust in credits. It inherited it. Film and TV credits are contractually determined by guilds, displayed on screen, and checkable against a public artifact. IMDb's "verified" means "matches the on-screen or official record." The paid layer sells **contact data plus development tracking on top of a free consumer database with huge organic traffic**. The credit graph is the lure. Contacts and pipeline are what people actually buy.
- [INFERENCE] The self-verified tier for commercials, theater and modeling exists because no on-screen artifact or guild record exists for those categories. That is Pholio's exact territory. IMDb's answer was to quarantine those credits in Pro and label them self-verified. It did not try to verify them.
- The 2022 policy update (age removal and related changes) is referenced in IMDb's release [FACT, page returned 403, excerpt only](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20221212005826/en/IMDb-Announces-Significant-Policy-and-Product-Updates-That-Empower-Entertainment-Professionals-to-Showcase-Themselves-to-Entertainment-Industry-Decision-Makers).

## 2. Muso.ai

- **What it is.** A music-credits database with claimable profiles. Its own materials describe about 4M creator profiles, about 50M tracks and 12M albums. Other figures vary by source, from "16M tracks verified" to "100M+ credits" [FACT](https://routenote.com/blog/manage-music-credit-statistics/), [FACT](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2024/11/25/muso-ai-credit-platform/).
- **How credits are recorded.** Credits are aggregated from industry data sources and "merged through an AI enrichment process." Analytics come from Spotify, Apple Music, TIDAL, YouTube, TikTok and others [FACT](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2024/11/25/muso-ai-credit-platform/).
- **Verification.** Credit changes are "validated by artists and master rights holders." Artists claim profiles through social login tied to the artist. Verified corrections go out to data partners such as AirGigs [FACT](https://intercom.help/musoai/en/articles/6226433-data-partners).
- **Scale of activity.** About 40,000 credit modifications per week, with clients including Sony Music Publishing (partner-written article) [FACT](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2024/11/25/muso-ai-credit-platform/).
- **Who pays.** Creators: Pro costs $9.99/month with a 7-day trial and adds daily stats and trend graphs. Claiming is free [FACT](https://routenote.com/blog/manage-music-credit-statistics/). There is also B2B work with publishers.
- **Funding.** I found no disclosed round. Third-party estimates (unverified) suggest about $1.9M revenue and a "no funding" listing [FACT that the estimate exists](https://prospeo.io/c/muso-ai). Founded 2017, with a patent granted May 2025 [FACT](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/musoai).
- **What worked.** [INFERENCE] Muso starts from *existing* label and distributor metadata, so profiles exist before anyone signs up. People then claim them to correct them, and vanity statistics ("your credits got X streams") give a reason to return. The creator is never asked to build the record from zero.
- **What's weak.** [INFERENCE] The business is small relative to its data (est. ~$2M revenue). Selling credit vanity to creators appears to be a modest business, not a large one.

## 3. Jaxsta (Australia): the cautionary tale

- **Founded** in Sydney in 2015 by Jacqui Louez Schoorl and Louis Schoorl. Beta launched June 2019 [FACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaxsta).
- **Model: "official" credits only.** Data was supplied by content owners, not crowdsourced. Partners included all three majors, Merlin, unions and distributors [FACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaxsta). A December 2024 ASX filing called it "the world's largest and only database of official music credits," with more than 380M verified credits [FACT](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20241209/pdf/06cdn25s0nv6hr.pdf).
- **Who paid.** The membership fee was waived during COVID (2020). In August 2021 it introduced a free Core tier and a US$49/year Plus tier (later renamed Creator). Business and Enterprise tiers followed in June 2022, plus a commercial API [FACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaxsta). Songtradr invested A$1.92M in 2020 for metadata access [FACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaxsta).
- **Trajectory.** ASX trading was suspended in June 2023 pending a funding outcome, and again in October 2023 over a late annual report [FACT](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20230616/pdf/05qq2swg7x0mw9.pdf), [FACT](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20231002/pdf/05vkw7j6vyv3jp.pdf). The company rebranded as Vinyl Group (ASX: VNL) in December 2023, and Jaxsta became a subsidiary [FACT](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/jaxsta-rebrands-as-vinyl-group/).
- **Closure.** Public subscriptions ended December 17, 2025 (Wikipedia lists a defunct date of November 21, 2025). The platform was "placed into hibernation," with prorated refunds and no personal data export. The stated reason: "the current model does not scale, which means we can no longer offer the service in a viable way" [FACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaxsta). Trade report: [themusic.com.au (403 to fetcher; excerpt)](https://themusic.com.au/industry/music-credits-database-jaxsta-announces-closure/h5U1m5qdnJ8/21-11-25).
- **Why it failed.** [INFERENCE] Jaxsta had the *best possible verification*: official, issuer-supplied data from the majors, 380M credits. It still could not build a business. Verified credits on their own were not something enough people would pay for. Creators paid $49/year for vanity and correction. B2B buyers already had their own rights databases. Hirers did not search Jaxsta to find producers. **This is the single most important precedent for Pholio's ISSUED-tier thesis: issuer-confirmed credit data at massive scale did not produce a viable business.**

## 4. Discogs credits

- **Model.** Anyone can submit or edit. Submissions must come from the physical item, and "web-submitting" from other sites is disallowed. Other users vote on accuracy, from "Entirely Incorrect" to "Correct and Complete." Voting rights are earned automatically through good activity [FACT](https://support.discogs.com/hc/en-us/articles/360008545114-Overview-Of-How-Discogs-Is-Built), [FACT](https://support.discogs.com/hc/articles/360004017694-Database-Glossary).
- **History.** Manual moderation produced a backlog of more than 20,000 items by August 2007. In March 2008 Discogs replaced moderation with community voting [FACT](https://localization.discogs.com/the-evolution-of-the-database-submission-guidelines).
- **Scale.** About 15.1M releases, 8M+ artists and 1.8M labels as of May 2022 (Wikipedia snapshot) [FACT](https://webcf.waybackmachine.org/web/20220512102908/https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discogs). Users added 114.2M items to Collections in 2025, and more than 920M items are cataloged in Collections in total [FACT](https://shorefire.com/releases/entry/discogs-breaks-all-time-collection-record-as-music-fans-catalog-more-than-114-million-items-in-2025).
- **Who pays.** Buyers and sellers in the marketplace, through seller fees. The credits database is free.
- **Why it worked.** [INFERENCE] Credits are a by-product of a *collector and marketplace* job: people catalog what they own and sell it. The data is maintained by obsessive third parties, not by the credited people, and is checked against a physical artifact. **The credited creative never maintains anything.** This is also the closest precedent for "draft credits from work already posted," with a crucial difference: Discogs has a canonical artifact (the sleeve), while an Instagram post is not a canonical credit document.

## 5. Streaming credits (Spotify / Tidal / Apple Music) and Credits Due

- **Spotify** added songwriter and producer credits on desktop in February 2018, later on iOS. The data came from labels, and corrections went only through labels and distributors. Spotify acknowledged credits "may be incomplete or contain inaccuracies" [FACT](https://techcrunch.com/2018/02/02/spotify-debuts-a-new-songwriter-credits-feature-on-desktop/), [FACT](https://www.hypebot.com/spotify-adds-songwriter-producer-credits-to-ios-app-promises-corrections-more-data/).
- In November 2025 Spotify expanded credits to engineers and other contributors and announced **SongDNA**, a collaborator, sample and cover graph with sample data from the WhoSampled acquisition, for Premium users [FACT](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-rolls-out-expanded-song-credits-previews-new-songdna-and-about-the-song-features-for-premium-users), [FACT](https://www.musicweek.com/publishing/read/spotify-expands-credits-with-new-features-including-songdna/093090). Main artists can hide or show third-party credits [FACT](https://support.spotify.com/at/artists/article/songdna).
- **Tidal** had liner notes from 2017 and interactive, clickable credit pages from July 2019 [FACT](https://www.engadget.com/2019-07-03-tidals-interactive-credits-let-you-explore-artists-other-proje.html).
- **Apple Music** added Song Credits with iOS 17 (2023). The Music Producers Guild publicly pushed for fuller production credits [FACT](https://routenote.com/blog/credits-on-apple-music/), [FACT](https://headlinerhub.com/mpg-urges-apple-music-disclose-full-music-credits-ios-17-update.html).
- **Credits Due** launched September 21, 2021 at the Ivors, led by Björn Ulvaeus, the Ivors Academy and the Music Rights Awareness Foundation. Its goal is complete metadata "at the point of creation": IPI/IPN/ISNI, ISWC, ISRC, titles and all contributor names. It cites about £500M per year unallocated or misallocated because of bad data. Spotify, Apple Music, BMG, PRS and CISAC backed it [FACT](https://musictech.com/news/abba-bjorn-ulvaeus-credits-due-music-royalty-500m/), [FACT](https://musicweek.com/publishing/read/abba-s-bjorn-ulvaeus-launches-credits-due-campaign-for-songwriters-and-composers/084190). A toolkit with a split sheet followed [FACT](https://dima.org/playlist/credits-due-toolkit-aims-to-boost-music-metadata-campaign/). I found no measurement of adoption since then.
- **Session Studio** (formerly Auddly, founded 2012, renamed 2019) was co-founded by Ulvaeus, Max Martin and Niclas Molinder. It is a free app that captures creator credits and identifiers through **QR check-in in the studio** and passes them to labels, distributors and PROs [FACT](https://sessionstudio.com/about), [FACT](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1570114999). CB Insights reports a March 2023 acquisition by Salt (single source) [FACT](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/auddly-vs-session).
- **Scale of the problem.** A UK government metadata report found one publisher included ISRCs in only 10% of registrations, and less than 0.1% of recordings arrived at DSPs mapped to an ISWC [FACT](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/music-streaming-metadata-report-and-project-update/executive-summary-music-streaming-metadata-report).
- [INFERENCE] Music credits matter because **money flows through them** (royalties). Even with that incentive, plus Ulvaeus, Max Martin and every major DSP behind the effort, point-of-creation capture remains unsolved. Pholio's credits carry no royalty incentive and Pholio is barred from money, so its credits have *less* pull than music credits, not more. Session's QR check-in on set is still a good UX pattern to borrow for the MUTUAL tier at a shoot.

## 6. Models.com

- **What it is.** The fashion industry's reference directory and credits database for models, photographers, stylists, hair and makeup artists, casting directors, agencies and magazines. Work pages list credits by role per image [FACT](https://models.com/work/various-editorials-in-progress-for-submission-beauty).
- **How credits get in.** Uploading needs an account approved for upload privileges, through an onboarding application. Uploaders are prompted to credit the crew, especially the photographer. **Only people who already have a Models.com page can be credited.** Users with linked profiles can tag themselves into existing works. Staff suspend upload privileges for duplicates [FACT, help-center excerpts; pages returned 403](https://help.models.com/article/29/how-do-i-upload-an-editorial-advertising-campaign-or-magazine-cover), [FACT](https://help.models.com/article/22/how-do-i-tag-myself-in-a-work-that-is-already-in-the-database). Agencies can add their own credit to talent pages [FACT](https://help.models.com/article/49/how-do-i-add-my-agencys-credit-to-a-model-or-talents-page-). There is a "See something wrong? Let us know" correction route.
- **Who pays.** Pro costs $20/month or $200/year for individuals, with a 15-day trial. Free accounts are capped at **50 people-page views per month**, and Pro removes the cap. Team pricing is $15 per user per month for 5+ users. Agency listings are paid ("inquire") and include Pro seats valued at $200 per user per year [FACT](https://help.models.com/article/15/what-is-modelscom-pro-membership).
- **MDX.** I could not find a Models.com product called "MDX" in any source. A work page slug includes "mdx" (an editorial series) [FACT](https://models.com/work/modelscom-the-warriors-mdx). Treat any claim of an "MDX pro subscription" as unverified.
- **Why it works.** [INFERENCE] Models.com is *editorially curated*. It does not cover everyone. Its rankings (Top 50 and similar) and the selectivity of who gets a page are what carry signal. The credit graph is credible because uploaders are pre-approved industry accounts and the work is published, recognizable editorial. The money comes from **buyers paying to browse** (the 50-view cap) and agencies paying to be listed. That is close to the IMDbPro model and the clearest fashion proof that "paid search seats" can work. **But Models.com already occupies this position for the top tier of fashion.** Pholio's opening is the tier below: Brooklyn FW, indie magazines and school shows, which Models.com does not cover. [INFERENCE] That lower tier is also where buyers are least willing to pay for search.

## 7. The Dots (UK)

- Founded by Pip Jamieson. Raised £1.5M in 2015 (Hegarty, Hambro Perks, Angel Academe) and £4M in December 2017 led by Hambro Perks, reported total about £5.5M. Craft lists $7.7M total [FACT](https://www.uktech.news/news/creative-network-the-dots-raises-1-5m-20151022), [FACT](https://techcrunch.com/2017/12/13/aiming-to-be-the-linkedin-for-creatives-the-dots-raises-4m/), [FACT](https://www.essential-business.pt/2019/02/21/joining-the-dots/), [FACT](https://craft.co/the-dots).
- **Credit model.** Members post projects and tag the whole team (editors, photographers, stylists and others). Jamieson called it "almost like a community-driven IMDB" [FACT](https://techcrunch.com/2017/12/13/aiming-to-be-the-linkedin-for-creatives-the-dots-raises-4m/).
- **Members.** 100k+ in 2018, 300k+ later, and "over 1 million" per a recent London Tech Week bio [FACT](https://techround.co.uk/startups/startup-profile-the-dots/), [FACT](https://londontechweek.com/speakers/pip-jamieson).
- **Current status (2025/26).** The Dots now **licenses its app as a white-label "member club" networking app**. Listed clients are Apple, Soho House's Works community, 180 Studios, Frieze and Dazed Media [FACT](https://londontechweek.com/speakers/pip-jamieson), [FACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pip_Jamieson). I found no reporting on layoffs, an acquisition or a shutdown.
- **Why it didn't become the standard.** [INFERENCE] (1) Tags were mutual-only and unverified. Nobody with authority issued them, so a Dots credit carried no more weight than a portfolio caption. (2) Revenue came from job posts and brand recruiting. Hirers had LinkedIn and Instagram, so the "graph" was never the thing being purchased. (3) The member count grew, but members did not need to return. (4) The pivot to white-label SaaS shows that the durable asset was the *software for communities*, not the public credit graph. **Pholio's FWB launch looks like The Dots' Soho House / Frieze deals in reverse: an institution brings its community.** The Dots ended up selling that as a private network. It did not turn it into a public verified graph.

## 8. LinkedIn (verification, skills, endorsements, fakes)

- **Verification scale.** More than 100M members have at least one verification, and members add about 30M verifications per year. Adoption rose 38%+ year over year in 2025. About 60% of verified members confirmed a **workplace** (mostly company email) and about 27% confirmed a **government ID**. The US accounts for about 40% of verified users. LinkedIn claims verified members get up to 60% more profile views and about 50% more post interactions (company-reported) [FACT](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/18/linkedins-profile-verification-push-is-accelerating-and-india-is-leading-the-charge-in-2025).
- **Methods.** Identity through CLEAR (US, Canada, Mexico), Persona (NFC passport) or DigiLocker (India). Workplace through work email at participating companies or Microsoft Entra. Education is also verifiable [FACT](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/140551), [FACT](https://smallbiztrends.com/linkedin-new-verification-features/).
- **What it does *not* verify.** Verification confirms *identity* and *employer*. It does not confirm what the person did. LinkedIn's verification is about the person, not the work [INFERENCE, consistent with the help page].
- **Skill Assessments killed.** LinkedIn discontinued Skill Assessments and removed badges and data from profiles in 2024. The stated reason: hirers said "examples of how a candidate applied their skills is increasingly valuable to assess fit," so skills are now tagged to jobs, projects and education [FACT](https://www.linkedin.com/help/learning/answer/a1690529). **This directly supports "credits over badges": LinkedIn itself moved from a test-based credential to skills anchored in work.**
- **Endorsements.** They still exist. I found no authoritative data on their decline, and no source showing LinkedIn removed them [FACT of absence]. [INFERENCE] They are widely seen as low-signal because they cost nothing to give. That supports Pholio's "no endorsements" stance.
- **Fake accounts.** In July–December 2025, 99.7% of fake accounts were stopped before any member reported them, 97.8% by automated defenses [FACT](https://about.linkedin.com/transparency/community-report). A reported H1 figure of about 21.6M fake accounts blocked or removed, roughly 95% at registration, is from secondary sources, and the year is unconfirmed [FACT that it was reported](https://www.cyberscoop.com/linkedin-fake-accounts-china/).
- **Content Credentials.** LinkedIn shows a C2PA "CR" label on images and video that carry credentials. It is applied automatically from embedded metadata and rolled out gradually [FACT](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/396225/linkedin-begins-labeling-ai-generated-content.html). Fortune documented how hard the display decisions were [FACT](https://fortune.com/2024/11/26/linkedin-content-credentials-labeling-challenges-c2pa).

## 9. Contra, Behance, Dribbble

**Contra**
- Founded 2019 by Ben Huffman and Gajus Kuizinas. Raised a $14.5M Series A, then a $30M Series B (NEA, November 2021), just under $45M in total. Went **commission-free** for freelancers in November 2021 [FACT](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/02/independent-worker-network-contra-goes-commission-free-as-it-secures-new-nea-led-capital).
- In 2024 it pivoted toward the buyer side ("Contra for Companies"). Company-reported figures: 700k independents, 20k clients, more than $30M in matched opportunities [FACT, single secondary source](https://yashgaikwad.substack.com/p/deep-dive-contracom).
- [INFERENCE] The move from a free creator network to monetizing companies is the same arc Pholio proposes. It is notable that Contra had to re-center on the buyer to make money.

**Behance (Adobe)**
- About 56M members as of December 2024 (Wikipedia, citing Behance's year in review), and "nearly 50 million" in 2023 [FACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behance), [FACT](https://portfolios.risd.edu/blog/year-in-review-2023).
- **Two credit mechanisms.** *Co-owners* must have a Behance account and accept the invite, and the project then appears on all co-owners' profiles. *Credits* appear only in the project info and **are not added to the credited person's profile**. Non-members can be invited by email [FACT](https://help.behance.net/hc/en-us/articles/204483494-Guide-Co-owning-And-Crediting), [FACT](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/business/enterprise/behance-for-teams.html).
- **Content Credentials.** Behance displays Content Credentials, still labeled beta, in its single-image viewer, as a "cr" icon aligned to C2PA [FACT](https://help.behance.net/hc/en-us/articles/4408307775643-Guide-Content-Credentials-Beta), [FACT](https://helpx.adobe.com/creative-cloud/help/cai/use-content-credentials.html).
- Founded with a "creative graph" ambition and reached 1M projects in 2011 [FACT](https://techcrunch.com/2011/08/05/with-its-eyes-on-the-creative-graph-behance-crosses-1-million-projects-published-milestone/).
- [INFERENCE] At 56M members, Behance has had team crediting for over a decade and has never become a hiring-grade credit record. Mutual credits on a portfolio site do not turn into trusted provenance, even at enormous scale. Its value to Adobe is distribution for Creative Cloud.

**Dribbble**
- Free tier available. Pro Lite costs $8/month ($48/year), Pro Standard $16/month ($96/year), and Pro Plus $199/month ($1,188/year). Annual subscribers transact without the designer platform fee [FACT](https://help.dribbble.com/en/articles/11062025-dribbble-pricing-and-payment-terms). Dribbble acquired Fontspring in February 2022.
- [INFERENCE] Dribbble monetizes *creators* for visibility and lead flow, not buyers for search. I found no evidence that Dribbble shots function as verified credits.

## 10. Read.cv and Polywork (identity startups that died 2024–25)

**Read.cv**
- Founded 2021 by Andy Chung. Acquired by Perplexity on January 17, 2025. The team joined Perplexity's design and engineering group, and the service wound down immediately. Data export ran until May 16, 2025. Terms were undisclosed [FACT](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/17/perplexity-acquires-read-cv-a-social-media-platform-for-professionals/).
- I found no user count or funding data [FACT of absence].
- [INFERENCE] Read.cv was loved by designers for its aesthetic. Taste alone did not produce a business, and the exit was an acqui-hire.

**Polywork**
- An Irish-founded "multi-hyphenate LinkedIn alternative." Raised $3.5M seed and a $13M Series A (a16z, the Collisons and others), more than $40M in total [FACT](https://unleash.ai/hr-technology/news/new-professional-social-network-polywork-launches-seed-funding), [FACT](https://www.unleash.ai/alternative-professional-social-network-polywork-raises-13m-in-series-a/), [FACT](https://x.com/businessposthq/status/1875104854881796185).
- Pivoted from a professional network to an AI personal-website builder around October 2023 [FACT](https://destructured.substack.com/p/is-polywork-pivoting-to-solve-the).
- Founder Peter Johnston announced the shutdown about December 31, 2024, after 3.5 years, **returning the remaining capital to investors**. He cited growth that was not sustained and no viable path to "a gigantic business" [FACT](https://x.com/multiplay3r/status/1873859669065978327). Hosted sites were removed [FACT](https://orchestra.b12.io/resource-center/website-basics/how-to-transfer-your-polywork-website.html).
- [INFERENCE] Polywork's core object was a self-claimed, badge-tagged activity timeline, effectively an all-CLAIMED credit system. It had no external authority and no buyer. Users felt it was "something nice to have," and when the social layer was removed, existing users left [FACT about the user sentiment](https://www.dev.to/conr/polywork-is-dead-to-me-153j).

## 11. Production crew databases and union rosters

- **Crew United** (Europe, mainly DACH) is the strongest analog to Pholio's MUTUAL plus editorial model. "Any (active) members involved in a given film project can enter your name." Most entries arrive through colleague networks. "All new entries made by our members are checked by the editorial team." Data is also pulled from press releases, funders and broadcasters. Only Crew United can delete entries, and the person who entered a credit is not disclosed [FACT](https://www.crew-united.com/en/croft/render/home/faq). Premium costs about €4.16/month annually and includes "Connection Tools" (who worked with whom, in which role). Companies pay €69.90/year [FACT](https://www.crew-united.com/en/croft/render/home/pricing). [INFERENCE] It works because German-language film and TV is a bounded, funded, broadcaster-registered industry with a finite set of productions, so editors can check claims against public funding and broadcaster records.
- **Mandy Network** (UK) has 2.6M+ members across cast and crew (directory claim) [FACT](https://alternativeto.net/software/mandy/about). Backstage, backed by TA Associates, acquired it along with StarNow in February 2021 [FACT](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210222005187/en/Backstage-Announces-Acquisitions-of-StarNow-and-Mandy-Network). A free account can view listings, and applying requires a paid subscription [FACT](https://blogs.lawrence.edu/careercenter/2022/04/job-search-resources-for-film-students-mandy-com-staffmeup-com.html).
- **Staff Me Up.** Free profiles, 5 applications per month on the free tier, and paid crew tiers (historically $19.99/month, or $12.50/month billed annually) [FACT, possibly dated](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/staff-me-up-jobs/).
- **ProductionHub.** Searching the crew and vendor database is free, and paid plans start around $75 per post [FACT, third-party](https://betterteam.com/ca/productionhub).
- **Shooting People.** I could not retrieve current pricing or membership data [FACT of absence].
- [INFERENCE] The pattern: crew job boards monetize the **job seeker**, who pays to apply. They are not credit authorities. Credits on them are self-entered resumes.
- **Union rosters are the true "issued" credit system in US film and TV.** The Contract Services Industry Experience Roster requires verified days, for example 30 union days in one year or 100 non-union days over three years for Local 600 (blog-sourced thresholds). Employment is verified by **payroll records (W-2)** or an employment verification letter signed by the production company (1099). Days generally require a theatrical or TV release [FACT](https://csatf.org/rosters-lists/industry-experience-roster/), [FACT, guide](https://blog.staffmeup.com/?p=946). [INFERENCE] Here verification rests on payroll, which is the money trail, an option Pholio is explicitly barred from owning. Pholio could at most *reference* payment evidence that a creative uploads. It cannot generate that evidence.

## 12. Credly / Open Badges / W3C Verifiable Credentials

- **Credly.** Pearson acquired it in January 2022 at a $200M total value. At that point it had issued 50M credentials to 25M people for more than 2,000 organizations, with $13.3M revenue in 2021, up 47% [FACT](https://plc.pearson.com/en-GB/news/pearson-acquires-digital-credentialing-leader-credly), [FACT](https://www.insidehighered.com/quicktakes/2022/02/01/pearson-acquires-credentialing-firm-credly-200m-deal). Its largest issuers in 2024 were Cisco (1.77M badges) and IBM (1.72M) [FACT](https://learn.credly.com/blog/celebrating-top-credly-issuers-of-2024).
- **Who pays: issuers**, meaning companies certifying their own product skills. Earners do not pay. [INFERENCE] Credly works because issuers have a commercial motive (driving adoption of their platforms through certification). That differs from FWB, whose motive for issuing credits is unclear.
- **Do hirers use them?** The evidence is mostly vendor surveys. 1EdTech: 87% of HR leaders have heard of badges, and 48% have encountered them in screening or hired a holder [FACT](https://www.1edtech.org/article/research-shows-growing-employer-support-competency-frameworks-talent-analytics-and-skills). Accredible (a vendor) claims 91% of employers look for credentials, yet only 46% regularly see them in applications, and credentials still do not flow into applicant tracking systems [FACT](https://www.accredible.com/reports/2025-state-of-credentialing-report). A peer-reviewed pilot study says the research is limited and that badges only have value if employers recognize them [FACT](https://sbij.scholasticahq.com/article/125642-digital-badges-a-pilot-study-of-employer-perceptions).
- [INFERENCE] I found **no independent field evidence** that W3C Verifiable Credentials or Open Badges change hiring outcomes in creative fields. Issuer-signed credentials have scaled where the issuer is a vendor with a commercial reason to issue, and the hirer recognizes the issuer's brand (Cisco, AWS). Cryptographic verification is not what creates value. **Issuer brand recognition is.** An "ISSUED by Fashion Week Brooklyn" credit is only worth what a casting director thinks of FWB.

## 13. Less obvious competitors and analogs

- **Creators Guild of America: Mosaic** (beta, reported March 2026). This is the **closest direct competitor to the thesis.** It is a "verified, public portfolio of every project," with credits "verified by third-party collaborators" and statuses including "Authenticated by / In review by / Issue flagged by [org]." Each creator gets a universal **Creator ID**, built with content provenance in mind. Adding credits is free. More than 12,000 creators were registered and being onboarded at launch. Partners include Loti AI (likeness protection) [FACT](https://www.mediaweek.com.au/safeguard-the-identities-meet-the-imdb-for-creators), [FACT](https://creatorsguildofamerica.org/credits). CGA is a nonprofit founded in 2023, with iJustine on its board [FACT](https://tubefilter.com/2023/08/25/creators-guild-of-america-ijustine-professional-service-organization-organized-labor/). It targets creators and influencers, not fashion, and it is "Authenticated by" an organization, which is Pholio's ISSUED tier nearly word for word.
- **Visie** (launched July 2024, Justin Woo, Australia-origin) is a curated directory of about 5,300 photo, film, fashion and styling professionals. "Every project credits the full creative team." Users tag each other, every profile is reviewed for quality and relevance, and there is no booking commission [FACT](https://visie.io/), [FACT](https://visie.io/faq), [FACT](https://lbbonline.com/news/new-creative-directory-visie-launches). This is a direct analog in fashion image-making.
- **Instagram enhanced tags** (March 2022). People tags can show the tagged account's profession category ("photographer," "makeup artist") to help crediting. The launch followed creator protests over uncredited work [FACT](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-enhanced-tags-creator-credits-announcement), [FACT](https://marketingbrew.com/stories/2022/03/09/instagram-rolls-out-enhanced-tags). [INFERENCE] **This is the raw data source for Pholio's "draft from Instagram tags" plan, and also its incumbent.** Meta already supplies role-labeled credits on the post. Pholio's value has to be the aggregation, persistence and verification Meta does not offer. Opinion piece: in about 90% of brand regrams the photographer goes uncredited (author's estimate, not data) [FACT that the claim exists](https://fstoppers.com/abuse/report/comment/432086).
- **IMVDb** is a community music-video database with director and DP profiles [FACT](https://imvdb.com/blog/tagged/cinematographer+profile).
- **Innoverse** is a fashion collaboration platform that manually verifies every account. **ISO Meet** connects users with "verified talents" (pre-launch) [FACT](https://innoverse.group/), [FACT](https://www.isomeet.com/).
- **Clowdy** (UK, 2015) let creatives tag contributions on uploaded work as a "LinkedIn for creatives." Current status unknown [FACT](https://techcrunch.com/2015/01/07/get-off-of-my-clowdy/). **Credictive** (2012) wanted to be "the IMDB for creative content and people" and appears to have disappeared [FACT](https://techcrunch.com/2012/05/08/credictive-wants-to-be-the-imdb-for-creative-content-and-people).
- **Session Studio** captures credits at the point of creation through QR check-in (see section 5).
- I found no 2023–2026 startup *specifically* pitching verified fashion-event credits issued by organizers [FACT of absence in my searches]. Mosaic and Visie are the nearest.

## Patterns: what makes credit systems succeed or fail

1. **Credits succeed when they are a by-product of something people already must do, and fail when they are a chore.** IMDb rides on contractual screen credits. Discogs rides on collecting and selling. Music credits ride on royalty registration. Union rosters ride on payroll. Crew United rides on a bounded, funded industry. The failures (Polywork, The Dots as a standard, Clowdy, Credictive) asked people to curate a record for its own sake. [INFERENCE from all of the above]
2. **Verification quality is not the bottleneck. Demand from buyers is.** Jaxsta had 380M issuer-supplied "official" credits from the majors and still closed because "the model does not scale." Behance has had team crediting for 56M members and is still not a hiring authority. Better verification alone does not make a business. [FACT on Jaxsta and Behance; INFERENCE on the conclusion]
3. **The money is buyers paying to *find and contact*, and access is gated.** IMDbPro gates contacts. Models.com caps free views at 50 per month and bundles Pro seats into agency listings. Crew United sells Connection Tools. Credly is paid for by issuers. Creator-paid vanity tiers (Muso $9.99, Jaxsta $49/year) produce small revenue. [FACT on prices; INFERENCE on revenue size]
4. **Trusted "verified" status almost always rests on an external canonical record**: on-screen credits, guild membership numbers, payroll records, the physical sleeve, broadcaster and funder data. Where none exists (commercials, theater, modeling), even IMDb gives up and labels the credit "self-verified" in a walled-off tier. **Pholio's categories (shows, editorials, shoots) are exactly the ones lacking a canonical record.** The ISSUED tier therefore has to *create* the canonical record. FWB can do that for runway. Most of the "weekly shoots" segment has no issuer. [FACT on IMDb tiers; INFERENCE on Pholio]
5. **Issuer brand is the whole value of an issued credential.** A Cisco badge matters because Cisco does. An FWB-issued credit is worth whatever casting directors and agencies think of FWB, and Models.com's selectivity is what gives its credits weight. Pholio has to pick issuers whose names carry weight with the buyers it plans to charge. [INFERENCE]
6. **Mutual tagging without an authority converges to portfolio captions.** This applies to The Dots, Behance credits (not even added to the credited person's profile), Clowdy and Visie. Crew United adds an editorial check and is respected. Mosaic adds org authentication. A MUTUAL tier needs either editorial review or an issuer behind it to carry weight. [FACT on mechanics; INFERENCE on weight]
7. **Credit systems get gamed exactly to the degree they're trusted and indexed.** IMDb self-insertion is driven by Google Knowledge Panel SEO, and services sell it for about ₹2,500. If Pholio credits are public and indexed, expect the same, starting with the CLAIMED tier. Keep CLAIMED credits out of search and indexing, or clearly separated, as IMDb does. [FACT on IMDb abuse; INFERENCE on recommendation]
8. **Badges and tests lose to work evidence.** LinkedIn killed Skill Assessments because hirers wanted "examples of how a candidate applied their skills." That supports Pholio's credits-over-scores stance. [FACT]
9. **Identity verification is now a commodity.** LinkedIn has 100M+ verified members through CLEAR, Persona and work email. Verifying a *person* is not a moat. Verifying *what they did on which work* still is not done well anywhere outside film and TV and music royalties. That gap is real, but its persistence across Jaxsta, The Dots, Behance and Polywork suggests the gap is a weak business, not an oversight. [FACT on LinkedIn; INFERENCE on the rest]
10. **Venture-funded identity networks without a buyer die or pivot.** Polywork ($40M+) returned capital. Read.cv was acqui-hired. The Dots (£5.5M) became white-label SaaS. Contra ($45M) pivoted to companies. Every one of them re-centered on whoever pays. [FACT on outcomes; INFERENCE on cause]
11. **Live competitive threat.** Creators Guild of America's Mosaic (March 2026) already ships "verified by collaborators" plus "Authenticated by [org]" plus a Creator ID for creators, free, with 12k+ in onboarding. Visie covers curated fashion and photo crediting. Instagram's enhanced tags already provide role-labeled credit data. Pholio's differentiation has to come from the *issuer network*, meaning fashion institutions such as FWB, schools and indie magazines, and from serving the agency, casting and buyer side. The credit data model alone is not a differentiator. [FACT on competitors; INFERENCE on implication]
