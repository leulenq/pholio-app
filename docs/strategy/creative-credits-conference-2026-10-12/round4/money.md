# Round 4 — Monetization: Mosaic changes the economic burden of proof

**[BET] Vote: reject the paid credit-network rebuild. Test one paid portfolio-intake workflow, while keeping stop/use Mosaic as an equally legitimate outcome.** A named organizer is a possible customer, not evidence of a business. I will not replace our failed universal-credit arithmetic with equally invented casting-desk arithmetic.

## Recantations

[FACT: fixed conference boundary] Model releases are contracts. My proposed release collection violated the explicit exclusion of contracts and contracting. I withdraw it, along with contract addenda, production consents that function as releases, pull letters authorizing borrowed goods, collections and paid immigration evidence. Product permission to display a person's name is separate from acquiring production rights. Pholio can charge for its own software; it cannot become the financial or contractual intermediary.

[ASSUMPTION, withdrawn] My “$1–3M ARR” forecast, NYC organizer cap, customer counts and month-nine agency launch lacked verified inputs. My 3% conversion and five-of-thirty pre-sale gates were proposed decision rules, not defensible proof of a venture market. Friendly prepayment buys concierge attention as well as software. It cannot establish software retention or viable support costs.

## Three attacks

**Founder:** [ASSUMPTION] “Offer a stated price” still leaves the hardest choice unresolved: what is the paid unit? A seasonal organizer may reasonably reject an annual subscription but purchase one casting. Test an event-priced workspace against the existing process, with an explicitly priced repeat event. Do not count its refusal of a poorly matched billing period as rejection of all intake value. Conversely, founder-operated designer reconciliation might sell an agency service rather than software.

**VC:** [ASSUMPTION] A returning-talent pool is not automatically an economic asset. Stale measurements, changed availability and renewed permissions impose maintenance costs. Measure the labor needed to make the pool usable at the second casting. Your narrower thesis is better specified, but its “small-business wedge” label still precedes demonstrated contribution margin, repeat purchasing and independent acquisition.

**Creative:** [ASSUMPTION] Free Mosaic is a reasonable recommendation for commercial-credit needs; it does not settle whether those records solve anyone's selection problem. We should observe both completion and actual external use. If an assistant obtains authentication and never shares it with anyone making a consequential decision, we have confirmed a publishing task, not economic value. Do not replace Pholio enthusiasm with Mosaic enthusiasm.

**Hirer:** [ASSUMPTION] Your time-saving pilot needs a baseline that includes all of Pholio's work. Transferring coordinator minutes to unpaid founders is not savings. Include setup, imports, support, applicant questions, corrections and exports. Also record whether a paid organizer buys again without the founder personally chasing every designer.

We have not verified its costs, conversion or sustainable revenue. Its broader bundle also includes activities Pholio expressly excludes. Matching its dues price would not reproduce its proposition.

Business costs $30 monthly with multi-profile tools. These are published paid offers, not proof of demand or profit. [FACT] [Kavyar's job documentation](https://help.kavyar.com/article/2mywaf3zfk-how-to-post-a-job) explicitly places applicant-management tools inside its Premium Business offer. No precise current price is established here. [BET] These references support testing paid workflow or useful data around free records, not charging for authentication itself.

## Options and economics experiment

[BET] **Stop/use Mosaic:** recommend the existing product when it satisfies the credit task; do not rebuild solely because its approval rules leave some people out. **Narrow Pholio:** charge the organizer for an event workspace covering portfolio review, designer selection and acceptance reconciliation. Keep applicants free. **Venture thesis:** unproven until repeated paid reuse, repeatable acquisition and expansion beyond founder relationships appear; credits growing is insufficient.

[BET] Run a priced real pilot after observing the current process. Report revenue minus infrastructure, messaging and all delivery/support labor valued at a stated replacement wage; separately disclose sales and onboarding labor. Compare event one with paid reuse. Record what founders manually subsidize and which steps software actually removes. A profitable concierge pilot warrants another test; a repeatable product requires improving labor economics without damaging outcomes. If buyers repeatedly prefer Forms after a competent trial, stop. No invented market ceiling is needed to make that decision.
