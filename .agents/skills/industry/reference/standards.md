# Industry Standards — Roles, Divisions, Materials, Stats, Operations, Money, Compliance

The master knowledge base. Sourced from working modeling/creative-talent practice (US-centric with EU/international notes). Norms vary by **market** (NYC/LA/Paris/Milan/London/Tokyo…), **tier** (new face vs. established vs. supermodel), and **division** — when in doubt, say which.

---

## 1. Agency structure & roles

Talent representation is not one company. It's a network.

- **Mother agency** — the agency that *discovers and develops* a model and manages their career. Often a smaller/local agency. Takes a smaller, *stacking* commission (commonly ~5–10%) and **places** the model with larger agencies in major markets. The mother-agent relationship is long-term and primary; other agencies are downstream. **Modeling software that assumes one talent ↔ one agency has already failed.**
- **Booking / market agency (a.k.a. "daughter" agency)** — represents the model in a specific market (e.g. a Paris agency, a NYC agency). A single model is commonly signed to *several* market agencies at once, **non-exclusively**, plus their mother agency.
- **Management vs. agency** — "management" companies manage career/brand and may not directly book day-to-day jobs the way an agency does; lines blur. Actors more often have *managers + agents* as separate roles.
- **Placement / scouting agency** — focuses on discovering and placing talent into bigger markets.

**People inside an agency:**
- **Scout** — finds new faces (street, social, open calls, scouting events/contests).
- **Booker / agent** — the day-to-day rep: submits talent to clients, negotiates, manages calendar/options, handles vouchers. Bookers sit on a specific **board**.
- **Head booker / board director** — runs a board (e.g. Women's, Men's, New Faces).
- **Agency director / owner** — runs the business.
- **New-faces / development booker** — manages models being built up (test shoots, building the book, polish, measurements, placement).
- **Accounting / billing** — invoices clients, tracks commission, pays talent (slowly).

**Markets & placement:** Models travel on **stays** (a "Paris stay," a "Tokyo stay"), living in **model apartments**, working a market for weeks/months. Placement, travel, visas, and per-market representation are core operations — not edge cases.

## 2. Divisions / boards — there is no single "model"

Agencies organize talent into **boards** (divisions). Standards, stats, and gatekeeping differ sharply. Any flow assuming one body type/one path is wrong.

| Board / division | What it is | Notes that affect product |
|---|---|---|
| **Fashion / Editorial** | High-fashion magazines, runway, campaigns | Strictest, narrowest physical standards; book/tearsheets matter most |
| **Commercial / Lifestyle** | Ads, catalog, e-comm, "real people" | Much wider range of age/size/look; relatability > editorial extremity |
| **Runway / Show** | Catwalk; tightly spec'd to sample sizes | Height/measurement gates are hard requirements per season |
| **Fit / Showroom** | Stands in for sample sizing during garment dev | Booked for *exact* measurements + reliability, not "look" |
| **Parts** | Hands, feet, legs, hair, lips | Specialized stats (ring size, shoe, nail condition) |
| **Curve / Plus** | Sizes above sample | Real, large, growing board — has its *own* standards, not "the exception" |
| **Petite** | Below typical fashion height | Its own commercial-heavy market |
| **Fitness / Sports** | Athletic, swimwear, activewear | Different stat emphasis (definition, athletic measurements) |
| **Mature / Classic** | 30s–70s+ | Major commercial demand; age is an asset, not a flaw |
| **Kids & Teens / Children** | Minors | Legal regime applies (see §7). Sizes by age; rapid change |
| **Influencer / Social / Digital** | Booked for audience + content | Stats include follower/engagement; often hybrid with talent |
| **Talent (actors, dancers, hosts, musicians)** | Performance | Reels, sides, unions, headshots over comp cards |

**Product implication:** division is a *first-class attribute* that changes which fields are required, which stats are shown, which standards apply, and what "good" looks like. A talent can sit on more than one board.

## 3. Talent materials (assets) — the vocabulary and the standards

These are the artifacts the industry runs on. Getting their names and rules right is most of "feeling real."

- **Comp card** (also **composite card**, **sed card**, **zed card**, "comp", "card") — the model's printed/PDF leave-behind.
  - **Standard size:** US **5.5″ × 8.5″** (half a US letter sheet); **A5** (148×210mm) internationally; **4″×6″** sometimes for commercial.
  - **Front:** one strong image (clean editorial headshot or ¾ shot) + model **name** + (often) agency.
  - **Back:** **3–5 images showing range** (4 is the industry standard) + **stats**.
  - It is *not* a business card, profile card, or résumé. Pholio generates these (Puppeteer/EJS) — terminology and layout must match.
- **Digitals / polaroids** — raw, unretouched images for agencies/casting to assess the true look.
  - **Set:** close-up headshot, ¾-length, full-length front, **profile/side**, and **back**; often a **smiling** and a **neutral**; swimwear/form-fitting to show the body honestly.
  - **Rules:** no/minimal makeup, hair down & natural, **plain background** (white wall), soft natural light, **form-fitting neutral clothing** (classic: black tank + skinny jeans/shorts), **no filters/retouching**, **current (≤3 months)**, often **dated**, accompanied by **accurate measurements**.
  - Despite the name "polaroid," these are normally phone photos. "Digitals" and "polaroids" are used interchangeably.
  - **Critical product rule:** if Pholio invites talent to send agencies their *best/retouched* portfolio shots as if they were digitals, it is coaching rejection. Digitals and book are different objects with opposite rules.
- **Book / portfolio** — the curated set of the model's best work (tests + tearsheets + campaigns). The thing a model "shows." Physical book and digital portfolio both exist.
- **Tearsheet** — a page from a published editorial/campaign; proof of real work. Building "tears" builds a career.
- **Test / test shoot** — collaborative shoot (often **TFP** — time/trade for prints) to build the book, try looks, develop new faces. "Testing" is a verb.
- **Headshot** — primary for actors/commercial talent; for fashion it's one image among the book.
- **Showreel / reel** — actors, dancers, hosts, presenters.
- **Stats / measurements card** — the structured data (see §4), shown on the comp card and in submissions.
- **E-comp / digital package / submission** — what a booker sends a client when pitching talent for a job (selected images + stats + availability). Distinct from the public portfolio.

## 4. The measurements / stats data model

Stats are **structured, gendered/division-specific, localized, dual-unit, and perishable** — never a freeform bio blob.

**Women (fashion/editorial baseline):** Height · Bust · Waist · Hips · Dress size · Shoe size · Hair color · Eye color. (Cup size for some commercial/swim.)
**Men:** Height · Chest/Suit · Waist · Inseam · Shoe size · Collar/Neck · Hair · Eyes.
**Shared/extended:** Shoe in **US / EU / UK**; garment sizes localized (US/UK/EU/IT/FR); for parts: ring/glove/nail; for fitness: athletic measures.

**Reference ranges (typical fashion/editorial — gates, not universals; commercial is far wider):**
- Women: height ~5′9″–6′0″ (175–183cm); bust ~32–34; waist ~22–25; hips ~33–35.
- Men: height ~6′0″–6′3″ (183–191cm); chest ~38–40; waist ~30–32; suit ~40R / EU 48.
- **Do not hardcode these as validation.** They exclude curve, petite, commercial, fitness, kids, mature — i.e. most of the actual market. Use them to *contextualize* a board, never to reject.

**Product rules:**
- Store and display **both metric and imperial**; international agencies require **cm**.
- Treat measurements as **versioned with a date**; digitals/stats are expected current (≤3 months) and re-confirmed. A stale stat is a miscast and a wasted booking.
- Units and which fields are required depend on **division** and **gender/category**.
- Measurements are **sensitive personal data** (more so for minors) — privacy and consent apply (§7).

## 5. Agency operations — how a roster actually runs

- **Roster / boards** — talent grouped by board; bookers own their board.
- **Scouting pipeline** — leads → open call / scouting event → request digitals → meeting → development offer or signing. Most inbound is rejected or "kept on file."
- **Development** — new faces get tests, polish, measurement tracking, and placement before they're truly bookable.
- **Submissions / packages** — booker pushes selected talent to a client's **casting** request (brief: dates, usage, budget, look, measurements).
- **Castings / go-sees** — talent meets client/casting director. **Go-see** = general meeting; **casting** = for a specific job; **call-back** = second round; **fitting** = garment fit before/at booking; **direct booking** = booked off the package/digitals without a casting.
- **Options & holds** — the calendar engine of the business (full state machine in `lifecycle.md`): **1st option**, **2nd option**, **hold**, **confirm**, **booked**, **release/cancel**, **bookout** (talent-declared unavailability).
- **The job** — call sheet, call time, rate, usage, wardrobe; on set the **voucher** is signed.
- **Vouchers** — the signed record of hours/rate/usage; the basis the agency bills the client and pays the model. No voucher → no pay.
- **Accounting** — invoice client → collect → deduct commission → **pay talent net, typically 60–90 days** later; advances sometimes offered.
- **Communication** — bookers live in fast inbound/outbound (email, WhatsApp, agency software). Pholio's agency side (inbox, casting board/Kanban, interviews, reminders, commissions) maps to this — audit it against these real steps.

## 6. Money & rights — the part SaaS usually fakes

- **Commission:** legitimate agencies charge **~15–20%** from the talent's fee (20% most common). Many markets also bill the **client a parallel service charge** (e.g. +20%). Mother-agent commission **stacks** (~5–10%) and booking agencies commonly **split 50/50** with the mother agency on their commission.
- **Rates:** quoted as **day rate** / half-day; plus **fitting rate**, **travel**, **overtime**. Editorial often pays little ("for the tears"); commercial/advertising pays via usage.
- **Usage / buyout / licensing** — the big money and the most-faked concept. Pricing is **territory × media × duration × exclusivity**:
  - *Territory:* local / national / regional / worldwide.
  - *Media:* social, web/e-comm, print, OOH/billboard, broadcast/TV, in-store, packaging.
  - *Duration:* 3/6/12 months, multi-year, **in perpetuity** (rare, expensive).
  - **Buyout** = one-time payment for defined usage with no further fees for that window; renewable per year/territory. Bigger reach → bigger buyout.
- **Exclusivity / conflict** — a model under contract to a brand can't shoot a competitor; usage deals often include category exclusivity.
- **Net & slow:** talent are paid **after** the client pays and commission is deducted — **commonly 60–90 days**. Any "you earned $X, paid instantly" with no commission/usage breakdown is fiction. A credible earnings view shows: gross rate, usage/buyout, agency commission (and split), net to talent, and expected pay date / voucher status.

## 7. Legal & compliance — minors and image rights

**Minors (under 18) are a different legal regime, not a smaller adult:**
- **Work permits** (jurisdiction-specific), **guardian/parental consent** on everything, **chaperone** on set, **limited working hours** and schooling/tutoring rules.
- **Trust/Coogan accounts** (California and similar) — a portion of a minor's earnings is protected in trust.
- **Heightened privacy:** measurements, full-length/swim images, and contact data for minors must be tightly controlled and consent-gated. Public exposure of a minor's stats or body images is a serious risk.
- A platform handling kids/teens must **branch** flows: who can see what, who consents, what's collected, how it's stored.

**Image rights & releases (all talent):**
- **Model release** governs how images may be used; usage rights are negotiated (see §6).
- Tests/TFP have their own usage terms between model/photographer.
- **Data protection (GDPR/CCPA):** measurements + photos are personal/biometric-adjacent data; collection, retention, and sharing need lawful basis and consent, especially cross-border (placement abroad).

**Safety / ethics standards:** industry initiatives (e.g. the Model Alliance) push for consent on nudity/sheer, safe sets, no-retouching transparency, and protection from exploitation. Credible talent software respects model agency over their own images and body data.

## 8. Credibility tells — the fast "real vs. fake" checklist

A surface reads **fake** when it:
- Uses wrong words: "selfies" for digitals, "business card" for comp card, "category" for board, "résumé" for book.
- Assumes one model type / one body / one path to representation.
- Ties a talent to exactly one agency; has no mother-agency, placement, or split concept.
- Treats stats as freeform text, single-unit, undated, or with hardcoded fashion-only validation.
- Has only "active/inactive" status — no options, holds, bookouts, availability.
- Shows earnings with no commission, no usage/buyout, and instant payout.
- Handles minors identically to adults; exposes measurements/body images without consent gating.
- Invites talent to send retouched glamour shots to agencies as "digitals."
- Calls a casting a "job application" with a "cover letter," or a go-see an "interview," without the real semantics.

A surface reads **real** when terminology is exact, the data model respects division/units/recency, the state model includes options/holds/availability, money shows commission+usage+net+timing, and minors/privacy are handled as a distinct, consented path.
