# Pholio Strategy Conference — Shared Brief (date: 2026-10-12)

## Company
Pholio is a two-founder, pre-launch startup in NYC with roughly zero real users. Over seven months they built a large app for models and agencies: talent portfolios, comp cards, agency application intake, casting/event modules, Stripe subscriptions and AI photo analysis. The founders now accept that the product never had a strong enough problem. They are open to a full rebuild.

## Fixed constraints and assets
- The name PHOLIO comes from "portfolio". The brand must keep that connection.
- Domain boundary: creative people. That covers professional presence, portfolios, identity, opportunity, and how creatives get found, selected and hired. No healthcare, government benefits or generic B2B.
- **Pholio must NOT own:** payments, money movement, invoicing, collections, contracts, contract enforcement or financial disputes. Its adoption must never require a brand or production to replace its accounting, procurement, payment or contracting infrastructure.
- Assets: a launch opportunity with Fashion Week Brooklyn (FWB). FWB is run by BK Style Foundation, a 501(c)(3) running since 2006, with roughly 40+ designers per season. Designers pay to show. Model registration today runs on Google Forms. Through the founders' network and the FWB ecosystem they can reach models, influencers, artists, musicians, photographers, stylists, hair and makeup artists, and designers.
- The existing codebase already has: an events module (two-stage selection, designer pick lists, offer/confirm), an immutable submission-snapshot and consent-record system, an image pipeline, agency verification against public registries, and a transactional email system.

## Current thesis under attack
"Pholio becomes a living, credible record of a creative person's actual work."
- Each credit records who did what, on which published work, for whom, and when, and links to the work.
- Credits come in three confirmation tiers, each shown in plain text:
  1. **ISSUED**: an organizer confirms credits in bulk, e.g. an event like FWB, a publication, a school show, a gallery or a label.
  2. **MUTUAL**: teammates confirm each other.
  3. **CLAIMED**: self-added and labeled as claimed.
- No ratings, reviews, endorsements or AI quality scores.
- Credits are drafted from work creatives already post (e.g. from Instagram tags), so the record stays current without the creative maintaining it.
- Launch: FWB issues credits for every show participant. Then school thesis shows, indie magazines, and photographers and stylists who shoot weekly.
- Money: free for creatives forever, with a modest Pro tier for presentation. Issuer tools are free at first. The later business is paid search seats on the verified graph for producers, casting directors, agencies and brands, on the IMDbPro model.
- Known precedent risk: The Dots (UK, founded 2014, £5.5M raised, 300–500k members) built team-tagged project credits ("community-driven IMDb") but never became the standard.

## Prior evidence already gathered (cite and extend, don't just repeat)
- Referrals and word of mouth account for about 64–75% of freelance work (Fiverr 2024: 67%; MBO: 75%). Marketplaces account for about 29%.
- IMDb treats film/TV credits as "verified". Commercial, theater and modeling credits are "self-verified" and appear only on IMDbPro.
- Muso.ai: music credits, claimable profiles, roughly 4M creator profiles, Pro at $9.99/mo.
- Instagram's reach rate is down about 14% YoY (Socialinsider 2026). Photographers have public disputes over uncredited brand reposts (Boy v. Forever 21, Quavo).
- AI-altered images: casting directors report applicants who don't match their photos.
- C2PA Content Credentials adoption is uneven. LinkedIn displays them; Instagram's display of them is weak.
- Agencies receive hundreds of applications a week and don't respond. Scams and impersonation are rampant on the talent side.

## Epistemic rules for everyone
- Label every claim as **[FACT]** (with a source URL), **[ASSUMPTION]** (believed but unverified) or **[BET]** (a deliberate wager).
- Never invent statistics. If you can't find a number, say so.
- Attack specifics, not vibes.
