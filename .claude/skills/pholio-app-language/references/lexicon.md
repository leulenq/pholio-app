# The working lexicon

> **Shared layer.** An identical copy lives at `pholio-site/.claude/skills/pholio-site-language/references/lexicon.md`.
> Edit both together; divergence between the copies is a defect.
The modeling industry's real vocabulary, with precise meanings, register
notes, and glossing guidance. Fluency here is a trust signal to both
audiences: a 19-year-old who has read r/modeling for a month and a booker
with twenty years in both instantly discount anyone who gets these wrong.

Compiled from live agency pages (Storm, Ford, The Society, Elite, Next,
Wilhelmina, Heroes, Premier, IMG, Select), The Fashion Spot and BFMA
glossaries, working models' own writing, and coaching sources, August 2026.

---

## 1. The materials

**Book.** The insider's word for a portfolio: 10 to 20 photographs that show
the model's ability to work. "Your book" is possessive and personal, the way
actors say "your reel". Pholio uses "book" natively. "Portfolio" is the
outsider-legible synonym; acceptable in metadata and first-touch surfaces,
but the product speaks "book".

**Comp card** (also composite, card; UK/EU: z-card, zed card; DE: Sedcard).
The industry's business card: 8.5 x 5.5 inches US standard, card stock,
front = hero shot + name, back = 3 to 5 photos + stats + agency block. Left
with the client at a casting. US copy says "comp card". "Composite"
unabbreviated reads dated. Do not say "zed card" to a US audience or
"comp card" to a German one without the local word nearby.

**Digitals** (legacy: polaroids, spoken: polas; agencies also say
"snapshots"). The most load-bearing term for Pholio. Unretouched, plain,
current photos: no makeup, no filters, natural light, plain background,
form-fitting clothes. Retaken constantly; agencies treat a set older than
roughly three months as stale. The native gloss that works: "your audition
tape", against the book as "the feature film". UK/EU still says "polaroids"
dominantly; US says "digitals".

**Tearsheet** (tear sheet, tears). The printed page from a magazine, paper,
or ad the model appeared in. Proof of experience. Editorial work often pays
in tearsheets rather than money; models know this and resent it, so never
romanticize tearsheets as compensation.

**Shot names.** Headshot, close-up, waist-up, three-quarter (3/4),
full-length, profile (always the side view of the face, never a bio page),
eye close-up, back. Agencies say "full-length" more than "full-body". Shot
counts and names vary by agency (2 to 8 shots under 13 observed names),
which is exactly the variance Pholio's spec registry exists to absorb:
always use the target agency's own shot names when writing against a
specific agency.

## 2. Genres and divisions

- **Editorial:** magazine work; prestige, artistry, little or no pay;
  strictest measurements.
- **Commercial:** advertising, catalog; "the girl next door"; steadier and
  better money; relaxed requirements.
- **Runway / show:** catwalk; strictest height; "show season", "show
  package".
- **E-comm:** high-volume e-commerce catalog work; a workhorse booking type.
- **Fitness, parts, fit, petite.** Parts = hands/feet/legs.
- **Curve.** The agency-side word for what retail calls plus-size. "Straight
  size" is the insider antonym. Say "curve" on industry-facing surfaces;
  never "plus-size" as an agency-facing label.
- **Lifestyle, beauty, swimwear, couture** as style registers (these are
  also Pholio's shipped style values).

## 3. Agency structure

- **Board / division.** How an agency organizes its roster; named after the
  physical wall of comp cards. New Faces / Development (newcomers being
  built), Main board (working models), Image board (top models, strictly
  managed), Direct board (booked from pictures without castings). Models
  identify by board: "I'm on the development board."
- **Booker.** The person at the agency who negotiates and books work. UK
  says "booker"; US talent says "my agent" or "my booker". The client-side
  gatekeeper is the **casting director**.
- **Mother agency / mother agent.** Discovers and develops a model, then
  **places** them with agencies in larger markets (placement agencies). "To
  place" is the verb of art. Commission flows agency-to-agency; it does not
  cost the model more to have a mother agent.
- **Open call.** Walk-in hours at an agency, no appointment. "An open call
  is a job interview." (Cattle call is the derisive synonym for any mass
  casting; never use it in copy.)

## 4. The pipeline verbs

- **Casting:** the appointment where the client chooses models; "the process
  of choosing models for a job."
- **Go-see:** US term, "literally: go see someone"; a general meet-the-client
  appointment. UK mostly just says casting.
- **Callback:** round two.
- **Option / hold.** The precision crown jewel. A client requests first
  refusal on a model's dates: "I'm on option for X." First option outranks
  second option. Options are **released** or **confirmed**; a competing
  booking triggers a **book or release**. Never confuse an option with a
  booking.
- **Booking / confirmed:** the job itself; "confirmed" is the word bookers
  send.
- **Direct booking:** booked from pictures or reputation, no casting.
- **Booking out:** the model blocking their own calendar dates.
- **Test / testing** (freelance world: TFP, "time for print"). Portfolio-
  building shoots. Agencies say "test"; "TFP" reads freelance/Instagram.
- **Scouted / signed / placed / represented.** Real industry words, but
  "get scouted" and "get discovered" as promises are scam-coded and banned
  (see banned-language.md). Describing a fact ("she was scouted at an open
  call") is fine; promising the outcome is not.

## 5. Money

- **Day rate / half day.** Eight consecutive hours including lunch; paid in
  full even if not used. Under-24-hour cancellation pays 100%.
- **Usage.** Separate fees per medium, scoped by **medium, term, territory**.
- **Buyout.** Purchasing broad or unlimited usage rights.
- **Commission.** The agency's cut, typically 20% in the US (UK often more).
  "A legitimate agency makes money when you make money" is the community's
  universal legitimacy heuristic.
- **Residuals**, **guarantee** (the advance a model must earn back on a
  foreign placement contract; "making your guarantee" is a real anxiety).

## 6. Measurements and stats

- Order is fixed: women Height, Bust, Waist, Hips, (Dress), Shoe, Hair,
  Eyes. Men: Height, Chest/Suit, Waist, Inseam, Shoe, Hair, Eyes. The bare
  string "34-26-36" is always bust-waist-hips.
- Dual units are the convention on agency sites and models.com:
  `Height 179 – 5'10.5"` etc. US-facing surfaces lead imperial, everywhere
  else metric; shoes given EU/US.
- Weight is conventionally absent from a fashion card (fitness excepted);
  Pholio's shipped rules match (age/DOB never printed for adults; kids
  cards never show body measurements).
- "Stats" is the noun. Being measured is "getting your measurements taken",
  and it is the single most emotionally loaded ritual in the industry:
  write near it with plain restraint, never cuteness.
- Anything mis-ordered, single-unit, or wrongly labeled reads amateur to a
  booker in half a second.

## 7. US vs UK/EU quick table

| Concept | US | UK/EU |
|---|---|---|
| Card | comp card | z-card, zed card; Sedcard (DE) |
| Digitals | digitals, snapshots | polaroids, polas |
| Client meeting | casting, go-see | casting |
| Agent | agent, booker | booker |
| Roster unit | division, board | board |
| Plus-size board | curve | curve |

Pholio's default is US vocabulary and American spelling, with market-aware
switches only on surfaces addressed to a specific non-US market.

## 8. Glossing policy: who gets what explained

Two audiences read the same surfaces. The rule: **write in the native term,
and gloss on first use only where newcomers are the primary audience.**

- **Newcomers reliably do not know:** digitals/polaroids, comp card,
  tearsheet, go-see, option/hold, book or release, mother agency, booking
  out, usage/buyout, new faces, testing. Gloss these with the industry's own
  metaphors, which are job-interview and business metaphors: casting = a job
  interview; comp card = a model's business card; digitals = the audition
  tape; tearsheet = the printed proof; new faces = the development board.
- **Newcomers arrive knowing** (ANTM-era diffusion): portfolio, headshot,
  runway, casting, agency, signed. Do not gloss these.
- **Never gloss on agency-facing surfaces.** Explaining "comp card" to a
  booker is disqualifying.
- A gloss is one clause, set once, in apposition: "your digitals, the plain
  phone photos agencies ask for first". Never a tooltip essay, never a
  glossary dump mid-page.

## 9. Pholio's naming discipline

- One name per object. The portfolio is **the book** ("The Book" as a nav
  proper noun). Photos in it are **frames**. The plain-photo set is
  **digitals**. The card is **the comp card**; its variants are
  **editions** with their shipped labels (The Standard, The Monograph, ...).
- The other side is **agencies** where factually agencies, **Recipients**
  where the contract's full scope applies, **organizers** for events,
  **bookers** when a person is meant. Never "users", never "clients" for
  the talent side, never "customers".
- The people on Pholio are **talent** (umbrella, singular-as-collective) or
  **models** where the surface is specifically about modeling. "Models,
  actors, and creatives" is the full formula when scope needs stating.
- Statuses, lanes, markets, edition names: use the shipped labels from
  product-facts.md verbatim. A label names the axis the thing actually
  varies on (lessons.md §21); read labels out of shipped code, not specs.
