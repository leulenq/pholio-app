# R0. First-principles model of the modeling industry (pre-research hypotheses, to be tested)

Written before reading any research output. Each claim is a hypothesis with a confidence tag
(H = high, M = medium, L = low). The research files R1-R5 confirm, refine, or refute these.

## A. The economic structure that everything else follows from
1. (H) The agency is a broker between clients (brands, magazines, casting directors) and models.
   It earns commission on work it books. It therefore selects talent on *bookability in a market*,
   not on abstract merit. Consequence: intake is a market-fit screen, not a hiring process.
2. (H) The agency's inventory is its roster; its unit of organisation is the board/division
   (women/men/new faces/curve/kids/commercial/classic/talent). Different boards have different
   physical gates and materials. Consequence: any "one model type" assumption is wrong.
3. (H) A model may be represented by several agencies in different markets, non-exclusively, with a
   mother agency that develops and places them. Consequence: "represented" is a relationship with a
   scope (agency, market, exclusivity, dates), not a global boolean on the person.
4. (H) Legitimate agencies do not charge to be seen or considered. Fees flow from bookings only.
   Consequence: any platform that sells visibility/reach/priority to talent is pattern-matched to
   the scam category; any copy implying payment changes outcome is trust-fatal and, in CA/NY,
   legally exposed.
5. (M) The agency's daily operating loop is bookings/options/holds/castings, not intake. Intake
   is a low-priority, high-volume task handled by new-faces / scouting staff, usually via email
   forms and Instagram DMs. Consequence: an intake tool must be lighter than a CRM, and must not
   pretend to be the booking desk.

## B. The intake / submission side, as the agency lives it
6. (H) The inbound object is a *set of plain photos + stats + basics*, not a résumé. Agencies say
   "submit your digitals/polaroids", "send us your natural photos", "apply". The photos are
   deliberately unretouched, no makeup, plain clothes, plain wall, current.
7. (H) Most submissions get no reply. Legitimate agencies say so ("we'll contact you if
   interested", "we cannot respond individually"). Consequence: a platform's default post-submit
   state is *silence*, and honest copy must say silence is the normal outcome, not an anomaly.
8. (M) Intermediate outcomes exist and are common: "we'd like to see more photos", "come in / meet
   us / open call", "we'll keep you on file", "not right now, try again in 6-12 months". Hard
   rejections with reasons are rare; agencies avoid giving reasons (liability, volume).
9. (M) Agencies do not call inbound people "applicants" or "candidates"; they say "submissions",
   "new faces", "girls/boys" (informally), "talent", or just names. "Applicant" reads HR.
10. (M) The meeting before signing is a "meeting", "come in", "casting" (UK), or "go-see" (rarely
    for agency meetings); "interview" is used but reads generic.
11. (H) The signing decision is the agency's; the model's counter-move is to accept/decline the
    contract. Contract types: exclusive/non-exclusive, mother agency, development, term length.
12. (H) Minors: guardian is the counterparty for consent and communication; agencies state
    minimum ages; kids' stats are age/height/clothing size only; permits/trust accounts apply to
    *work*, not to submitting. Consequence: the platform's minor path must branch at consent and
    at what is displayed.

## C. Materials
13. (H) Comp card: printed/PDF leave-behind; front hero + name; back 3-5 images + stats +
    *agency* contact. A represented model's card carries the agency's booking contact, never the
    model's own phone. An unrepresented model's card carries their own contact or none.
14. (H) Digitals/polaroids: separate from the book; raw; time-stamped by convention; re-shot
    often. Putting digitals in the book or book shots in digitals is a category error.
15. (H) Book/portfolio: curated best work; tearsheets are published proof; tests are shoots that
    build the book. Models say "my book"; agencies say "portfolio" on websites for the public.
16. (H) Stats: fixed order (women: height, bust, waist, hips, dress, shoe, hair, eyes; men:
    height, chest, waist, inseam, suit, shoe, hair, eyes), dual units common, shoe localized, no
    weight (except fitness), no age for adults. Software that stores stats freeform, single-unit,
    or undated reads amateur.
17. (L) A "model ID" or Wallet pass has no industry precedent; the nearest artefacts are the comp
    card and the agency's e-comp/package. A pass that reads as a comp card (name, agency, stats,
    QR to book) could be acceptable; one that reads as a credential/badge would be invented.

## D. Casting and events
18. (H) Casting vocabulary: casting, go-see, callback, fitting, option (1st/2nd), hold, confirm,
    release, booked, cancelled, bookout. Options are placed on dates; they are released or
    confirmed; "book or release" resolves conflicts. None of this is intake vocabulary.
19. (H) Event/fashion-week model calls: posted by organizers/designers/casting directors; ask
    height/age/walk/availability; outcomes: shortlisted, confirmed, released/not selected;
    compensation disclosed (paid/unpaid/TFP/gifted); 18+ often required for shows.
20. (M) "Offer" in modeling means a booking offer or a representation offer; "offer letter" is HR.
    "Slot" is used loosely for show line-ups; "pool" is casting-agency internal language.

## E. Trust and truth
21. (H) A platform can know: what was sent, when, to whom, whether it was opened/downloaded,
    whether the recipient recorded an action. It cannot know intent, interest, suitability,
    likelihood, or "readiness". Any UI that says "agency is interested", "you're a strong fit",
    "ready for agencies", "match score" is asserting beyond data. Acceptable: counts and events
    with the observer named ("Opened by Elite NY, 2 Sep").
22. (H) Representation status is a legal fact the platform cannot verify unless both parties
    attest; it must be labelled as "declared by talent" / "recorded by agency" accordingly.
23. (M) "Verified agency" must mean a specific mechanism (registry match, domain check, manual
    review) and the copy must say which.
24. (H) Silence-as-outcome must be attributed to the platform's window, not to the agency
    ("no response within Pholio's 30-day window", not "the agency closed your application").

## F. Words that would make a professional flinch (hypotheses)
- HR/gig: applicant, candidate, interview, pipeline, hire, job, gig, recruiter, offer letter, resume.
- Social/creator: followers-first framing, "get discovered", "get seen", "boost", "exposure".
- SaaS: dashboard, workflow, lead, deal, funnel, CRM stage names, "activity feed".
- Amateur: selfie, photo shoot pics, "model card"/"business card", "measurements: 34/24/34 inches"
  single-unit, "category" for board, "plus-size" on agency surfaces (curve), "cattle call".
- Invented: any coined noun for a standard artefact (e.g. renaming the comp card, the book, digitals,
  or the submission) or any credential-sounding object.

## G. What I expect Pholio-type products get structurally wrong (to check, not assume)
- Treating the submission as a status pipeline with rich agency-side states the agency never uses.
- Representing "represented" as one boolean, no market/agency scope.
- Talent-set availability leaking as "Available" to agencies when it is not a booking-desk fact.
- Coaching book shots into the digitals slot, or requiring a "portfolio" for a submission.
- Signing-board / Kanban naming that assumes agencies "sign" through a tool.
- Notifications that narrate agency intent ("X is reviewing you").
- Minor handling that shows stats or exact age to agencies.
