# R3: What "Verified" Can Mean, and How Credential and Credit Systems Get Gamed

Lane: trust and safety. Prepared 2026-10-12 for the Pholio strategy conference.
Tags: **[FACT](url)** = sourced. **[INFERENCE]** = my reasoning from the facts. **[ASSUMPTION]** = believed, not verified. **[GAP]** = searched for, not found.

Bottom line first: every credential system in this survey that tied its badge to something cheap (money, self-assertion, a friend's click) lost the badge's meaning within months to a few years. The ones that hold up tie the badge to an issuer that has something to lose, plus an artifact the claim can be checked against. Pholio's three tiers are only as strong as (a) who is allowed to be an ISSUER and (b) whether a credit points at a checkable artifact. MUTUAL is the tier most open to gaming. ISSUED is the tier with the worst failure mode, because a fake or pay-to-play issuer mints "verified" credits in bulk.

## 1. Credit fraud in existing credit databases

**IMDb (film/TV)**
- [FACT](https://help.imdb.com/article/imdbpro/manage-your-imdb-page/verified-affiliations-on-imdbpro/GA8B6VK2JXAAWNME) IMDb Help separates "verified" credits (film, made-for-TV, music video) from "self-verified" credits (commercials, modeling, theater, dance, voice-over). Self-verified credits show only on the IMDbPro page. In other words, IMDb itself declines to vouch for modeling credits, which is Pholio's core category. (A separate IMDb eligibility page lists commercials as an eligible title type, so the line is blurry. [FACT](https://origin-help.imdb.com/article/contribution/filmography-credits/eligible-credits/GFXKZE9VXLB5CR8M))
- [FACT](https://qz.com/imposters-game-bollywood-imdb-pages-to-fool-google-1849393865) Quartz (2022) reported dozens of fabricated IMDb profiles with fake top-billed cast credits on major Indian releases, including *KGF: Chapter 2*. The profiles were used to get Google knowledge-panel-style legitimacy. IMDb's edit history is not public, unlike Wikipedia's. Original discovery: [FACT](https://peabee.substack.com/p/16-the-case-of-fake-imdb-credits).
- [FACT](https://fiverr.com/buzzmoremedia/create-new-imdb-page) Fiverr sellers openly offer IMDb page creation in packages ("20 film and TV credits" in a base tier), plus STARmeter promotion ([FACT](https://www.fiverr.com/s/Eg7YdaD)). I did not verify that these sellers add credits for work never done. [GAP]
- [FACT](https://www.overlawyered.com/2007/05/so-you-want-to-be-in-pictures/) A litigant who claimed producer credits (incl. *My Big Fat Greek Wedding*) sued IMDb over their omission. The court treated it as a SLAPP suit and ordered him to pay IMDb $6,270 in fees. Lesson: hosts of a credit database get sued both for including and for omitting credits.
- [FACT](https://www.thewrap.com/it-producer-roy-lee-accused-of-fraud-over-fees-and-credits-for-dozens-of-projects-in-lawsuit/) A lawsuit against producer Roy Lee alleges that someone on Lee's team contacted IMDb to have the plaintiff's executive-producer credits removed. These are allegations only. The point is that credits are a currency people fight over, and the database becomes the battlefield.
- [FACT](https://screendaily.com/pga-to-take-legal-action-against-phoney-producer-credits/4020425.article) The Producers Guild of America ran a campaign against counterfeit "produced by" credits. The guild's "p.g.a." certification mark exists because the raw credit had been devalued. This is the closest real precedent for an issuer-certified credit.
- [FACT](https://www.comedy.co.uk/forums/thread/29297/2/) Crowdfunding tiers sell "executive producer" credits to backers, so a credit can be bought legitimately with no work done. [INFERENCE] Pholio will see the same thing: a designer at a pay-to-show event can "issue" a credit to anyone who paid.

**Music (Spotify, Muso.ai)**
- [FACT](https://www.hypebot.com/spotify-has-a-big-fake-artist-problem-but-did-it-pay-to-create-it/) Music Business Worldwide named 50 "fake artists" (pseudonymous producer acts on Spotify's own playlists) with more than 520M streams. Spotify denied the allegation. The listed name was not the real creator.
- [FACT](https://lennoncihak.substack.com/p/fake-artist-scam) One researcher found 49 versions of the same 53-second track uploaded under different names with different writing credits. Distributor-entered credits are self-asserted and unchecked.
- [FACT](https://gzeromedia.com/amp/the-10-million-spotify-scam-2669158964) US prosecutors charged Michael Smith with taking about $10M in streaming royalties using AI-generated songs credited to fake bands. That is fake creators at industrial scale.
- [FACT](https://rightstech.com/2020/10/muso-ai-is-fixing-the-industrys-credits-problem-starting-with-creators/) Muso.ai lets users amend credit data only after ID verification (scan of an ID card). Changes are ultimately validated by artists and master-rights holders before being shared with data partners. [INFERENCE] Muso's defense is the one Pholio needs: identity plus a *rights-holder* confirmer, not a peer. [GAP] I found no public Muso dispute-resolution process or any data on disputed-credit rates.

**models.com**
- [GAP] I found no documented models.com credit-error or fraud cases. models.com credits are editorially curated, with agencies submitting and staff publishing. [ASSUMPTION] It is a closed issuer model with low fraud and low coverage. Treat it as the "editorial issuer" end of the spectrum, not as evidence.

## 2. "Verified" badge markets and how badges lose meaning

- [FACT](https://fortune.com/2022/11/11/no-free-insulin-eli-lilly-casualty-of-elon-musk-twitter-blue-verification-mess) In Nov 2022 an $8/month Twitter Blue check let an impersonator of Eli Lilly tweet "insulin is free". The stock fell about 2.2% that day ([FACT](https://semafor.com/article/11/11/2022/eli-lilly-stock-price-tumbles-after-fake-tweet)), and Twitter paused paid verification ([FACT](https://www.nbcchicago.com/news/business/money-report/twitter-pauses-paid-verifications-after-users-abuse-service-to-impersonate-brands-and-people/)).
- [FACT](https://www.axios.com/2023/04/20/twitter-blue-checkmarks-verifications-removed) On 2023-04-20 Twitter removed legacy checks. Blue then meant "paying subscriber", gold meant "Verified Organization" at $1,000/month, and grey meant government.
- [FACT](https://www.newsguardtech.com/press/most-americans-put-misplaced-trust-in-xs-blue-check-verification-system/) A NewsGuard/YouGov survey (Nov 2023) found that 60% of X users did not know the blue check now essentially means payment, and about a quarter wrongly believed it meant the account is authentic. **This is the central lesson: users keep the old meaning of a badge long after its criteria change.** [INFERENCE] A Pholio "ISSUED" label will be read as "Pholio vouches for this person", whatever the fine print says.
- [FACT](https://dailyhive.com/canada/x-misinformation-israel-hamas-war) NewsGuard: 186 of the 250 most-engaged misinformation posts in the first week of the Israel–Hamas war (74%) came from X-verified accounts.
- [FACT](https://appleinsider.com/articles/23/02/19/meta-verified-is-mark-zuckerbergs-1199-competitor-to-twitter-blue) Meta Verified launched in Feb 2023 at $11.99/month (web) or $14.99/month (iOS). It requires a government ID matching the profile name and photo ([FACT](https://www.bitdefender.co.uk/blog/hotforsecurity/meta-seeks-to-boost-authenticity-and-security-for-facebook-and-instagram-users-with-paid-verified-badge/)). [INFERENCE] Meta's badge proves "a real adult with this name pays us". It says nothing about professional standing, yet in creative circles it is read as notability.
- [FACT](https://siliconangle.com/blog/2017/09/03/details-6m-instagram-accounts-sale-online-employees-accused-corruption/) In 2017 Mashable reported Instagram verification being sold through insiders for "anywhere from a bottle of wine to $15,000". [INFERENCE] Any manual, discretionary verifier is open to bribery and social engineering, including Pholio's staff and any FWB volunteer with issuer rights.
- [FACT](https://www.malaymail.com/news/tech/gadgets/2021/04/27/how-much-is-your-social-media-profile-worth-on-the-dark-web/1969734) Dark Web Price Index 2021: hacked Instagram accounts sold for about US$45. [INFERENCE] If Pholio uses "owns this Instagram handle" as proof of identity, the attack costs about $45 plus phishing.
- [FACT](https://www.kaspersky.com/blog/beware-of-twitter-blue-fake-accounts/49199/) Scammers bought blue checks to impersonate support accounts (e.g., Booking.com). The giveaways were a stray hyphen and a recent join date. [INFERENCE] Account age and handle similarity are cheap, effective signals Pholio should show next to any badge.

## 3. Collusion, Sybils, and endorsement inflation in peer-confirmation systems

- [FACT](https://scholarworks.boisestate.edu/cs_facpubs/162) Wu, Dhakal, Xu & Cho (IEEE 2018) found that raw LinkedIn endorsement counts have "serious limitations" when false endorsements exist "for self-promotion or collusive promotion". Weighting by endorser authority or trustworthiness performs better. They estimate 4–6% false endorsements in their real dataset. Authority weighting stays best up to 10–25% false endorsements.
- [FACT](https://hrmonline.com.au/?p=577) Practitioners describe endorsements as a "popularity contest of no real substance", and reciprocity ("endorse back") is the norm. [GAP] I found no peer-reviewed reciprocity rate, and I will not quote a number.
- [FACT](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/social-business/linkedins-looking-make-endorsements-more-relevant-heres-how) LinkedIn itself built about 84 candidate signals to judge whether an endorsement is accurate. That means the platform had to model endorser credibility because the raw peer signal was not usable.
- [FACT](https://press.aboutamazon.com/2022/7/amazon-targets-fake-review-fraudsters-on-social-media) Amazon sued admins of more than 10,000 Facebook groups that brokered fake reviews. One group had over 43,000 members, and posts disguised "refund after review" to avoid moderation. Amazon reports more than 12,000 staff working on fraud and abuse. [INFERENCE] Peer-confirmation abuse gets organized into marketplaces once the signal has value. A MUTUAL-confirmation ring among 20 aspiring models is trivial to coordinate in a group chat.
- [FACT](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials) The FTC's rule on fake reviews and testimonials took effect 2024-10-21. It covers buying and selling fake reviews/testimonials and fake social-media indicators, with civil penalties up to $51,744 per violation in 2024, inflation-adjusted ([FACT](https://dwt.com/insights/2024/08/ftc-finalizes-rule-banning-fake-consumer-reviews)). [INFERENCE] Pholio has no reviews, but if paid search seats are sold on "verified" credits that turn out to be bought, the FTC framing of fake social indicators is close enough to matter.
- [FACT](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~yuhf/sybilguard-sigcomm06.pdf) SybilGuard (SIGCOMM 2006) rests on the idea that Sybil clusters connect to the honest graph through only a few "attack edges". SybilRank was deployed at Tuenti and processed 160M nodes ([FACT](https://en.blogthinkbig.com/sybilrank-telefonica)).
- [FACT](https://people.mpi-sws.org/~gummadi/papers/sybil_defense.pdf) These graph defenses assume few attack edges, and that assumption often fails in practice. A 2017 study of review networks (Yelp/Dianping/Amazon) describes "elite Sybil" attacks that recruit real, highly rated users. Their fake reviews bypass filters at higher rates ([FACT](https://par.nsf.gov//servlets/purl/10087133)). [INFERENCE] In Pholio's world the attackers are *real* creatives doing favors for friends, which is the elite-Sybil case where graph analysis is weakest.

**Defenses that work (synthesized)** [INFERENCE, drawing on the facts above]
1. *Issuer reputation with stake*: weight a confirmation by who confirms it (Wu et al. authority score; PGA mark; Muso's rights-holder validation).
2. *Artifact linking*: a credit must point to a published work that exists independently, so a claim can be refuted by looking at it.
3. *Asymmetry*: mutual confirmations that are only reciprocal pairs or closed cliques get discounted. Confirmations from an *outside* party (a publication, a client) count more.
4. *Rate limits and velocity checks*: cap how many credits a new account can confirm per period. Flag bursts.
5. *Visible provenance*: show "confirmed by whom, when" instead of a bare tier word, so the reader judges the confirmer.

## 4. Identity proof options: what they prove, what they cost

- [FACT](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/verified--linkedin-crosses-100m-member-milestone) LinkedIn passed 100M members with a verification in 2025. It offers a self-serve API so other platforms can show LinkedIn-verified identity and workplace. LinkedIn claims up to 60% more profile views for verified members (company-reported).
- [FACT](https://news.linkedin.com/2023/april/linkedin-s-new-verification-features-include-clear--microsoft-en) LinkedIn identity verification runs through CLEAR in the US and Persona elsewhere (NFC passport; [FACT](https://news.linkedin.com/2023/february/LinkedIn-and-Persona-verification-expansion-to-16-more-countries)). Workplace verification uses a work email or Microsoft Entra Verified ID. [INFERENCE] Workplace verification does not fit freelancers, since a stylist has no corporate email. That removes LinkedIn's strongest signal for exactly Pholio's population.
- [FACT](https://about.linkedin.com/transparency/community-report) LinkedIn's Jul–Dec 2025 report says 99.7% of the fake accounts it stopped were stopped proactively, 97.8% by automated defenses. Absolute counts did not render on the page. A secondary source puts 2023 removals/blocks at more than 121M ([FACT](https://besedo.com/blog/linkedin-fake-accounts/), secondary).
- [FACT](https://withpersona.com/pricing) Persona's Essential plan starts at $250/month with a 12-month minimum. Pricing is usage-based and per-step. Veriff lists about $0.80 per verification and iDenfy about $1.35 ([FACT](https://starterdocs.js.org/docs/comparisons/verify-id), secondary, may be stale). [INFERENCE] Verifying 1,000 FWB participants costs low four figures a year, which is affordable. The real cost is friction: an ID selfie flow loses some share of users. [GAP] I found no credible public drop-off rate.
- [FACT](https://developers.secure.facebook.com/docs/instagram-basic-display-api) Meta shut down the Instagram Basic Display API on 2024-12-04, and its docs state it "is not an authentication solution". The replacement Instagram API with Instagram Login supports Business/Creator accounts ([FACT](https://smashballoon.com/instagram-is-shutting-down-basic-display-api-continue-displaying-instagram-feeds-on-your-site/)). [INFERENCE] Handle ownership can be proven by OAuth (needs a professional account) or by a bio-code challenge (put token X in your bio). Both prove *control of the account now*, not that the person is the one in the photos, and not who did the work.

## 5. Artifact provenance: C2PA, reverse search, AI fakes, stolen portfolios

- [FACT](https://c2pa.org/faq/) The C2PA FAQ states that "the core C2PA Content Credentials specification does not support attribution of content to individuals or organizations". It proves integrity (tampering breaks the signed hash) and the history of edits or AI generation. **It does not prove who made the image, and it cannot prove that a stylist or MUA worked on it.**
- [FACT](https://www.softwareseni.com/how-c2pa-content-credentials-work-and-what-their-limits-are/) Re-encoding on WhatsApp/iMessage/Facebook and screenshots strip credentials silently. A missing credential proves nothing, and a validly signed photo of a screen showing a fake is still "valid" (secondary source). [INFERENCE] Pholio drafts credits from Instagram, which strips C2PA, so the provenance chain is usually already broken by the time Pholio sees the image.
- [FACT](https://petapixel.com/2010/09/15/photographer-offers-groupon-deal-using-stolen-photographs-chaos-ensures) The Dana Dawes case (2010): a photographer sold 1,175 Groupon packages on a portfolio mostly stolen from other photographers. Commenters exposed it with reverse image search.
- [FACT](https://www.insidehook.com/internet/war-photographer-tricked-everybody-didnt-exist/amp) "Eduardo Martins" (2017) placed stolen and mirrored conflict photos with outlets including the BBC. Mirroring was used specifically to defeat reverse-image search, and Getty removed his images. [INFERENCE] Reverse search catches lazy theft, not deliberate theft.
- [FACT](https://petapixel.com/2014/01/16/new-york-photographer-brags-stealing-photos-called-biz/) A New York wedding photographer filled his portfolio with others' work and called it "biz". This is common enough to have watchdog sites.
- [FACT](https://petapixel.com/2025/07/29/ai-generated-models-now-appear-in-vogue-magazine/) Guess ran AI-generated "models" (by Seraphinne Vallora) in the August 2025 US Vogue. The disclosure was a small note that is easy to miss. Levi's 2023 Lalaland.ai test drew similar backlash ([FACT](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/ai-models-levis-controversy-backlash-rcna77280)). [INFERENCE] A credit like "model: Vivienne" can be issued for a person who does not exist, so an issuer can be honest and the credited "creative" still be synthetic. ID verification of the credited person is the only check.
- [FACT](https://www.cbc.ca/1.6397323) Model Alina Leasenco's photos and name were used for fake profiles selling faked adult content, twice. Jess Davies reports chronic catfishing with her old images and an ID-plus-form burden to report each fake ([FACT](https://nation.cymru/news/instagram-making-it-harder-to-report-fakes-says-welsh-former-glamour-model/)). [INFERENCE] A credible public credit graph makes a model's likeness *more* valuable to steal. Impersonation reports have to be a first-class flow.

## 6. Legal and safety exposure

**Disputed credits and defamation**
- [FACT](https://thefader.com/2014/09/23/alicia-keyes-stylists-legal-fracas) Credit attribution on Instagram is contractual and political. Alicia Keys's team said tagging a designer would have breached a Givenchy contract. Stylist credit fights go public ([FACT](https://www.bellanaija.com/?p=421342), the Tonto Dikeh/Toyin Lawani case). Brands and celebrities strip creatives' credits ([FACT](https://www.papermag.com/instagram-2649343382.html)). [INFERENCE] "I did the styling, not her" disputes are routine. Some credits are *deliberately* omitted for contract reasons, and auto-drafting from tags will sometimes surface a credit that a party is contractually barred from claiming publicly.
- [FACT](https://www.minclaw.com/legal-resource-center/what-is-section-230-of-the-communication-decency-act-cda/) Under Section 230 (Zeran v. AOL), a US platform is generally immune for third-party content even after notice. Immunity does **not** cover content the platform itself creates. [INFERENCE] This is the key design point. A credit a *user* claims is third-party content. A credit *Pholio drafts* from Instagram tags and labels "ISSUED" or "MUTUAL" may count as Pholio's own content or material contribution, which weakens 230 protection. Have humans confirm drafts before they are published, and show the confirmer's name.
- [FACT](https://www.overlawyered.com/2007/05/so-you-want-to-be-in-pictures/) A director sued Amazon/IMDb for $2.15M over a false credit linking her to pornographic films (defamation/libel). Wrong credits can be defamatory, not just inaccurate.

**Right of publicity**
- [FACT](https://www.dmlp.org/node/11202) NY Civil Rights Law §§50–51 bars use of a living person's name, portrait, picture or voice "for advertising purposes or for the purposes of trade" without prior written consent. Damages are available, plus exemplary damages if the use was knowing. The limitations period is 1 year, and there is a newsworthiness exception. [INFERENCE] Showing a non-member's name and face on a credit page is probably fine as information. Using it in Pholio's *paid search* product or marketing ("find talent like X") is closer to "purposes of trade". Get counsel on this before the IMDbPro-style business launches.

**Minors**
- [FACT](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/resource-center/models-laws-governing-the-modeling-industry-in-the-u-s) Since 2013, NY treats under-18 print and runway models as child performers. Employers need a DOL certificate, must notify the DOL before fittings and shows, must keep records, and must pay at least 15% of earnings into a trust. Under-16s need a designated responsible person and have hour limits ([FACT](https://hollywoodmomblog.com/child-models-modelallianceny-new-law-passed-protecting-child-models-16-younger-goes-into-effect-on-11-20-13/)).
- [FACT](https://unanswered.io/guide/do-models-have-to-pay-to-walk-in-new-york-fashion-week) A non-authoritative guide reports designers charging parents $1,200–$1,500+ for child models to walk. [GAP] I could not verify this independently.
- [INFERENCE] A credits network that publishes "who walked which show, when" turns into a schedule of where a minor was, and with forward-dated casting or "confirmed for" states, where she *will be*. Minors' credits should be (a) never forward-dated publicly, (b) published only after the event, (c) guardian-controlled, (d) excluded from paid search by default. GDPR Art 17(1)(f) also gives a specific erasure ground for data collected from children through information-society services ([FACT](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-17-gdpr/)).

**Deletion vs. an "immutable record"**
- [FACT](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-17-gdpr/) GDPR Art 17(3) exceptions to erasure are freedom of expression and information, legal obligation or public task, public health, archiving and research, and legal claims. There is no exception for "our product is an immutable record".
- [FACT](https://www.lockelord.com/newsandevents/publications/2019/11/deletion-completion-under-the-ccpa) CCPA §1798.105(d) lists deletion exceptions including exercising free speech, security and fraud detection, and legal obligations. Legal claims sit in §1798.145.
- [FACT](https://www.perkinscoie.com/en/news-insights/french-data-protection-authority-issues-guidance-on-application.html) The CNIL's blockchain guidance: keep personal data off-chain and only a hash or reference on the immutable layer, so erasure is possible by deleting the underlying data. [INFERENCE] "Living, credible record" must mean *append-only audit log + deletable personal data*, not truly immutable. If creative A deletes her account, the credits other people earned on the same work must survive with A shown as "a collaborator", which is a real data-model requirement.

**DMCA**
- [FACT](https://blog.galalaw.com/post/102ftwe/tis-the-season-make-certain-that-you-renew-your-dmca-designated-agent-with-the) The §512(c) hosting safe harbor needs a designated agent registered with the Copyright Office, renewed every 3 years, plus a repeat-infringer policy that is actually enforced ([FACT](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/11/dmca-designated-agent-safe-harbor-takedown-repeat-infringer-guide), secondary). [INFERENCE] Pholio will host images that many people credit, and the photographer, not the model, usually owns the copyright. Expect takedowns *between collaborators* on the same credit. Section 230 does not cover IP claims ([FACT](https://www.minclaw.com/legal-resource-center/what-is-section-230-of-the-communication-decency-act-cda/)), so DMCA is the only shield there.

## 7. Scam vectors a credits network would create

- [FACT](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/modeling-scams) FTC: modeling scammers charge for test shoots and photos or to "secure your spot". Fake agencies display photos of models they never represented and name brands that supposedly hired their talent. [INFERENCE] An ISSUED credit becomes the new "brands that hired us" prop: a fake agency can point to a Pholio-issued credit graph.
- [FACT](https://form.jotform.com/260485991034159) Pay-to-walk is live at NYFW-branded independent shows. One current registration form charges a $625 total fee, which includes an outfit, hair and makeup, the runway walk and photos. [INFERENCE] If such an organizer is an ISSUER, Pholio converts a $625 purchase into a "verified" runway credit that looks identical to a paid booking. That is pay-to-play credential laundering.
- [FACT](https://edgexpo.com/2017/02/21/edge-fast-fact-fashion-week-designer-costs) Independent "fashion weeks" charge designers $250–$1,000 (Harlem Fashion Week) up to $1,500–$25,000 (Nolcha) to show. The brief notes FWB's designers pay to show too. [INFERENCE] Pay-to-show on the *designer* side is the norm and not a scam. The integrity question is whether *talent* paid, and whether the event happened at the claimed scale.
- [FACT](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/chicago/news/designer-fashion-show-producer-scam-claim) A Texas designer says he paid thousands for a Chicago show that never happened, and the producer went silent the day before. [INFERENCE] A fake issuer can mint credits for an event that never took place. Issuer onboarding must verify the event, not just the organization.
- [FACT](https://dk.fashionnetwork.com/news/Philipp-Plein-defrauded-of-900-000,1066717.html) An impersonator of Kanye West's circle took about $900K from Philipp Plein with a forged signature for a fake NYFW performance. Brokers sell fashion-week invitations that Chanel says are never sold ([FACT](https://louispisano.substack.com/p/inside-the-fashion-week-black-market-836)). [INFERENCE] Organizer impersonation is proven at the top of the market. Expect "Fashion Week Brooklyn casting" lookalike accounts that DM models a Pholio link, or a fake FWB-styled issuer page, the moment FWB credits carry value.
- [FACT](https://www.mvsb.com/2025/12/30/ftc-alerts-consumers-to-virtual-casting-call-scams/) Dec 2025 reporting describes an FTC warning about fake virtual casting calls that use professional sites and fake contracts (secondary; FTC original not confirmed). [INFERENCE] A messaging or "opportunity" layer on top of a verified graph is the highest-risk surface. Scammers will target exactly the people with credits, because credits signal ambition.

## Verification ladder (proposal)

Principle: every tier label must answer **"who vouches, with what at stake, about what exactly?"** Show that answer in plain text next to the credit, not a bare tier word, because users will read any badge as "Pholio vouches for this person" (NewsGuard 60% finding). Identity verification is a property of the *person*, and credit confirmation is a property of the *claim*. Keep them separate. Twitter's mistake was merging them into one check.

**Tier 0: CLAIMED** ("Added by [name]")
- Means: the account holder says so. Nothing else.
- Requirement to resist gaming: must link a public artifact (URL or image) and show account age. Claims cannot feed paid search ranking.
- Still open to: outright fabrication, stolen portfolios (Dawes, Martins), AI-generated work, and claiming work that was contractually un-credited. Acceptable only because the label says exactly what it is.

**Tier 1: IDENTITY-VERIFIED PERSON** (applies to the person, not the credit: "ID checked")
- Means: a government ID plus liveness matches the profile photo (Persona/CLEAR/Veriff, about $1/check), *and* control of the linked Instagram handle (OAuth or bio-code).
- Resists: synthetic models (Guess/Vallora type), catfish accounts built from a real model's photos, and multiple accounts per person (Sybil inflation of MUTUAL).
- Still open to: hacked or bought handles (about $45; re-check on handle change), a real person claiming false work, insider bribery (Instagram 2017), and minors lying about age. Required for anyone who confirms others' credits or acts as an issuer.

**Tier 2: MUTUAL** ("Confirmed by [name, role] and [name, role]")
- Means: at least two *ID-verified* collaborators on the same linked artifact confirm each other's roles. At least one confirmer must sit outside the claimant's existing confirmation cluster (no closed pairs).
- Defenses: Tier 1 required to confirm; confirmations weighted by the confirmer's own issued-credit history (the Wu et al. authority score); velocity caps for new accounts; reciprocal-only pairs and cliques flagged and down-weighted; confirmers can be challenged, and a confirmer caught lying loses confirming rights across their whole graph.
- Still open to: friend rings of real people doing favors ("elite Sybil", where graph detection is weakest), role inflation ("assistant" becoming "stylist"), and collusion on a real artifact. **Recommendation: MUTUAL should never be sold as "verified" in the paid product. It is social corroboration, not verification.**

**Tier 3: ISSUED** ("Issued by [org], [event, date]")
- Means: an issuer *organization* (not a person) confirms who held which role at a specific, independently evidenced event or publication.
- Issuer onboarding must include: legal-entity check (e.g., FWB's 501(c)(3) status, which the existing agency-registry verification can reuse); event evidence (venue, date, public schedule or press); named accountable signatory who is ID-verified; a written issuer attestation that **no talent paid to participate** (or the credit is labeled "paid participation"); and per-event caps matched to plausible headcount.
- Defenses: issuer reputation is public and revocable. One substantiated fraud revokes *all* of that issuer's credits retroactively, with a visible notice. Issuers cannot issue credits to their own accounts. Dispute window before credits go public.
- Still open to: pay-to-walk laundering ($625 NYFW-branded shows), fake or never-happened events (Chicago case), organizer impersonation and lookalike issuers (Plein-type), compromised issuer accounts minting bulk credits, and issuer error at scale (one bad CSV mis-credits 200 people). This tier has the **largest blast radius**, so issuer count should grow slowly and by hand.

**Tier 4 (optional, later): ISSUED + ARTIFACT-BOUND** ("Issued by Vogue Italia, September 2026, page 112")
- Means: an issued credit plus a durable artifact Pholio has archived (a C2PA-signed original from the photographer where available, or a hash of the published page or file), where the issuer is also the publisher or rights holder.
- Still open to: C2PA proving integrity but not authorship (per C2PA's own FAQ), stripped credentials in the Instagram pipeline, and publisher errors. This is the strongest tier available, and it remains a record of what an accountable party *said*, not a proof of truth.

**Cross-tier rules the record needs to survive law and safety:**
1. Personal data is deletable. The log of confirmations is append-only (CNIL pattern). Departed collaborators become "a collaborator".
2. Minors: no forward-dated or "confirmed for upcoming" credits, publication only after the event, guardian control, excluded from paid search.
3. Pholio-drafted credits (from Instagram tags) stay private drafts until a human confirms them. Otherwise Pholio becomes the author (Section 230 risk).
4. Disputes: the disputed state is shown in plain text ("Role disputed by [name]"). Pholio does not adjudicate creative credit beyond issuer confirmation.
5. DMCA agent registered and a repeat-infringer policy in place before launch. Impersonation reporting is first-class and works for non-members.
6. Paid search seats expose Tier 1 identity plus Tier 3–4 credits only, with MUTUAL and CLAIMED clearly separated. Selling access to unverified claims as a "verified graph" is the Twitter Blue failure, with an FTC angle.
