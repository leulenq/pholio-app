# R4 — Casting, go-sees, options/bookings, and event / fashion-week model calls (2026)

Research topic 4 of the industry brief. All sources accessed **2026-09-03** unless noted. Evidence rules per
`BRIEF.md`: primary sources first, verbatim where wording matters, region and source-count labelled, gaps
declared. No Pholio product code, docs or skill files were read.

---

## 1. Summary of the mental model

There are **two structurally different systems** that both get called "casting", and conflating them is the
single biggest modelling error a product can make.

**System A — the represented pipeline (agency ↔ client, the industry's default).**
A client (brand, magazine, designer, production, or a casting director hired by one) issues a brief. Agencies
respond by sending a **package** — "digital cards of the models they're pushing that season, with measurements,
walk videos, and recent images" (Vibrant Casting). The client either **requests** specific models, calls a
**casting** / **go-see**, or **direct books** off the package with no meeting at all. Interest hardens through a
graded ladder of non-binding-to-binding claims on the model's calendar: *request → casting → callback →
availability check → option (1st / 2nd) / hold → confirm → fitting → job*, with **release** as the exit at any
point. **The model is not a party to any of this.** Everything is negotiated agency ↔ client; the model is told
by their booker. The model's only unilateral moves are to **book out** (declare unavailable) and to show up.

**System B — the open event call (organizer ↔ individual, how most fashion-week-adjacent events actually run).**
A production company or regional fashion week posts a public **model call** / **open casting** with a form.
Anyone applies. The organizer screens, runs an **in-person casting / model call / audition**, then produces a
**roster / pool / shortlist** which it hands to the participating **designers**, who make the actual selection.
This pick-list step is not folklore — organizers describe it explicitly in their own copy (Omaha, FashioNXT,
Greenville, The Bureau, Fashion Week Haus; §2.3). Compensation here is usually *not money*: hair and makeup,
photos, a program-book credit, "exposure". The FashioNXT page states the split baldly: agency models get
"$60 per Calendar Show or Shoot Date"; unaffiliated models get a "New Face Development Program" of benefits.

Three things are true across both systems and shape every state model:

1. **Interest is graded and revocable, and the grades have names.** Nothing goes from "seen" to "booked". A
   product with a binary applied/accepted model is describing HR software, not casting.
2. **Silence is the default reply.** Every legitimate player I sampled either says so explicitly ("If you were
   not selected, you will not receive any notification" — Omaha Fashion Week) or writes the equivalent ("we are
   unable to respond to all inquiries" — ONE Management). Nobody sends rejections at scale.
3. **The decision is a fit judgement, not a merit ranking.** "Each designer chooses their own models based on
   what fits their collection best" (Omaha); "designers cast for their own collections… demand for your look
   shifts season to season" (Vibrant/Bureau). Practitioners say *not selected*, *not right for this*, *released*
   — never *rejected*, *unqualified*, *did not meet the bar*.

## 2. Vocabulary

### 2.1 The option / booking ladder (the core of the topic)

| Term | Meaning | Who says it | Region | Ev. count | Verbatim |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Request** | Client asks the agency for a named model specifically, rather than a type | booker, CD | universal | 4 | "If you are requested by a designer you will hear directly from…" — KCFW Model FAQ. Go-sees can be "specific: the client has requested you specifically" — theFashionSpot glossary |
| **Package** | The set of digital cards an agency sends a client/CD for a brief or a season | booker, CD | universal | 3 | "Agencies send packages: digital cards of the models they're pushing that season, with measurements, walk videos, and recent images" — Vibrant Casting. Syngency ships a top-level **Packages** feature area |
| **Availability check / avail check / "check her chart"** | Non-binding question: is she free on these dates? | booker, CD | US (avail), universal (availability) | 3 | "'On Avail' typically indicates that a talent, or their representative, has confirmed that they are available to work during the shoot dates… This is typically the first step towards booking someone" — Casting Networks status docs |
| **Pin** | Informal strong interest; talent may still take other work but is asked to flag conflicts | CD (film/commercial, bleeds into fashion) | US | 3 | "A 'Pin' is an informal expression of strong interest… It is not a formal booking, and multiple actors may be pinned for the same role" — Casting Networks |
| **First refusal** | Client gets first chance to book before the model accepts a clashing job | booker, CD | US + UK | 3 | "A client has requested the right of first refusal on a model during a specified time" — theFashionSpot (definition of *option*) |
| **Option / hold** | A dated claim on the model's calendar. Model must keep the dates free; the client has not committed | **booker (primary owner of this word)**, CD, model | universal | 7 | "OPTION: Also referred to as a 'hold'. A client has requested the right of first refusal on a model during a specified time. That means the model is obligated to be available for the client's job if booked, and may not accept any other assignment." — theFashionSpot glossary |
| **First option** | This client has priority for those dates | booker | universal | 4 | "A 'first option' means that client has priority over any other for that time period" — theFashionSpot |
| **Second option / second hold** | Next in line if the first option releases | booker | universal | 4 | "A 'second option' (or 'second hold') means the client has a claim to the model if the person holding the first option decides not to book the model for that time" — theFashionSpot |
| **Book or release** | Forcing move: the first-option client must confirm or give up the model, usually within 24h | booker | US (idiomatic); UK equivalent is the 24h provisional-booking rule | 3 | "'Book or release' will be enforced when secondary jobs are ready to confirm" — Vision Models LA, Booking Conditions. "Weather permit options: book or release will be enforced for options when secondary job is ready to confirm" — The Rock Agency |
| **Provisional booking** | UK contractual name for what the US calls an option | booker (UK) | **UK** | 2 | "Provisional bookings will be automatically cancelled if they are not confirmed by the client (by signing the booking confirmation form) within 24 hours of the proposed booking." — Tess Management; near-identical in ILM Models §5 |
| **Confirm / confirmed** | The option becomes a real booking. In UK terms this is a signed **booking confirmation form** | booker, CD, model | universal | 8 | "An option is a hold, not a booking. Confirmations often land a day or two before the show, sometimes the night before." — Vibrant Casting |
| **Booking** | The confirmed job itself | everyone | universal | 10+ | Syngency talent app surfaces "upcoming Confirmations, Options, Bookouts" |
| **Release** | The claim is dropped; the model is free. Neutral, not a rejection | booker, CD | universal | 5 | "This status means a talent is no longer being considered… It is a common part of the casting process and formally communicates to the talent that they can pursue other opportunities without conflict." — Casting Networks, *Released* |
| **Cancellation** | A **confirmed** booking is killed; money is usually owed | booker, client | universal | 5 | "Cancellation Policy within 24 hours, full fee. Within 48 hours, half fee." — Vision Models LA. "Within 24 hours of the booking call time the full booking fee will be charged… Outside 24 hours… but within 48 hours… half the booking fee" — Tess Management |
| **Book out / bookout** | The **model's** own declaration of unavailability | model → booker | universal | 3 | "Notification to your agent that you're not available for a casting or job… you've 'booked out'." — modelmanagement.com glossary |
| **Chart** | The model's schedule, held by the agency | booker | universal (older usage) | 1 | "Agency-speak for 'your schedule', maintained by them." — theFashionSpot |
| **Charge-back** | Model no-shows a job; client bills the agency, agency bills the model | booker | US | 1 | "When you accept a job and don't do it, the client can charge your agency for it. Your agency then charges you." — theFashionSpot |
| **Exclusive** | Model walks only one show that season, or opens/closes only one | booker, CD | fashion week | 2 | "They're offered, not applied for, and they flow through agency relationships with top casting directors." — Vibrant Casting |

**Important regional/domain split on "hold":** in SAG-AFTRA-governed film/commercial work a hold implies money
("Technically speaking, CDs can't 'hold' actors (per SAG rules)… When the word 'hold' is involved, it usually
means money is owed" — Ramani Leah, CSA, *The Casting Director's Cut*). In fashion, *option* and *hold* are used
interchangeably and generally carry **no** cancellation liability until confirmed — that liability attaches at
confirmation (see the 24/48h tables above). A product must not promise a fashion option is compensable.

| Term | Meaning | Evidence |
|---|---|---|

### 2.4 Terms practitioners would flinch at

| Wrong term | Why it reads wrong | What is said instead |
|---|---|---|
| **Applicant / candidate** | "Candidate" is pure HR and appears in **zero** of the ~25 real notices sampled. "Applicant" is genuinely used by *event organizers* ("All applicants will receive an email notification on March 11, 2026" — Greenville FW; "Every application personally reviewed" — The Bureau) but **never** by agencies about their board or by bookers about a casting | agency side: **submission**. Event side: **applicant** is acceptable; a person past the screen is a **model**, then **selected model**, then **confirmed** |
| **Interview** (for a client casting) | An interview is a conversation about your suitability for employment. A casting is a 90-second physical audition — "A runway casting lasts ninety seconds" (Vibrant Casting) | **casting**, **go-see**, **callback**, **fitting** |
| **Pipeline** | Sales/ATS metaphor. Bookers say *chart*, *board*, *options*, *the pool* | **chart** (schedule), **board** (roster), **options** |
| **Recruiter** | The scam-adjacent word. Agencies use it only in impersonation warnings: "individuals falsely representing themselves as agents, scouts, managers, or model **recruiters** for NEXT Management" | **scout**, **booker**, **agent**, **casting director** |
| **Hire / hiring** | Models are usually independent contractors, not hires. Casting Networks' own docs slip once ("officially hired for your production") but the industry word is different | **book**, **booked**, **confirm** |
| **Job posting / job / gig** | "Gig" is amateur-poster language: "This is a paid modelling gig in the Norfolk County Ontario area" (AllCasting user post). Bookers do say "the job" for a confirmed booking, but never "gig" | **casting**, **booking**, **the job** (only once confirmed), **show** |
| **Offer letter / offer** | There is no offer letter. The instrument is a **deal memo** (NY, now statutory) or a **booking confirmation form** (UK) | **deal memo**, **booking confirmation** |
| **Rejected / rejection** | No legitimate player writes this to a model. They release, or say nothing | **released**, **not selected**, **not right for this season**, or silence |
| **Accepted** | Implies a symmetric agreement; the reality is asymmetric selection | **confirmed**, **booked**, **selected**, **requested by** |
| **Application status: In review → Interviewing → Offer** | Recognisable ATS ladder; wrong at every rung | Unviewed → Viewed → Selected → Under Consideration → On Avail → On Hold → Booked / Released (Casting Networks' actual ladder) |

## 3. Workflow / state model as practitioners describe it

### 3.1 The represented pipeline (System A)

```
CLIENT BRIEF
   │  owner: client / casting director
   ▼
PACKAGE SENT            agency → client. Digital cards: measurements, walk video, recent images.
   │  owner: booker      Reversible, obligates nobody.
   ▼
REQUEST  ──or──  CASTING / GO-SEE  ──or──  DIRECT BOOKING
   │  owner: client. Casting = job-specific; go-see = general; direct booking skips the room entirely.
   ▼
CALLBACK / RECALL       owner: casting director. Narrowing. "It is not unusual to get a callback
   │                    before getting a job or being turned down." (modelmanagement.com)
   ▼
AVAILABILITY CHECK      owner: client asks, booker answers. Non-binding both ways.
   │
   ▼
OPTION (1st / 2nd) ≡ HOLD ≡ (UK) PROVISIONAL BOOKING
   │  owner: client places it, booker records it on the chart, model must keep dates free.
   │  Model may NOT accept clashing work while on 1st option.
   │  Second option = queued behind the first.
   │  UK: auto-expires in 24h unless the booking confirmation form is signed.
   │  Forcing move — BOOK OR RELEASE — owner: booker, triggered when a competing job wants to confirm.
   ├──────────────► RELEASED  (terminal, neutral, no money owed, model is free)
   ▼
CONFIRMED / BOOKED      owner: client (signs booking confirmation) → booker → model.
   │  From here cancellation costs money: <24h = full fee, 24–48h = half fee (US and UK both).
   ▼
FITTING                 owner: designer/stylist. Post-option or post-confirmation. Paid in UK terms.
   ▼
REHEARSAL / CALL TIME / JOB
   │
   ▼
(POST) usage, invoice, voucher; CANCELLATION if killed after confirmation; CHARGE-BACK if the model no-shows.
```

Orthogonal, model-owned: **BOOK OUT** — the model tells the agency they are unavailable for a window. This is
the only state the model writes. Syngency's talent app exposes exactly three things to the model:
"upcoming Confirmations, Options, Bookouts."

### 3.2 The open event call (System B) — the state model a talent-facing product would actually be modelling

```
NOTICE POSTED                    owner: organizer / producer.
  fields observed across notices: market + dates, deadline ("Casting ends September 6, 2026"),
  in-person vs virtual, age gate, height/size gate (or explicit "no height requirement"),
  what to bring/wear, compensation type, agency-optional field, consent checkboxes.
   ▼
APPLICATION / SUBMISSION         owner: model. Free at legitimate events ("Free to apply",
   │                             "There is never a fee to be cast" — The Bureau).
   ▼
INTERNAL REVIEW / VETTING        owner: organizer or its casting partner.
   │   "All casting submissions will be reviewed by Develop Model Management." (Omaha)
   │   "The casting team personally reviews every submission — there's no algorithmic filter." (Bureau)
   ├──► SILENT DROP  ← the overwhelmingly common terminal state. No message is sent.
   ▼
INVITED TO CASTING / MODEL CALL  owner: organizer. Dated, in-person, sometimes ticketed by queue number.
   │   "If you are confirmed, you will be invited to an in-person casting." (Fashion Week Haus)
   │   "you will be notified for the date and time of your casting call." (Fashion Week SD)
   ▼
CASTING ATTENDED                 measurements, headshot, full-length, walk video captured on the day.
   ▼
ROSTER / APPROVED POOL           owner: organizer. Being on it is NOT a booking.
   │   "Unfortunately, not every model that makes the roster will be selected by a designer." (KCFW)
   ▼
SHORTLIST SHARED WITH DESIGNERS  owner: organizer; decision passes to the designer.
   │   Omaha: private gallery link "shared only with current Omaha Fashion Week designers
   │   for the purpose of selecting their models."
   │   Bureau: "the casting team builds shortlists from the approved pool and designers pick their lineups."
   │   FashioNXT: "Designers are in full control of the models they show."
   ▼
REQUESTED / SELECTED BY DESIGNER owner: designer. Communicated by the organizer, not the designer.
   │   "If you are requested by a designer you will hear directly from [organizer staff]." (KCFW)
   ▼
CONFIRMED  → FITTING → REHEARSAL → CALL TIME → SHOW
       KCFW's actual email sequence to a selected model:
       (1) Rehearsal Email with the designer's date and time, ~one month in advance
       (2) Call Time Email after rehearsal weekend, with the designer's official call time
       (3) General Information Email the week of the shows (parking, what to bring)
       Bureau: "Selected models get their show details — call time, fitting, and logistics —
       confirmed directly by the casting team."
   ▼
CARRY-OVER, NOT REJECTION        "a strong application that misses one season is kept in view
                                  for the next." (Bureau)
```

### 3.3 Who owns each transition (the answer to "who acts")

| Transition | Owner | Model's role | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Post notice / brief | organizer or client | none | yes |
| Submit / apply | **model** | the only push the model makes | withdraw only |
| Review, vet, shortlist | organizer / casting director | none, and usually **cannot see it** | yes |
| Share pool with designers | organizer | consent given at application time | — |
| Select / request | **designer or client** | none | yes, until confirmed |
| Availability check | client asks, **booker** answers | answers via booker | yes |
| Option / hold | **client places**, booker records | must keep dates free | yes — release |
| Book or release | **booker** forces it | none | resolves to book or release |
| Confirm | **client** signs; booker relays | accepts the call time | only at a cost |
| Cancel | client (or agency) | none | no — fees apply |
| **Book out** | **model** | the model's only write | yes |
| Release | client / CD | none | terminal, neutral |

### 3.4 What is visible to the model — the most product-relevant finding

Casting Networks, the largest professional casting platform, **deliberately hides most of the ladder from
talent**. Verbatim from its status documentation:

> "*Talent on a Premium membership can view this status." — applied to **Selected** and **Under Consideration** only
>
> "All statuses listed below are not visible to any talent or their representative. These are only for internal
> organizational purposes." — applied to **Pinned, On Avail, On Hold, Backup, Booked, Released**

So the industry's own tooling exposes at most *"we looked at you"* and *"you're still in it"*, and keeps
pinned/avail/hold/booked/released internal, to be communicated by a human (the booker) if at all. A product that
surfaces a raw internal funnel to talent is inventing a transparency the industry does not practise — and the
practitioner objection is concrete: options and pins collapse constantly, and telling a model they are "on hold"
when the CD has three others pinned for the same slot creates false expectation.

## 4. Data conventions in casting and event notices

**Fields observed in real 2026 notices** (Runway 7 NYFW, Couture Fashion Week NY, The Bureau, modelmanagement.com,
AllCasting, Greenville FW, Omaha FW, FashioNXT):

- **Deadline, expressed as a close date**, not "open until filled": "Casting ends September 6, 2026"
  (modelmanagement.com); "Expires on 9/6/2026" (AllCasting).
- **Market + show dates, together**: "New York · Sept 9-13 / Miami · May 29-31 / Los Angeles · Oct 16-18"
  (Runway 7). Season identity matters more than a generic date range: "F/W 2026", "S/S '26".
- **In-person vs online, stated explicitly.** "In-person casting only in NYC; virtual casting is not available."
  (Runway 7). "Unfortunately, we do not offer any kind of virtual casting opportunities for any season."
  (Omaha FW). modelmanagement.com has a literal field: "Online or in person job / collaboration → In person".
- **Age gate, always present, and it is a hard gate.** "Age 18+ (no exceptions)" (Runway 7); "Age: minimum 18"
  (Couture FW); "Applicants must be 18 or older… I confirm that I am 18 years of age or older" as a required
  checkbox (The Bureau); "Models must be at least 12 years old" (Omaha); "15 years of age or older by April 1,
  2026" (Greenville); "Age 18 to 50" as a range field (modelmanagement.com).
- **Height/size gate — present or *explicitly negated*, never omitted.** Hard: "If you are not at least 5'9″,
  please do NOT apply… Height: Females 5'9″ and taller, Males 6′ and taller, in **bare feet**; Dress size
  (females): 0-4; Suit (males): 40-44 regular" (Couture FW); "5'7" tall without shoes" / "5'10" tall without
  shoes" (Greenville). Explicitly negated: "Is there a height requirement? No. There is no universal minimum
  height requirement for this casting call." (Runway 7); "We do not place any requirements on weight or body type
  or height." (Omaha); "There is no single cutoff. Designers cast for their own collections" (The Bureau).
  **Height cutoffs are a fashion-board / couture-runway gate, not an industry-wide fact** — the open-event tier
  routinely disclaims them.
- **Measured barefoot / without shoes** is the convention wherever height is gated (2 of 2 sources that specify).
- **"Walk required" is stated as an instruction, not a skill tag.** "You will be asked to walk. We will ask to
  see your legs, arms and upper back (to see if you can model open back gowns)" (Couture FW). "Bring high heels
  and outfits that show the walk clearly (not loose-fitting or ankle-length dresses)" (FashioNXT). "Women are
  encouraged to wear heels at least 3 inches tall when recording their runway walk" (Omaha). Runway 7 requires
  "nude or black heels at least 4 inches high and platform" for women and teens 14+.
- **Photo spec, always four-ish and always anti-retouching.** "four photos (headshot, full body, 3/4 shot, comp
  card), and a walk video" (The Bureau); "All photos must be unretouched and containing no filter… please do not
  submit any photos wearing makeup" (DNA Models); "Please no nude/filter photos. Ideal image format is .jpg,
  under 5 megs" (LOOK); "no makeup, no baggy clothing, and no poses" (ONE Management).
  Recency is stated as a value: *"Send the phone photo from this month, not the retouched editorial from two
  years ago. We're casting who shows up to fittings."* (The Bureau).
- **Agency field is optional but present.** "Agency Name (Optional)" (Runway 7); "there's a field for your
  agency so bookings get coordinated properly" (The Bureau) — i.e. representation is a *routing* fact, not an
  eligibility fact, in open events.
- **Compensation type is a first-class field.** modelmanagement.com renders it as `Payment: Collaborative shoot
  or event / Collaboration` plus `Additional payment details: TFP`. AllCasting shows `$200 CAD/day`, `$40/hour`,
  or narrative ("This is a volunteer opportunity"). Backstage/StarNow accept paid and unpaid but require honesty
  about the rate (secondary summary of their posting conditions; page 403'd to direct fetch).
- **What organizers do NOT publish:** the model's stats on a public page, other applicants, the roster before it
  is final, or the designer's identity before selection.

## 5. Compensation and the legal/ethical posture on unpaid event work

**The unpaid tiers, in organizers' own words:**

- Greenville FW: "Models will participate in a training workshop led by industry professionals. They will also
  be featured in the 2026 Greenville Fashion Week program book and will receive access to the event's online
  photo gallery for their portfolio. **No additional compensation will be provided.**"
- Runway 7 NYFW: benefits are "Full hair and makeup provided at the venue", "Exclusive Model Activations &
  Gifting", after-party access, and "Exposure to 120+ Designers". Plus: "**Models may not request or demand
  compensation from designers.** Any financial agreements between models and designers are independent of
  Runway 7."
- FashioNXT: the split is explicit and is a *representation* split, not a skill split —
  Agency Models: "$60 per Calendar Show or Shoot Date (Up to $180 for three show dates)".
  Unaffiliated Models: enrolled in "UpNXT-Models: A New Face Development Program", benefits only — runway
  mentorship, "Representation opportunities by leading agencies (please inquire)", social posting, "Professional
  images: one for each of their runway looks". Also: "Professional/agency models will be given priority."
- The Bureau (the most carefully worded of the sample): "Compensation is handled per booking — it varies by
  designer, market, and show, and is discussed individually once you're selected. Applying is free, and there is
  never a fee to be cast."
- modelmanagement.com's own taxonomy makes unpaid a labelled category: "Collaborative shoot or event",
  "Collaboration", "TFP".

**Rights grabs travel with unpaid work.** Runway 7's model terms: participation grants the right to use the
model's "image, likeness, photos, and video footage taken before, during, and after the show… across all
platforms and media formats, **in perpetuity and worldwide, with no further compensation owed to the model**."
Compare the represented side, where usage is the *product*: UK agency terms grant "the right… to use one image
via a single published medium for one year… in the United Kingdom only" and charge separately for anything more
(Tess Management §2, ILM §2). This is the single sharpest asymmetry between System A and System B.

**US regulation — New York Fashion Workers Act, effective 19 June 2025** (NY DOL, primary):
- Model management companies must register; registration valid two years, renewal ≥90 days before expiry.
- **Deal memo**: "Provide models with a written or digital copy of their deal memo before work begins. This deal
  memo must state the model's total compensation and payment term" — and it must be provided "in the language
  you request". The model must agree to the compensation **before work begins**.
- Commission capped at 20%; **no signing fees or deposits**; written approval required before deducting
  job-related costs; representation agreements capped at three years; no automatic renewal without written
  approval; power of attorney must be optional and cannot cover digital replicas.
- **Digital replica**: "Obtain clear written approval from the model to create or use a model's digital replica.
  This approval must be separate from the representation agreement." Clients owe the same duty.
- **Client duties**: 1.5× the contracted hourly rate beyond eight hours in a 24-hour period; "at least one
  30-minute meal break during any work that lasts over eight hours"; and "Allow models to bring their agent,
  manager, chaperone, or other representative."
- Management must "Inform models about any financial relationship that might exist between the model management
  company and the client."

**Model Alliance (primary, advocacy):** models are "considered employees under some laws and independent
contractors under others" — in New York, employees for Unemployment Insurance and Workers' Compensation;
independent contractors under the FLSA. Payment in kind is the recognised abuse: models are "paid in 'trade'
instead of money" (secondary press summarising Model Alliance research). Unionisation is legally unclear
precisely because of contractor classification.

**UK/EU:** Equity organises fashion models and has an agreement with the British Fashion Council covering London
Fashion Week — pay in line with agreed rates, hours of work, breaks, **pay for fittings**, food and drink
backstage, changing-area privacy, a 16+ minimum age, and advance notice of any nudity or semi-nudity requirement
(secondary — press/union summaries; I could not retrieve the agreement text itself). This is corroborated
structurally by UK agency booking terms, which price fittings as billable time at half the hourly rate with a
minimum charge. **Conclusion: in the UK represented market, an unpaid fitting is anomalous; in the US open-event
market, unpaid fittings and rehearsals are the norm.** Equity also campaigns to abolish audition fees
(#AbolishAuditionFees) and publishes a "Vetting casting notices" guide — i.e. the union's stated position is
that a notice is something a performer should be able to verify before attending.

## 6. Trust and legitimacy signals

**What legitimate players say, near-verbatim and repeatedly (5+ independent sources):**

- "You should never pay to attend a casting. You should never share photos in the nude or in lingerie." — Ford Models
- "Please note that SMG does not request nude/lingerie photos, and we will never ask you to send us money." — SMG
- "There is never a fee to be cast." — The Bureau
- "We will only ever contact you back through a verified @premiermodelmanagement.com domain." — Premier Model Management
- "All FORD Models representatives correspond via email domains that end with fordmodels.com." — Ford
- "there have been reports of individuals falsely representing themselves as agents, scouts, managers, or model
  recruiters" — NEXT Management (note: **"recruiter" appears only inside a scam warning**)
- "These impersonators are targeting models through social media or unsolicited messages, requesting money,
  inappropriate images, or in-person meetings under false pretenses—such as scouting, portfolio development, or
  model training." — ONE Management
- Data minimisation: "YOUR APPLICATION DATA WILL BE KEPT HERE FOR NO LONGER THAN 30 WORKING DAYS." — Neal Hamil Agency
- Named consent for downstream sharing: "I consent to The Bureau storing and processing my application details
  and photos for model casting purposes, **and sharing my comp card with designers casting for Bureau fashion
  weeks**. I can request deletion at any time" — The Bureau

**A live counter-example worth naming, because it shows where the line is.** Runway 7's NYFW casting page
carries a "SAFE CASTING GUARANTEE — Runway 7 never requests explicit images or payments from models", and, on
the same form, a required checkbox: "I understand the **Runway 7 Model Kit is required** to participate in the
casting process", with the explanation that the kit "gives you access to the Runway7 App, now a core part of our
casting process. If a designer selects you, that's where you'll receive the request, your fitting time, and any
important updates." The page also sells priority: "Unlock the **Skip the Line** benefit by spending a total of
$100 or more on Runway7 merchandise… Enter your order number(s) — up to 3 — to fast-track your registration",
while stating "A merchandise purchase is optional and is not required to apply." Whatever one concludes about
this specific operator, **pay-to-be-seen and paid queue priority are exactly the patterns every established
agency's own warning copy tells models to treat as a red flag**, and a product that implements "pay to be seen
first" is implementing the scam pattern regardless of intent.

**Terms scam operations use that legitimate players avoid:** guaranteed earnings ("No ethical US agency
guarantees a new model anything" — theFashionSpot), mandatory paid "portfolio development" / "model training",
recruitment framed as a career-services purchase, and any request for payment as a condition of being seen.

**Counts:** 13 of 14 sources contact **only** the selected. **1 of 14** (Greenville FW) commits to notifying all
applicants — and it does so with a *date*, not a status page. **"No response = no" is stated explicitly** in 5 of
14 (Omaha, VNY, ONE, LOOK, The Agency Arizona), and Omaha goes further and forbids follow-up.

**Re-application intervals — evidence is thin and I will not overstate it.** I found **no primary agency or
organizer page** stating a numeric resubmission interval. What primary sources say is *seasonal*, not numeric:
- "a strong application that misses one season is kept in view for the next" — The Bureau
- "Stay tuned to Omaha Fashion Week social media and website for information on our next model call. **Each
  season brings a new set of designers looking for a different aesthetic.**" — Omaha FW
- "Please do not submit multiple applications." — Couture Fashion Week (an anti-spam rule, not an interval)
The commonly repeated "wait 6 months" / "4–6 months" / "6–12 months with materially updated digitals" figures
come only from **coaching/secondary blogs** (mymodelreality, Photography Shark, photosbyanette). Treat as
folklore-with-consensus, not as an industry rule. The defensible primary-source statement is: **the natural
re-entry unit is the season, and the thing that justifies re-entry is new digitals or a changed look.**

## 8. Open questions and contested points

1. **Does an option create any obligation on the client in fashion?** *Contested.* theFashionSpot says the model
   "is obligated to be available… and may not accept any other assignment" — obligation flows only one way.
   Casting Networks says "If a formally 'held' performer is ultimately not used, a cancellation fee may be due
   depending on contractual agreements", and CSA's Ramani Leah says "When the word 'hold' is involved, it usually
   means money is owed." UK agency terms resolve it cleanly the other way: fees attach at the **signed booking
   confirmation**, and an unconfirmed provisional booking simply lapses in 24 hours with nothing owed. Safest
   reading for a product: **an option obligates the model and not the client, unless a contract says otherwise.**

2. **Is "option" or "hold" the head term?** theFashionSpot treats them as synonyms ("OPTION: Also referred to as
   a 'hold'"). Casting Networks makes them *different rungs* (On Avail < Pinned < On Hold). Fashion practice
   favours **option** (with 1st/2nd numbering); screen/commercial practice favours **avail/pin/hold**. A fashion
   product should lead with *option* and treat *hold* as its synonym, not as a separate state.

3. **Is "applicant" acceptable?** *Contested and context-dependent.* Event organizers use it constantly and
   naturally ("All applicants will receive an email notification" — Greenville; "Every application personally
   reviewed" — The Bureau). Agencies never use it about talent — they say **submission**. So: acceptable in an
   event-call surface, wrong in an agency-roster surface.

4. **Is "audition" acceptable for modelling?** Partly. theFashionSpot: "normally used for acting jobs, but also
   can be used for modeling." FashioNXT literally brands its event "Model Auditions". But bookers say *casting*.
   Regional/lower-tier events say *audition*; the represented market does not.

5. **How real is designer pick-list sharing?** *Settled, with strong evidence.* Four organizers describe it in
   their own copy (Omaha's private designer-only gallery link, FashioNXT's "Runway Pre-Selection for Designers",
   Greenville's "Models are presented to… Designers… for initial selection", The Bureau's shortlists), and one
   product requires explicit consent for it ("sharing my comp card with designers casting for Bureau fashion
   weeks" — The Bureau). **Consent language for onward sharing is the norm-setting example to follow.**

6. **Should a talent-facing product show internal casting statuses?** *Contested, with the industry's own tool
   siding against.* Casting Networks hides Pinned/Avail/Hold/Backup/Booked/Released from talent entirely, and
   gates even Selected/Under Consideration behind a paid tier. Meanwhile every event organizer's copy trains
   models to expect **nothing at all**. Any product that shows more than "received / under review / invited /
   confirmed" is ahead of industry practice — which may be a genuine improvement, but it is a deliberate
   departure, not a reflection of how casting works, and it should never invent a *rejection* event that real
   organizers do not send.

7. **Thin evidence, flagged:** exact numeric resubmission intervals (§7); the text of the Equity/BFC London
   Fashion Week agreement (only press summaries retrieved); Backstage's own posting conditions (Cloudflare 403
   on both `backstage.com/deadlines-disclaimers/` and the StarNow mirror — used only via search-result summary);
   Models.com's casting/booking-board conventions (models.com/work returned no substantive content); the AMA
   (UK Association of Model Agents) site returned an empty body.

## 9. Synthesis for a product audit

**The real end-to-end state model, minimal and defensible:**

`Notice posted → Submitted → Reviewed → (silent drop) | Invited to casting → Casting attended → In the pool/roster
→ Shortlisted to a designer/client → Requested → On option (1st/2nd) → Confirmed → Fitting → Rehearsal/call time
→ Show/job` — with **Released** available as a neutral exit from any state after *Requested*, and **Book out**
as the model's own overlay on the whole calendar.

**Confirmation language that real organizers actually use:** "you will hear directly from [named person]";
"Selected models will be notified through email by [date]"; "you will be notified for the date and time of your
casting call"; "Selected models get their show details — call time, fitting, and logistics — confirmed directly
by the casting team"; "If you are confirmed, you will be invited to an in-person casting". Note the pattern:
**a named human, a channel, a date, and the next concrete artifact (call time / fitting / rehearsal email).**

**Decline language that real organizers actually use:** almost none. Where it exists it is *"not selected"* and
never *"rejected"*: "If you were not selected, you will not receive any notification"; "not every model that
makes the roster will be selected by a designer"; "a strong application that misses one season is kept in view
for the next". On the represented side the word is **released**, and it is framed as freeing the model, not
failing them.

**What a talent legitimately expects after applying:** confirmation of receipt at most; review with no reply;
contact only on interest; a named person and a date if selected; and no status page. A product that promises
more than this is promising something the industry does not currently deliver — which is a legitimate product
choice, but it must not be described to agencies as "how casting works".

**Words that would read as HR software or gig-economy software rather than casting** (ranked by how badly they
break the frame): `candidate`, `recruiter`, `hire/hiring`, `offer letter`, `pipeline`, `interview` (for a client
casting), `job posting`, `gig`, `rejected/rejection`, `applicant` (agency contexts only), `accepted`,
`application status: in review → interviewing → offer`, `screening`, `talent acquisition`, `req`.

**Words that carry the frame correctly:** `submission`, `package`, `board`, `chart`, `open call`, `model call`,
`casting`, `go-see`, `callback`, `fitting`, `request`, `direct booking`, `availability`, `option (1st/2nd)`,
`hold`, `book or release`, `confirm/confirmed`, `booked`, `released`, `book out`, `cancellation`, `call time`,
`roster`, `shortlist`, `lineup`, `new face`, `deal memo`, `comp card / digitals / polaroids`, `season`.
