# R2: Adjacent Platforms (Creator, Talent and Creative-Industry)

Lane: platforms next to a "verified creative work record" (issued, mutual and claimed credits), with a Fashion Week Brooklyn launch.
Researched 2026-10-12. Tags: **[FACT](url)** means sourced. **[INFERENCE]** means my reasoning from the facts. **[NOT FOUND]** means I searched and found no reliable number.

Source caveat: several pricing figures come from third-party guides because vendor pages blocked fetches (models.com, Production Paradise and FMD all returned 403/404). Where sources conflict, both figures are given.

## 1. Fashion and production directories (pay-to-list)

### Models.com (agency/creative side)
- What it is: the de facto industry database of fashion work, with model and creative pages, agency listings and editorial rankings.
- [FACT](https://help.models.com/article/13/how-does-the-database-work) Work is uploaded by creative agencies and individuals, who tag the artists and models in the credits. **Profile pages for artists and clients are made by models.com staff** "based on their career accomplishments, longevity and influence."
- [FACT](https://help.models.com/article/61/adding-talent-credits-to-a-new-uploaded-work) Only people who already have a Models.com page can be credited on an upload. A crew member without a page can't be tagged. Uploader accounts must be approved, and identity is checked by linking the Instagram or Twitter on the person's page.
- [FACT](https://help.models.com/article/49/how-do-i-add-my-agencys-credit-to-a-model-or-talents-page-) Only "Agency admins" of listed agencies can add or remove representation on a model's page.
- [FACT](https://models.com/about/listings/modeling/) Agency listings are tiered (Basic, Basic Plus, Standard, Premium, Premium Plus). They include Pro seats: 2 on Basic, up to 5 on Basic Plus, unlimited on higher tiers, each seat valued at $200/yr. Prices are by inquiry only.
- [FACT](https://models.com/about/services/) Individual Pro costs $20/month. Pro Team costs $15/user/month for 5+ users.
- [FACT](https://help.models.com/article/46/what-are-the-benefits-of-having-an-agency-listing-on-models.com) Models.com says listing tier "does not reflect how they are viewed in the industry," and a listing does not guarantee editorial inclusion or rankings.
- [FACT](https://fashionweekdaily.com/the-wizards-of-oz) The database came with a 2008 relaunch and is invite-only.
- What worked [INFERENCE]: Credibility comes from editorial gatekeeping (staff decide who gets a page) plus agency-uploaded credits. Agencies pay because listings and Pro seats are where clients, casting directors and scouts look. **Industry members pay for search seats (Pro), and agencies pay for presence. Models do not pay.** This is close to the model the brief intends to copy.
- What failed or its limits [INFERENCE]: Exclusion is built in. Assistants, emerging HMUAs, small-fashion-week casts and anyone without a page are invisible. That is the gap an "issued credits" product would fill.
- **Lesson:** The paid-seats business already exists in fashion, and it's attached to the scarce, curated layer. A long-tail graph (FWB, school shows) will not command Models.com-level seat prices. Value comes from the top of the graph, not its size. Pholio's opening is the people Models.com structurally won't credit. That is also a buyer-willingness problem, because those are the people seat-buyers search for least.

### Fashion Model Directory (FMD)
- [FACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_Model_Directory) Started in 1998, online in 2000, taken over by Fashion One Group in 2002. It lists ads, covers, editorials and shows per model.
- [FACT](https://fashionmodeldirectory.com/info) Since 2017 it has been a non-profit data project run by volunteers and supported by sponsors. It does not guarantee accuracy.
- [FACT](https://fashionmodeldirectory.com/agencies/women-direct-new-york) Many agency pages carry an "UNVERIFIED" label and letter ratings (for example "AA (Recommended)").
- **Lesson:** A credits database with no paying side decays into volunteer upkeep. It also shows that "verified" vs "unverified" labelling is an old idea. Labels alone don't create value unless someone pays to search or pays to be confirmed.

### Le Book / The Book
- [FACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Book) A long-running reference directory for fashion, photography and advertising creatives, best known through its CONNECTIONS trade shows.
- [FACT](https://apanational.org/events/apa-sf-lebook-connections-2025/) Exhibitor tables at CONNECTIONS SF 2025 had a standard price of $7,500 ($4,500 APA member, $3,750 emerging). The package bundled two award entries and a one-year LeBook.com membership.
- [FACT](https://fashionunited.uk/news/fashion/le-book-presents-connections-the-custom-made-tradeshow-for-the-creative-community/2015101918027) CONNECTIONS is an invitation-only face-time event with "pre-qualified creative decision makers" (art buyers, CDs, photo editors).
- [NOT FOUND] Current lebook.com directory listing prices.
- **Lesson:** Le Book's real revenue unit is **access to buyers in a room**, with the directory bundled in. The industry pays for introductions to decision-makers, not for data.

### Workbook
- [FACT](https://aphotoeditor.com/2019/11/06/photo-directories-sourcebook-review/) In 2019, Workbook cost $2,000 (web) to $7,500 (Pro tier with a two-page spread in the print books).
- [FACT](https://www.diyphotography.net/?p=169325) The basic online directory listing (41,000+ members) is free. Portfolio and marketing services are paid.
- [FACT](https://www.diyphotography.net/?p=169325) Workbook has launched WORK NOW!, with plans from $7/month. (Single secondary source.)
- **Lesson:** Prices for print-era directories are falling toward SaaS levels. The willingness to pay that remains is for **promotion to buyers**, not for being listed.

### Production Paradise
- [FACT](https://www.productionparadise.com/faq.html) There are no free listings. Basic membership is a one-year listing, a 10-image gallery and a bio. Higher tiers add promotion in its Spotlight and Showcase digital magazines. (From search snippet; page now 404.)
- [FACT](https://productionparadise.com/subscription/become-a-member.html) Applications are reviewed, listed professionals "should have a proven track record with prominent brands," and pricing goes through a sales consultant.
- **Lesson:** Vetting by track record is the credibility mechanism, and creatives pay. Pholio's free-for-creatives stance cuts against this, deliberately.

### Wonderland
- [NOT FOUND] A "Wonderland" directory product. Search only returned Wonderland Magazine editorials credited on models.com ([example](https://models.com/work/wonderland-magazine-making-a-splash)). Wonderland functions as a credit **source** (an indie publication whose credits matter), not a directory. [INFERENCE] It belongs on the "issuer" list (publications), not the competitor list.

### Vogue Runway (credit publication, also pay-to-list)
- [FACT](https://apple.co/2H6FIDG) The archive holds 20,000+ shows and 1M+ images back to 1988.
- [FACT](https://www.glossy.co/?p=74914) In fall 2019 the team covered 391 collections over 28 days in four cities.
- [FACT](https://www.businessoffashion.com/articles/media/vogue-runway-is-charging-some-brands-to-post-their-collection-images) Brands can pay about $20,000/year to have collection images uploaded twice a year, subject to editor approval. Paid listings are not marked as paid.
- **Lesson:** Even the most prestigious runway database sells placement to designers. **Designers pay for visibility**, which matters for FWB because FWB designers already pay to show.

## 2. Casting and talent platforms

### Spotlight (UK)
- [FACT](https://spotlight.com/memberships/performers/) UK performers pay £171.50 + VAT/yr (£205.80 inc VAT), or £15.60 + VAT/month. Discounts exist for graduates, young performers and disabled performers.
- [FACT](https://support.spotlight.com/en/articles/11812289) Joining requires one Equity-contract featured role, **or two professional paid performance credits**, or qualifying training, or a recommendation from a Spotlight-registered agent or a Casting Directors' Guild/PMA member. Extras, unpaid work and training productions don't count, and contracts may be requested as evidence.
- [FACT](https://www.backstage.com/uk/magazine/article/spotlight-accused-of-discriminatory-joining-rules-more-uk-news-69600) The older criteria required 4+ featured credits or accredited training. They were loosened after "discriminatory" criticism.
- [FACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotlight_(company)) Founded in 1927 (first directory: "236 performers and 2 dogs"), online since 1997, last print edition 2017. Used by production companies, broadcasters, ad agencies and casting directors.
- [FACT](https://www.castingnetworks.com/?p=100593) Talent Systems (owner of Casting Networks) agreed to acquire Spotlight in January 2021. [FACT](https://flashesandflames.com/?p=134401) A 2019 estimate put the base at about 60k actors paying about £150/yr.
- [FACT](https://www.screendaily.com/news/uk-actors-union-equity-loses-legal-case-against-casting-directory-spotlight/5208502.article) Equity sued over the fees. On 3 Sep 2025 the High Court found Spotlight is "a marketing tool," not an employment agency, and doubted the fees exceeded a reasonable estimate of costs. [FACT](https://www.artsprofessional.co.uk/news/newsreel/equity-wins-right-to-fight-spotlight-ruling-at-court-of-appeal) Equity won permission to appeal in November 2025. [NOT FOUND] An appeal outcome.
- [FACT](https://dev.morningstaronline.co.uk/article/performers-launch-class-action-lawsuit-against-rip-casting-directory-spotlight) Fees rose about 30% after the Talent Systems takeover.
- What worked [INFERENCE]: Spotlight is the strongest precedent for a **verified directory**. Entry is gated by **credits that can be evidenced**, buyers (casting) are the reason talent pays, and nearly 100 years of being the default made it compulsory.
- What failed [INFERENCE]: Monopoly pricing produced union litigation and reputational damage. Charging the supply side for access to buyers is profitable and resented.
- **Lesson:** A credit-gated directory becomes standard when **the buyers use it as their only search tool**. Spotlight's gate is "two paid professional credits," which is close to an issued-credit threshold. Pholio could make that threshold free and evidenced, but it only works if buyers search there.

### Casting Networks / Talent Systems
- [FACT](https://www.castingnetworks.com/introducing-casting-networks-essential/) The talent Essential tier is $9.99/month.
- [FACT](https://www.castingnetworks.com/?p=100593) Talent Systems also owns Cast It Systems and Casting Frontier. [FACT](https://www.privsource.com/acquisitions/deal/MySjkR) It acquired eTribez and Staff Me Up. [FACT](https://www.castingnetworks.com/?p=100593) RedBird Capital took a majority stake in July 2022.
- [FACT](https://app.spotlight.com/news-and-advice/news/csa-and-talent-systems-announce-partnership/) It has a multi-year partnership with the Casting Society (CSA).
- **Lesson:** Roll-up consolidation plus a guild partnership is how casting tools lock in the buyer side. For FWB, the parallel is an issuer partnership (BK Style Foundation) as the legitimacy anchor.

### Breakdown Services / Actors Access
- [FACT](https://breakdownservices.com/index.cfm/main/faq) Actor profiles are free. Talent representatives must be licensed and pay to receive breakdowns. Casting releases breakdowns to actors at no charge (a small fee applies to non-union projects).
- [FACT](https://sidehusl.com/actors-access/) Premium actor membership costs about $68/yr (secondary source). An older model charged $2 per submission ([actingplan](https://actingplan.com/?p=507)).
- **Lesson:** The paying party is the **intermediary** (agents/managers), not the casting director, and casting directors stay free because they are the scarce attention. [INFERENCE] This argues against charging casting directors early, which would mean Pholio's "paid search seats" are a late-stage revenue line.

### Backstage
- [FACT](https://flashesandflames.com/2021/02/25/backstage-expands-ex-us/) Backstage claimed 250k paid subscriptions in 2021, with estimated US revenue of $15–20M (trade estimate, not confirmed).
- [FACT](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210310005623/en/Audax-Private-Debt-Provides-Financing-to-Support-Backstages-Acquisitions-of-StarNow-and-The-Mandy-Network) It acquired StarNow and The Mandy Network (2021) with TA Associates investment, and later FilmFreeway, Coverfly and Voice123 ([privsource](https://www.privsource.com/acquisitions/deal/D8SWn6)).
- [FACT](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/371281/cast-crew-acquires-backstage-holdings.html) Cast & Crew (a payroll company) acquired Backstage Holdings in 2022.
- **Lesson:** The talent-pays model works at scale for open casting calls (access to gigs). [INFERENCE] It's notable that a payroll company bought Backstage, because the money layer consolidates the talent layer. Pholio has ruled out money, so its moat has to be data or identity.

### Swipecast
- [FACT](https://www.protein.xyz/booking-models-has-never-been-easier/) A 2015 New York "Uber for models" marketplace. It takes a 10% commission, and models receive 90%, paid in 1–2 days.
- [FACT](https://springwise.com/uncategorized/app-modeling-industry-bypasses-agencies) It positioned against agencies' roughly 40% cut. [FACT](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/swipecast) It claimed clients including Nike, Estée Lauder, Tommy Hilfiger, Vogue and W.
- [FACT](https://builtinnyc.com/company/swipecast) It is invite-only, with about 11 employees. [NOT FOUND] Any 2024–2026 activity.
- **Lesson:** "Bypass the agency" marketplaces stay small. Agencies hold supply and relationships, and models risk their agency contracts by booking around them. [INFERENCE] Pholio should help agencies rather than route around them, and it has also ruled out the transaction fee these marketplaces depend on.

### Zodel
- [FACT](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.zodel) A US model-booking app (LV, LA, NY, Miami, Chicago) with escrow, approved accounts and ID checks. It advertises fees "as low as 5%."
- [FACT](https://www.tycoonstory.com/where-do-brands-actually-hire-models-7-platforms-and-agencies-compared/) It claims 800+ verified models and 1,000+ bookings (self-reported, from a promotional source).
- **Lesson:** At small scale, "verified" means ID verification, not work verification. Nobody in this tier verifies work history. That gap is real, but nobody has shown buyers pay to have it filled.

### Snapcast
- [FACT](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1497685246) A model scouting app (Bookt LLC) where premium subscribers can be messaged by scouts and agents. It is rated 1.0/5 on 5 ratings and was last updated in March 2022.
- **Lesson:** Model-pays "get seen by agencies" apps attract scam suspicion and low trust. [FACT](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1497685246) Its weak footprint fits the brief's note on scams. Pholio must never look like pay-to-be-scouted.

## 3. Creator platforms

### Linktree
- [FACT](https://u2l.ai/blog/linktree-pricing) Tiers run Free / $8 / $15 / $35 per month. Pro went from $9 to $15 in November 2025. Seller fees are 12% (Free) and 9% (Starter/Pro). It has 50–70M users (sources differ).
- [FACT](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/linktree-review-2026-is-it-worth-it) $61.6M annual revenue and a $1.3B valuation (single secondary source, unverified).
- [FACT](https://www.tubefilter.com/2023/12/14/linktree-link-in-bio-acquires-koji/) It acquired Bento (June 2023) and Koji (December 2023) and shut both down.
- **Lesson:** Presentation for creatives is a huge free funnel with low conversion and a tier-plus-commerce-fee model. The "modest Pro tier for presentation" competes directly with Linktree, Beacons, Squarespace and Cargo. [INFERENCE] It won't fund the company, and link-in-bio is consolidating through acquisitions.

### Beacons (media kits)
- [FACT](https://help.beacons.ai/en/articles/4695681) The free plan includes a media kit. Paid tiers are $10, $30 and $90/month, with a 9% fee on Free/Creator.
- [FACT](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2156243) It raised a $6M seed led by a16z in May 2021.
- **Lesson:** Media kits are **self-asserted credentials** (stats synced from platform APIs, brand logos self-claimed). [INFERENCE] The "past brand partners" logo wall on a media kit is exactly a CLAIMED credit. Brands accept it because audience data is API-synced, but the logos aren't confirmed.

### Passionfroot
- [FACT](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/21/passionfroot-is-building-a-platform-for-brand-and-creator-colloboration-with-a-focus-on-b2b-sector) Creator storefronts for B2B sponsorships. It took about 5% on bookings (2024).
- [FACT](https://www.contentgrip.com/passionfroot-creator-marketing/) It raised a $15M Series A (Insight Partners, 2026), with more than $21M total, and is expanding to the US. The CEO is relocating to New York.
- **Lesson:** Money flows to whoever handles discovery, contracting and payment. Pholio excludes the last two, so its revenue has to come from discovery alone.

### Collabstr
- [FACT](https://collabstr.com/pricing) Brands pay a 10% hiring fee on free (5% on Premium). Creators pay 15% per completed order ([faq](https://collabstr.com/faq?hl=en-GB)). Pricing page claims 870k–930k+ creators.
- **Lesson:** A two-sided take rate of about 25% shows brands **will pay for transactable discovery**. Pholio's no-money rule closes off the most proven creator-economy revenue model.

### TikTok One (formerly Creator Marketplace)
- [FACT](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/how-creators-can-sign-up-for-tiktok-one) Creators must be 18+, in a launched region, and compliant with branded-content policy. Third-party guides cite a 10k-follower threshold ([Later](https://later.com/blog/tiktok-creator-marketplace/)).
- [INFERENCE] TikTok vouches for creators with first-party performance data. The platform itself is the issuer.

### YouTube BrandConnect / Creator Partnerships
- [FACT](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9385307) Brand inquiries arrive in YouTube Studio's Earn tab. Creators set suggested rates for long-form and Shorts.
- [FACT](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/marketing-strategies/video/kimberly-clark-youtube-brandconnect-case-study) It uses first-party Google data to match creators and brands.
- [FACT](https://ppc.land/youtube-creator-partnerships-replaces-brandconnect-in-7-markets/) It is being rebranded as Creator Partnerships in 7 markets, with Open Calls expanding beyond the US in 2026.
- **Lesson for both:** Platforms already issue the most trusted creator credentials (audience, performance, completed brand deals), and they keep them inside the platform. [INFERENCE] Pholio can't compete on audience truth. Its niche is **offline, multi-person production credits** (runway, shoots) that platforms don't model.

### Instagram collab posts and partnership ads
- [FACT](https://www.inro.social/blog/instagram-collaboration-post-how-to-collab-add-after-posting-more) Collab posts allow up to 5 invited collaborators (6 accounts total). Each invitee must **accept**, and the post then appears on every grid with pooled engagement. Feed and Reels only.
- [FACT](https://epidemicsound.com/blog/instagram-collab-posts) Paid partnerships need the separate paid-partnership label. That label lets the brand see insights and run the post as an ad.
- [FACT](https://www.netinfluencer.com/meta-partnership-ads-outperform-licensed-ugc-across-130m-usd-in-ad-spend-report-finds/) Zuckerberg said on the Q1 2026 call that the Partnership Ads revenue run rate more than doubled YoY to $10B (as reported by Netinfluencer/Agentio).
- **Lesson (critical):** **An Instagram collab post is already a MUTUAL credit.** It is invite-and-accept, capped at 6 people, and lives on a platform where the work is seen. [INFERENCE] This is both the best drafting signal (collab acceptance is strong evidence of a mutual credit) and the biggest threat. Meta could add roles ("styled by," "HMU by") to collabs. The 6-person cap and missing roles are Pholio's opening, because a runway show involves 30–80 people.

### HypeAuditor and Modash (audit tools)
- [FACT](https://modash.io/modash-vs-hypeauditor) Modash claims 250M+ indexed profiles, from $99–199/month.
- [FACT](https://blog.hypeauditor.com/hypeauditor-fake-followers-detection/) HypeAuditor's 1–100 Audience Quality Score uses an ML model trained on 53+ behavioral patterns and claims 95.5% fake-follower accuracy (vendor claim). Pricing starts around $299/month ([clickanalytic](https://www.clickanalytic.com/compare/hypeauditor-alternatives/)).
- **Lesson:** **Brands do pay $100–400/month per seat to verify creators**, but what they verify is **audience authenticity**, not work history. This is the closest proof that "paid search over a verified graph" sells. The verified attribute that sells is reach, which Pholio has chosen not to score.

- **Cosmos:** [FACT](https://www.cosmos.so/blog/the-future-of-cosmos) "Millions of users," 10M+ images saved per month, #1 in Design in 28 countries, Apple's 25 Apps of 2025. **It uses web research to auto-caption every image with creator, publication and date** ("Creators deserve attribution"). [FACT](https://pulse2.com/cosmos-15-million-series-a-closed-as-it-expands-social-discovery-and-attribution-features) It raised a $15M Series A (Shine, Matrix; January 2026), $21M total, for a social graph plus "restoring credit to images." [FACT](https://www.cosmos.so/blog/the-future-of-cosmos) No monetization disclosed.
- **Vero:** [FACT](https://www.inverse.com/article/41772-vero-free-for-life-offer-extraordinary-demand) It promised subscriptions after 1M users (2018), then extended "free for life" indefinitely. [FACT](https://mwm.ai/apps/vero/971055041) The App Store still offers "Founding Member" free for life. [INFERENCE] A creative-community app that never turned on its revenue model.
- **Lessons:** (1) Portfolio hosting is a commodity at $5–15/month, and the Pro presentation tier sits in this crowded band. (2) **Cosmos is the closest well-funded threat**, because it already does automated attribution of image to creator to publication with fresh capital aimed at "credit." It credits the photographer of an image, not the full team, and has no issuer confirmation. (3) Are.na and Vero show that taste-driven creative communities get loved but under-monetize.

## 5. Fashion-week credit culture

- [FACT](https://help.models.com/article/61/adding-talent-credits-to-a-new-uploaded-work) For major shows, team credits are published mainly on **models.com**, uploaded by agencies or approved users and limited to people with pages. Typical credits are designer, stylist/fashion editor, hair, makeup, casting director, sometimes producer and manicurist ([example](https://models.com/work/viktor--rolf-viktor--rolf-fw-12-show), [Miu Miu FW19](https://models.com/Work/miu-miu-miu-miu-fw-19-show)).
- [FACT](https://models.com/work/shayneoliver-shayne-oliver-debut-show) Some models.com show pages note "source: vogue.com." Vogue Runway imagery is the visual record, and models.com is where the team credits sit.
- [FACT](https://fashionunited.uk/news/fashion/backstage-make-up-at-london-fashion-week/2017092025948) The backstage workforce includes junior makeup artists, hairdressers, dressers, producers and technicians beyond the named leads. [INFERENCE] Published credits typically name only the key artist ("makeup by Pat McGrath"), and the assisting team is uncredited.
- [FACT](https://cfda.com/news/live-now-the-official-new-york-fashion-week-schedule) The CFDA publishes the official NYFW schedule (via RUNWAY360), which lists shows, not team credits.
- [NOT FOUND] Any evidence that smaller fashion weeks (including FWB) publish full team credits. Searches on FWB returned press coverage naming the founder (Rick Davy) and designers ([Brooklyn Eagle](https://brooklyneagle.com/articles/2019/10/09/the-man-behind-brooklyns-own-fashion-week/)) but no model or crew credit lists. [INFERENCE] Below the major-city tier, credits live only in scattered Instagram tags. That is the gap an "ISSUED" credit fills, and it is a gap only if participants care. Validate it with FWB participants and don't assume it.
- **Lesson:** Credits in fashion are a **status good controlled by gatekeepers** (models.com staff, Vogue editors). An issuer like FWB confers less status than Vogue. The credit's value to a stylist or model depends on the issuer's prestige. [INFERENCE] For the long tail, the value may be **proof of being a working professional** (anti-scam, anti-AI-catfish), more than prestige.

## Cross-cutting lessons for issued, mutual and claimed credits

1. **Spotlight proves credit-gated directories can become compulsory**, but only after buyers adopt them as their default search, and the supply side ends up paying. Pholio inverts this (creatives free, buyers pay later), which is what Models.com Pro does, but Models.com has the editorial prestige layer. [INFERENCE]
2. **Mutual credits already exist as Instagram collabs** (invite and accept, max 6). Use them as the drafting signal, and differentiate on roles, team size above 6, and issuer confirmation. [INFERENCE]
3. **Issuer prestige is the value of an ISSUED credit.** FWB is a fine bootstrap issuer, but seat-buyers (casting directors, agencies) pay for the top of the graph, not the long tail. [INFERENCE]
4. **Verification that sells today is audience authenticity** (HypeAuditor and Modash at $100–400/month per seat) **and identity** (Zodel, Models.com uploader checks), not work history. [FACT + INFERENCE]
5. **Every model-booking marketplace that bypassed agencies stayed small** (Swipecast, Zodel, Snapcast). Pholio should be agency-compatible. [INFERENCE]
6. **Presentation tiers are commoditized** at $5–15/month, with link-in-bio consolidating through acquisitions. [FACT]

**Summary [INFERENCE]:** Nobody pays for "verified work history" as such. People pay for **access to buyers** (supply side), **deal flow** (intermediaries), **search over a curated or prestige layer** (buyers, at about $20/month per seat, not IMDbPro-scale enterprise), and **fraud and audience verification** (brands). A credits graph monetizes only if it becomes **the place buyers search** (the Spotlight and Models.com path). Designers paying for runway visibility is the nearest paying customer in the FWB ecosystem, which suggests an issuer-side product (the event's official credit record and casting intake) may monetize before search seats do. That fits Pholio's existing events module.
