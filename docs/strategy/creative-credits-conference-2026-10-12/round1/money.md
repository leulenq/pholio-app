# Round 1: money (Monetization)

## 1. Verdict
The thesis has no paying customer for its first 18 months. It stacks three revenue lines (a free creative side, a Pro presentation tier, and IMDbPro-style search seats later), and the research has evidence against each one. So it is an unfunded nonprofit with a cap table.

## 2. Three strongest arguments

**A. Verified credits are not a product anyone buys. Jaxsta proves it at the top possible quality.** [FACT] Jaxsta held 380M+ official, issuer-supplied credits from all three majors, which is better verification than Pholio will ever have. It closed in 2025 because "the model does not scale" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaxsta; ASX filing https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20241209/pdf/06cdn25s0nv6hr.pdf). The pattern repeats elsewhere. Muso turned 4M profiles into an estimated ~$2M in revenue (third-party estimate, unverified: https://prospeo.io/c/muso-ai). FMD decayed into volunteer upkeep once it had no paying side (https://fashionmodeldirectory.com/info). Polywork returned its capital (https://x.com/multiplay3r/status/1873859669065978327). [INFERENCE] Verification quality is not where the revenue comes from. Whoever has the budget and the pain is.

**B. The fashion money already flows to organizers and to presence, not to the creative's record.** [FACT] Designers pay to show: Harlem FW charges $250–1,000 and Nolcha packages run $1,500–25,000 (https://edgexpo.com/2017/02/21/edge-fast-fact-fashion-week-designer-costs). Vogue Runway sells about $20k a year in placement to designers (https://www.businessoffashion.com/articles/media/vogue-runway-is-charging-some-brands-to-post-their-collection-images). Le Book sells a $7,500 table in front of buyers (https://apanational.org/events/apa-sf-lebook-connections-2025/). Kavyar publishers charge submission fees (https://help.kavyar.com/article/p03wnxoixc). FWB already staffs hair, makeup, styling, dressers and casting for every show, and its model registration runs on Google Forms (https://www.fashionweekbrooklyn.com/registration-process). [INFERENCE] The organizer has revenue, a recurring operations problem every season, and the roster. The thesis makes the organizer's tools free "at first". That gives away the one buyer with a budget, a card, and a real problem.

**C. Charging creatives for visibility makes money but destroys trust, and the presentation tier is a commodity.** [FACT] Spotlight earns £205.80/yr per performer and is now in litigation with Equity over its fees (https://www.screendaily.com/news/uk-actors-union-equity-loses-legal-case-against-casting-directory-spotlight/5208502.article). Snapcast's pay-to-be-scouted app has a 1.0 rating (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1497685246). Portfolio and link-in-bio tools sell at $5–15 a month, and Linktree is consolidating the category through acquisitions (https://u2l.ai/blog/linktree-pricing). [INFERENCE] Pholio cannot sell creatives ranking or reach without becoming the scam it claims to fight. A $8 presentation Pro is fine as a supplement, but it is a Linktree feature and will not fund the company.

## 3. The claim most wrong
**"The later business is paid search seats on the verified graph … on the IMDbPro model."**
The IMDbPro model does not transfer, for five reasons:
1. IMDb *inherited* trust from contractual on-screen credits and enormous organic traffic. What Pro actually sells is contact data and development tracking (R1 §1). Pholio has neither.
2. IMDb itself walls off modeling, commercial and theater credits as "self-verified" (https://help.imdb.com/article/imdbpro/manage-your-imdb-page/how-do-updates-on-my-resume-work/GMLWQXHRJL84G66W).
3. The fashion seat business already exists: Models.com Pro at $20/mo, with a 50-view free cap (https://help.models.com/article/15/what-is-modelscom-pro-membership). It sits on the prestige tier, while Pholio's graph covers the tier that seat-buyers search least.
4. Hirers hire through contacts: 82% of fashion jobs (https://bectu.org.uk/news/three-quarters-of-creative-industries-jobs-go-to-friends-and-connections-reveals-bectus-bectu-survey), and only 7% of commercial buyers use open search (https://campaigns.at-edge.com/survey-results/).
5. Breakdown Services keeps casting directors free because their attention is the scarce side (https://breakdownservices.com/index.cfm/main/faq).

Arithmetic [ASSUMPTION on price]: at $240/yr per seat, $1M ARR needs about 4,200 paying hirers who search a graph of Brooklyn and school-show credits. No research file found any hirer asking "is this credit verified?" (R4 "Against" #2).

## 4. Proposed revision: who pays, for what, how much, when

**Rule zero:** No payment can buy an ISSUED credit, a ranking, a badge or visibility. Paying for a tool and being authorized to issue credits are separate decisions. Issuer vetting (R3 ladder) runs whether or not the organizer pays. An organizer who is not vetted can use the tool, but its credits display as "Added by [organizer], not verified". This closes the pay-to-walk laundering hole that trust will raise ($625 NYFW-branded forms: https://form.jotform.com/260485991034159).

**Payer 1, now (seasons 1–2): event and show organizers, for "Show Desk".** Show Desk is the operations tool for running a show: open-call intake that replaces Google Forms, designer pick lists, offer/confirm, consent records, call sheets, and a published team-credit page per show. All of it is already in the codebase. Credits are a by-product of that work, not the product. [BET] Pricing is $20 per designer slot per event with a $250 minimum, so FWB's ~40 slots come to about $800 a season. FWB gets 2 free seasons as a design partner, then pays. Designers pay organizers $250+ to show, so $20 a slot is under 10% of the cheapest known fee. It goes on a card through Stripe subscription billing. No procurement is involved, and Pholio never touches money moving between the parties. Targets: regional fashion weeks (Queens is already the same operator), school BFA shows, indie runway producers, and photo/zine submission calls.

**Payer 2, month 6 onward: creatives, for packaging they need at a gate, never for being seen.**
- Pro at $8/mo covers presentation, comp cards and custom domains.
- An "Evidence Pack" at $29 one-off, or included in Pro, produces a dated, issuer-attested credit dossier for agency applications, grants and O-1B petitions.
- [FACT, secondary] About 19k O-1 visas were issued in FY2023, with O-1A and O-1B combined (https://tryalma.com/learn/o1-approval-rate-statistics). That makes the dossier a niche line, not the main business, but it puts verification to work exactly where R4 found real demand.

**Payer 3, conditional, no earlier than about 18 months: hirer seats, gated by evidence.** Seats launch only after a measured trigger (for example, ≥200 distinct hirer accounts searching weekly). Price is $20/mo per seat, the Models.com benchmark, paid by personal card or expensed. The seat buys *contact*: messaging creatives requires an ID-verified hirer account plus a card on file. Payment then works as friction against scammers, the way Breakdown Services gates on licensed reps. It is never a trust signal on its own, which is the Twitter Blue lesson. Search over CLAIMED and MUTUAL credits stays free and separated, and only ISSUED credits sit in the paid layer.

**Kill:** the "free for creatives forever" promise. Promise that the *record* is free forever. Do not promise that the *tools* are (Vero's "free for life" trap: https://www.inverse.com/article/41772-vero-free-for-life-offer-extraordinary-demand).
