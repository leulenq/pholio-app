# Round 1: VC (skeptical investor), opening position

## 1. Verdict
As written, the thesis is The Dots plus Jaxsta with a smaller issuer: a credit graph that has no buyer, no why-now, and a schema anyone can copy in a quarter. In its current form I would not fund it, because it is a lifestyle niche dressed up as IMDbPro.

## 2. Three strongest arguments

**A. Better verification has already been tried at 1,000x scale, and the business still failed. [FACT]**
Jaxsta had more than 380M official credits, supplied by issuers that included all three major labels. It still went into "hibernation" in Dec 2025, saying "the current model does not scale" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaxsta); [ASX filing](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20241209/pdf/06cdn25s0nv6hr.pdf)). Behance has offered team co-ownership to about 56M members and is still not a hiring authority ([Behance help](https://help.behance.net/hc/en-us/articles/204483494-Guide-Co-owning-And-Crediting)). The Dots raised about £5.5M on "community-driven IMDb" credits and now sells a white-label member app to Soho House, Frieze and Dazed ([London Tech Week](https://londontechweek.com/speakers/pip-jamieson)). The ISSUED tier is Pholio's only real improvement on The Dots, and Jaxsta already proved that ISSUED on its own does not make a business. Verification quality was never the bottleneck. Buyer demand was.

**B. The search-seat model points at the wrong payer in the wrong tier, and the arithmetic is small. [FACT + ASSUMPTION]**
IMDbPro does not sell credits. It sells gated contacts and development tracking, built on a free consumer database with enormous organic traffic. Even IMDb puts modeling and commercial credits in a walled-off "self-verified" Pro tier ([IMDb help](https://help.imdb.com/article/imdbpro/manage-your-imdb-page/how-do-updates-on-my-resume-work/GMLWQXHRJL84G66W)). In fashion, the paid-seat position is already held by Models.com, at $20/month with a 50-view cap on the free tier ([Models.com](https://help.models.com/article/15/what-is-modelscom-pro-membership)), and it covers the top tier, which is where seat-buyers actually hire. Pholio's opening is the regional and BFA tier, where buyers are least willing to pay (R1 §6, R4 §3). R4 found **no source** showing a fashion or photo hirer asking whether a credit is verified, and 75–82% of fashion and film jobs come through contacts ([Bectu 2025](https://bectu.org.uk/news/three-quarters-of-creative-industries-jobs-go-to-friends-and-connections-reveals-bectus-bectu-survey)).

Here is the arithmetic [ASSUMPTION]. A very optimistic case is 2,000 paying seats at Models.com's $240/year, which comes to **$480k ARR**. Muso, which has roughly 4M profiles and a $9.99 Pro tier, is estimated at around $2M in revenue with no disclosed round (R1 §2, unverified estimate).

**C. There is no why-now and no moat, and well-funded players are already in the slot. [FACT]**
- Creators Guild of America's **Mosaic** (Mar 2026) offers "Authenticated by [org]" credits, collaborator verification and a Creator ID. It is free and onboarded 12k+ creators at launch ([CGA](https://creatorsguildofamerica.org/credits)). That is the ISSUED tier almost word for word.
- **Cosmos** raised a $15M Series A in Jan 2026 specifically to restore credit to images ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/cosmos-15-million-series-a-closed-as-it-expands-social-discovery-and-attribution-features)).
- **Instagram** already provides role-labeled tags and invite-and-accept collab posts (R1 §13, R4 §4).

FWB, the anchor issuer, has about 40 designer slots per season. R4 estimates that is a ceiling of about 1,000 credited people a year across Brooklyn and Queens. That is a seed list, not a network effect. The only why-now I believe in is AI-altered photos combined with talent-side fraud, and the thesis treats that as a footnote.

## 3. The claim most wrong
**"The later business is paid search seats on the verified graph … on the IMDbPro model."**

This is the claim that makes the thesis look venture-scale, and it has the weakest evidence in the brief. IMDbPro works because the film and TV credit was already canonical (set by guild contracts and shown on screen), and IMDb inherited that trust. Pholio's categories have no canonical record, so Pholio would have to *manufacture* trust from one regional issuer and then persuade hirers who hire by referral to start paying to search. A plan that says "free for everyone now, buyers pay later" is a plan with no customer.

## 4. Proposed revision (attack this)
**Flip the payer. Pholio becomes the casting and roster system of record for organizers who select creatives: events, school shows, indie magazines, and later music-video and brand productions. Credits are the exhaust of that workflow, not the product.**

- **Product:** an open call that replaces Google Forms, then two-stage selection, designer pick lists, offer and confirm, consent snapshots, and a published credit page generated automatically from the confirmed roster. All of this already exists in the codebase (brief). Creatives get free profiles because the organizer requires them to apply. That is the acquisition loop, so no admin pitch is needed.
- **Money [BET]:** the organizer pays a per-event or per-season SaaS fee for its own tool. This is Pholio billing its own customer, so Pholio never moves money between parties and procurement never changes. A second line is a per-designer "casting room" add-on that the organizer bundles into the show fees designers already pay. Search seats are deferred indefinitely and only revisited if credit pages show organic hirer traffic.
- **Venture test, with kill criteria by the end of the Spring 2027 season [BET]:**
  1. FWB runs 100% of model and crew registration through Pholio.
  2. At least 5 organizers *outside the founders' network* pay at least $1,500/season.
  3. Credit pages receive at least 200 lookups from accounts that were not participants.

  If (2) fails, this is a tool business. Be honest about that and do not raise venture money. If (2) passes, the scale story is the casting workflow for every small production that hires creatives. That is the Talent Systems / Casting Networks path, not IMDbPro.

## 5. The question I most want answered
**To growth, money and hirer: name the first person, by title and organization, other than FWB, who pays Pholio in the next 120 days. Which weekly job do they already do that Pholio replaces, and what tool do they stop using?** If nobody can name that person, every graph slide in this deck is decoration.
