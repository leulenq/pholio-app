# Round 4 — Skeptical VC: recant the opportunity subscription

**Vote: REJECT H as a venture thesis; AMEND toward a portfolio-based repeat-casting desk.** I withdraw my proposal to ration free applications and sell unlimited applications. My precedent established that someone can sell access to work. It did not establish that Pholio owns work worth buying access to. A subscription to ten future calls, two of them tests, would monetize the founders’ promise before demonstrating its value.

## Corrections before argument

[FACT about our evidence] The Adorama citation was a shoot-planning guide, not a representative measurement of photographer frequency. “One test a month” must become [ASSUMPTION], and every loop calculation using it loses its factual foundation. Measure actual dated productions; distinguish shoots led from shoots joined and paid jobs from tests.

[FACT] Muso publishes creator, Business and developer products, including catalog API subscriptions: [creator pricing](https://muso.ai/pricing), [platform pricing](https://platform.muso.ai/pricing). Those are published offers, not realized revenue. I retract “Muso-scale means $2M” and the inference that Muso proves a ceiling for credit businesses. Our revenue estimate was unverified and cannot carry that argument.

[ASSUMPTION, withdrawn as market cap] “2,000 NYC leads,” “200k–500k global leads,” and “under $100k organizer revenue” were scenarios assembled from guessed counts and adoption. Multiplication is not market research. Neither Brooklyn nor NYC establishes a global ceiling. We need a countable customer segment and actual purchase behavior before making TAM claims.

[BET, withdrawn] My 1,000-active-user, 25%-cross-call and 4%-conversion venture gates were arbitrary screening proposals, not validated venture criteria. The 5% prepayment threshold also has no demonstrated relationship to retention or economics. Twenty friendly purchases cannot establish a scalable market; zero purchases in an undersupplied feed cannot kill the entire category.

## Two decisive primary checks

[FACT] [Backstage’s subscription help](https://help.backstage.com/en/articles/12633157-backstage-subscriptions-benefits) explicitly includes applying to calls, searchable profiles, alerts and application tracking. This validates an existing paid bundle, not our demand. Its [performer page](https://www.backstage.com/actors-and-performers/) advertises thousands of jobs available daily. We cannot borrow that inventory advantage by putting FWB on a feed.

[FACT] [Spotlight’s performer eligibility](https://support.spotlight.com/en/articles/11812289) accepts specific professional credits or other qualifying routes; under its experience route it excludes unpaid work and requires qualifying contractual or financial evidence. Its [memberships page](https://www.spotlight.com/memberships/) also offers casting-professional subscriptions. My blanket assertion that scaled casting always subsidizes buyers was wrong. Spotlight connects a qualified population to professional casting. It does not validate charging beginners to apply to TFP shoots.

## The fight

**Devil:** Your opportunity record writes “selected” and “worked” into one pipeline too easily. Acceptance establishes intent; attendance establishes presence; completed work establishes neither publication nor permission to disclose. You have named a reader, but your free-posting marketplace still needs differentiated supply. Calling its posters verified does not make their gigs valuable or safe.

**Money:** Your “3% means seed, otherwise no venture” is accounting theater. Also, model releases are contracts. Selling release collection inside Pro crosses the explicit founder boundary. Remove it rather than making the old production suite palatable through a $12 price.

**Founder:** Allowing assistants to initiate credits fixes authorship, not demand. Your median-two-productions gate could be passed by enthusiastic friends exchanging confirmations without a single casting decision changing. Seven months of building should make us especially suspicious of a retention metric detached from an external task.

**Hirer:** “Agency intake from month nine” is an unsupported calendar. Test payment now for a real intake task. A reviewer saying they recognize a confirmer in Figma is weaker evidence than watching them use the record on actual applications.

## One thesis, one reader and payer

[BET] **Pholio is the portfolio and returning-talent desk for independent runway organizers.** The reader is the organizer’s casting lead; the payer is the organization buying intake, reusable talent files, shortlist coordination and returning-participant invitations. FWB is the first design partner, not proof of a market. Designer reviewers can access the selected pool without replacing anyone’s procurement, contracts or payments.

Credits are a free portfolio output after a named organizer confirms participation. Selection alone creates no work credit. The second use is the next real casting: prior participants refresh their portfolio and consent; the organizer revisits its previous pool. This compounds as reusable records and relationships, not a universal reputation score.

[ASSUMPTION] Rebuilding forms, reconciling designer lists and chasing confirmations costs enough to buy this. Observe FWB doing those tasks; offer a clearly priced next-event pilot to an unrelated organizer. Compare real time saved and selection outcomes, then seek an actual renewal. Record failed sales and reasons. Until those observations exist, this is a testable small-business wedge with unknown expansion potential. I prefer that honest uncertainty to inventing venture arithmetic or selling creatives access to an empty room.
