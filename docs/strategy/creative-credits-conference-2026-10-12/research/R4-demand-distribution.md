# R4: Demand side and distribution

Lane: how hirers vet creatives, whether they would ever confirm a credit, how much creatives care about credit, the FWB ecosystem, other NYC scenes, and how often anyone looks credits up.
Researched 2026-10-12. Tags: **[FACT](url)**, **[INFERENCE]** (my reading of the facts), **[ASSUMPTION]**. Where I could not find a number, I say so.

---

## 1. How hirers actually vet creatives

### Discovery and shortlisting
- **[FACT](https://campaigns.at-edge.com/survey-results/)** AtEdge Creative Survey, Oct 2024. Respondents were creative directors 27%, producers 12% and art buyers 10%; 44% worked at agencies and 12% at in-house brands. **44%** find photographers and directors mainly through portfolio platforms (AtEdge, Workbook, Found, Wonderful Machine). **24%** ask their network, **10%** contact reps or production companies directly, and only **7%** use Google or social search. The survey does not give its sample size.
- **[FACT](https://campaigns.at-edge.com/survey-results/)** In the same survey, the platforms "most important to their work" were Instagram 80%, LinkedIn 56%, and Behance and TikTok 17% and 15%. 71% want a creative "who brings ideas and can execute". 57% said influencers contribute "not at all" to their campaigns.
- **[FACT](https://go.photoshelter.com/photographers/blog/2013-survey-buyers-want-photographers)** PhotoShelter and Agency Access, 2013 (n=340 buyers). 42.1% used search engines and 25.2% used reps, agencies or directories. Among editorial buyers who discover photographers through social media, 55% use Instagram. *This is old data.*
- **[FACT](https://wonderfulmachine.com/article/phone-calls-spring-summer-2019/)** Wonderful Machine's 2019 client calls found that clients "frequently rely on Instagram to find new talent" and that LinkedIn "continues to grow in importance".
- **[FACT](https://bectu.org.uk/news/three-quarters-of-creative-industries-jobs-go-to-friends-and-connections-reveals-bectus-bectu-survey)** Bectu Big Survey, UK, Oct 2025, more than 5,500 non-performing creative workers. **75%** got their most recent job through a friend or contact, rising to **82%** in film, TV drama and fashion. Only **27%** of fashion roles were publicly advertised. Bectu calls it a "closed hiring culture". This extends the prior brief's 64–75% referral figures with a fashion-specific number.
- **[FACT](https://www.glossy.co/fashion/you-need-instagram-to-make-it-as-a-model-even-if-youre-kendall-jenner/)** Glossy, 2016. Mary Clarke of Mother Model Management said Instagram is about **30%** of her agency's scouting, and "way higher" at other agencies. She said social status "affects day rates". Clients and casting directors "want to know more" about personality and communication. Estée Lauder signed Kendall Jenner as the "ultimate Instagirl".
- **[FACT](https://www.glossy.co/fashion/you-need-instagram-to-make-it-as-a-model-even-if-youre-kendall-jenner/)** Same piece. Show producer Stefan Campbell described a common strategy: take a lesser-paid show to gain followers, then negotiate higher pay. *Follower count works as currency in fashion even where credits do not.*
- **[FACT](https://www.castingnetworks.com/news/do-followers-matter-top-casting-directors-weigh-in-on-social-media-and-acting-careers)** Casting Networks, from the search summary because the page returned 403. Three acting casting directors said follower counts "in most cases, no" do not factor in, but what a person posts does. A manager at Untitled said social media "can make the difference" between two actors.
- **[FACT](https://artsprofessional.co.uk/news/newsreel/social-media-following-factor-casting-roles-actors-say)** UK actors report auditions asking for follower data or a minimum follower count. The Casting Directors' Guild called this "disheartening". *Taken from the search summary; the page returned 403.*

### Verification behavior: do hirers check credits?
- **[FACT](https://www.backstage.com/advice-for-actors/backstage-experts/why-actors-shouldnt-lie-their-resumes/)** A casting director writing in Backstage found that an actor's claimed lead role was false: she was not the lead, the understudy or in the ensemble. Advice pieces say casting directors "talk to each other" and "check IMDb". **No statistic exists on how often this happens.**
- **[FACT](https://www.starnow.com/magazine/article/imdb-for-actors-explained-80523/)** Industry professionals use IMDbPro to research people and projects, and casting directors use it to search talent by skills and eligibility.
- **[FACT](https://help.imdb.com/article/contribution/filmography-credits/adding-filmography-credits/GAB5B33MBLEG73CT)** IMDb accepts only credits that are "verifiable at the point of submission", and the evidence bar rises with the rank of the title. For on-screen credits it wants a screen capture of the credit roll. Call sheets, contracts and pay stubs are accepted only for *uncredited* roles. Commercial, modeling, music and theater credits are "self-verified" and appear only on IMDbPro.
- **[FACT](https://atg.wa.gov/modeling-agencies-teens)** Washington State Attorney General: casting directors "frequently contact talent agents to ask for referrals". *The trust check runs through the agent, not through a record.*
- **[FACT](https://www.beyondborderglobal.com/resources/o-1b-for-fashion-professionals-runway-editorial-and-brand-reputation-evidence-strategies)** For O-1B visa petitions, fashion photographers, stylists and models prove their record with tear sheets, campaign evidence and **testimonial letters from industry figures confirming work history**. **[FACT](https://meritdraft.com/decisions/o-1b-dismissed-fashion-model-2018-03-30.html)** A 2018 USCIS appeal concerned a model's O-1B that was revoked because a testimonial letter was falsified.
- **[FACT](https://www.shrm.org/in/topics-tools/employment-law-compliance/job-references-can-misleading)** Employers commonly use neutral-reference policies, with HR confirming only dates and positions. A trade write-up of a SHRM survey said about 54% of organizations have policies against giving references, out of liability fear ([vehicleservicepros](https://www.vehicleservicepros.com/aftermarket-business-world/news/article/21176712/reference-checks-identify-poor-performers)). *This figure is secondary and dated.*
- **[FACT](https://www.getphyllo.com/post/3-common-influencer-vetting-methods-fail)** / **[FACT](https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2019/40956/how-marketers-vet-social-media-influencers)** ACTIVATE 2019 (n=111 marketers). Marketers vet influencers on content (89%), engagement rate (81%), audience demographics (73%) and **prior brand affinities (61%)**. 52% have a process for detecting fake followers. A 2026 vendor guide warns that brands too often accept metrics the creator self-reports.

### Friction that blocks confirming a credit
- **[FACT](https://www.rocketlawyer.com/q-and-a/answer/are-portfolio-usage-rights-different-for-confidential-projects)** When a contract has a confidentiality clause or NDA, unreleased work generally cannot be shown without client permission. **[FACT](https://www.freelancermap.com/blog/can-i-share-nda-protected-work-on-my-portfolio-tips-and-advice/)** Some clients bar freelancers from even *saying* they worked on a campaign.
- **[FACT](https://www.iphotography.com/blog/business-photography-industry-legal-terms-explained/)** Commercial shoots routinely carry embargoes, buyouts and exclusivity that hold images until a launch date.
- **[FACT](https://aftermath.site/most-video-game-artwork-will-never-be-seen.md)** Freelancers work around NDAs with password-protected pages, private PDFs, or showing work only during interviews.
- **[FACT](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/less-than-half-of-developers-say-their-studio-has-a-credits-policy-new-survey-finds)** IGDA Developer Satisfaction Survey, 2024 (n=777). Only **48%** of studios have a credits policy. **13%** were not credited on their last project, and **13% of freelancers** get no in-game credit. Confidence of being credited falls from 71% to 41% if the developer leaves before release. **[FACT](https://igda.org/resources-archive/igda-game-crediting-guidelines-10-1-march-2023-update)** IGDA guidelines say anyone who worked on a game for 30 days or 5% of development should be credited, but the guidelines are voluntary. In 2023, 51.3% of 582 respondents said they "never, seldom or sometimes" receive credit ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/features/game-credits-are-a-broken-mess)).
- **[INFERENCE]** The game industry has a professional association, written guidelines and a cultural norm of credit rolls, and still roughly half of studios have no policy. Corporate issuers do not volunteer credits without obligation. Brands, which have no credit-roll norm at all, will be worse issuers than events or publications whose identity *is* the published work.

## 2. How much creatives care about credit
- **[FACT](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/bmg-backs-credits-due-campaign-launched-by-abbas-bjorn-ulvaeus)** **Credits Due** was launched by Björn Ulvaeus at the Ivors in Sept 2021 with the Ivors Academy and the Music Rights Awareness Foundation. It pushes for creator metadata (IPI/ISNI, ISWC, ISRC, names and roles) to be captured *at the point of creation*. BMG was the first major to commit. *The key lesson: capture credits at creation, not after the fact. That matches the "issued at the event" idea.*
- **[FACT](https://completemusicupdate.com/ivors-academy-launches-campaign-to-end-ghostwriting-when-music-is-created-for-film-and-tv/)** Oct 2025: the Ivors Academy launched "End Ghostwriting", a campaign against uncredited composition in film and TV.
- **[FACT](https://www.grammy.com/news/the-recording-academy-launches-give-fans-the-credit/)** The Recording Academy ran "Give Fans the Credit" in 2012 because digital releases dropped liner notes. It took **13 years** for **[FACT](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-rolls-out-expanded-song-credits-previews-new-songdna-and-about-the-song-features-for-premium-users)** Spotify to expand credits to engineers and musicians and add SongDNA, in Nov 2025. Those credits come from **labels and distributors**, which are institutional issuers.
- **[FACT](https://thefashionlaw.com/elie-taharis-reposting-of-photo-on-social-media-is-not-trivial-or-fair-use)** In *Iantosca v. Elie Tahari*, a federal court in New York held that tagging or crediting a photographer in a caption is no defense to infringement. *A credit is not a license. Pholio must not imply that a credit grants any usage right.*
- **[FACT](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/ikrd/quavo-photographer-instagram)** Photographer Breanna Seward's public call-out of Quavo, and later Motown, for uncredited reposts drew wide sympathy from other photographers. **[FACT](https://petapixel.com/2017/04/21/copyright-censorship-instagram-marie-claire-stole-photo/)** A photographer said Marie Claire reposted a collaborative shoot with the credits stripped, including the model's credit.
- **[FACT](https://techcrunch.com/2015/01/07/get-off-of-my-clowdy/)** "In the creative industry, your portfolio is the currency you trade in. Many people collaborate on a project but few are recognised." This is the founder pitch of Clowdy, a 2015 credits-showcase startup that no longer operates. *It is precedent for the pitch, not for the outcome.*
- **[FACT](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/notofreelabour-sg-ad-creatives-initiate-campaign-to-combat-exploitation)** Singapore's #NoToFreeLabour campaign claims creatives lose up to US$6,620 a year to unpaid spec work. *The method is not stated.*
- **[FACT](https://www.vice.com/en/article/yes-i-am-model-v15n4/)** TFP (time for print) is the standard early-career trade: nobody is paid and everyone gets images. Photographers post large numbers of TFP casting calls. **[FACT](https://retouch4.me/blog/trade-for-print-or-time-for-print-photos-depending-on-who-you-ask-is-one-of-the-first-concepts-a-portrait-photographer-encounters-the-model-doesnt-get-paid-the-photographer-do)** Practitioners say TFP between two beginners often produces weak work.
- **[FACT](https://help.kavyar.com/article/p03wnxoixc)** Kavyar, the dominant submission platform for indie fashion magazines, requires every submitted photo to be "properly credited at the time of submission" with **Instagram handles for the whole team**. Publishers may charge **non-refundable submission fees**. *A pay-to-be-published economy already exists, and credits are the product being bought.*
- **[INFERENCE]** Creatives care intensely about *attribution as visibility*, meaning being tagged on the post that circulates. There is far less evidence that they care about attribution *as a verified record*. The visible outrage is about lost exposure and lost licensing fees, not about lack of proof.

## 3. Fashion Week Brooklyn and the regional ecosystem

### Verified FWB facts
- **[FACT](https://www.fashionweekbrooklyn.com)** FWBK is produced by BK Style Foundation, a 501(c)(3) working in Brooklyn "since 2006". It runs twice a year. The site banner lists Brooklyn, London, Japan, Paris, Canada and Africa. It was named No. 5 International Fashion Event by Fashionado in 2015. Models, stylists, beauty team, creatives, volunteers and designers all register through **linked Google Forms**, which confirms the brief.
- **[FACT](https://www.fashionweekbrooklyn.com/schedule)** Season 2 2026 ran Oct 4–8. It had six events and **about 40 designer slots**, some designers appearing more than once: a Brooklyn x Japan / Pratt Creative Exhibition (7), Slayway LGBTQIA+ (5), Young Designers with **Rutgers** (7), Up Next emerging (5) and Very Brooklyn Show (9). Venues were small, including Atolye NYC in Park Slope and Industry City.
- **[FACT](https://www.fashionweekbrooklyn.com/registration-process)** Designer registration includes the FWBK **hair and makeup team, styling team, dressers, music director, casting from FWBK's open call, a models coordinator, and PR outreach**. Shows run 3–4 days with **7–8 designers a day** and about 15 minutes each. Standard is 10–15 looks, with more looks at "additional registration cost". **No fee amounts are published.** *I could not verify the brief's "designers pay to show" from a public source; the "additional cost" wording implies a base cost.*
- **[INFERENCE]** That package is significant: FWB itself staffs the hair, makeup, styling, dressing and casting for every show, so it already holds the full team roster for each show. Credit issuance would be a byproduct of rosters it already keeps. The weak point is photographers, who are mostly not on that roster.
- **[FACT](https://visitbrooklyn.nyc/now-in-bklyn/a-very-brooklyn-fashion-show-2026-celebrates-20-years-of-fashion-week-brooklyn)** The 4th "A Very Brooklyn Fashion Show" was held Oct 8, 2026 at Industry City with 8 designers. It was co-presented with Brooklyn Made and the **Brooklyn Chamber of Commerce**. Sponsors were Wells Fargo, TD Bank and Brooklyn Arts Council, with funding from NYC DCLA. 2026 marks FWB's **20th anniversary**. **[FACT](https://brooklynmadestore.com/pages/brooklyn-fashion-show-2026)** Selected designers get no stipend; the offer is "exposure".
- **[FACT](https://www.bkreader.com/featured-news/where-brooklyn-at-fashion-week-brooklyn-highlights-hip-hop-culture-in-bk-the-metaverse-and-beyond-6831994)** Founder Rick Davy, a stylist, reportedly sold his condo to self-fund it. The 2023 season ran at the Brooklyn Navy Yard and included Karl Kani and Starter in a Hip Hop 50 show, a Web3 digital show and a kids' show. Davy: "we lend credibility to their work". *The founder's own positioning is credibility-lending, which fits the thesis.*
- **[FACT](https://qns.com/2026/07/queens-center-launch-fashion-week-queens/)** **Fashion Week Queens** launched July 17–18, 2026, co-founded by Rick Davy and Jack Mapira and organized by BK Style Foundation. It had a ribbon-cutting with the Queens Borough President and a runway at Queens Borough Hall with **6–7 designers**.
- **[FACT](https://www.tokyoweekender.com/art_and_culture/fashion/fashion-week-brooklyn-from-new-york-to-tokyo/)** Japanese designers such as Momoyo Stoby show at FWBK. Davy is creative director. I found **no evidence of a standalone Tokyo edition**.
- **[FACT](https://www.bklynlibrary.org/calendar/bklyn-fashion-academy-central-library-business-20240724-0500pm)** BKLYN Fashion Academy model castings at Brooklyn Public Library (2022–24) feed FWB showcases. Models needed a comp card and must be 17+. **[FACT](https://www.brooklynbp.nyc.gov/event/the-peoples-runway-2/)** FWB ran casting for the Borough President's People's Runway (Sept 2025). **[FACT](https://www.brooklynbp.nyc.gov/peoplesrunway2026/)** The People's Runway is on the CFDA's official NYFW calendar.
- **[FACT](https://projectcasting.tagdiv.com/?p=1104914)** Third-party FWB-linked casting listings offer "exposure at a major fashion event" and networking, with no stated wage. *I found no evidence that FWB charges models.*
- **[INFERENCE] Scale:** about 40 designer slots, with roughly 10–15 looks each and models walking several shows. That is likely a few hundred unique participants per season including hair, makeup, styling and dressers, but **I have no published headcount**. At 2 seasons plus Queens, the ceiling is probably about 1,000 credited people a year. It is a seed, not a network.

### Pay-to-show and pay-to-walk risk
- **[FACT](https://www.buzzfeed.com/hannahmarder/are-fashion-week-models-paid)** BuzzFeed, Oct 2022, on non-CFDA "NYFW" platforms. Runway 7 confirmed **designers pay a production fee**, and model pay is "up to the designer". Models reported walking 7 shows and being paid for one, or netting about $85 across two shows. **Art Hearts** said models signed waivers to walk "for exposure". Hair and makeup artists said going unpaid is "very standard". CFDA declined to comment. "NYFW" is used loosely by events not on the CFDA or IMG calendars.
- **[FACT](https://caplinnews.fiu.edu/?p=20474)** / **[FACT](https://edgexpo.com/2017/02/21/edge-fast-fact-fashion-week-designer-costs)** Fees reported elsewhere: Harlem Fashion Week $250–$1,000 per designer; Nolcha packages $1,500–$25,000. One designer said showing is "99 percent of the time" not free, at around $1,500. *These are from search summaries; the pages were not opened individually.*
- **[FACT](https://www.news24.com/citypress/trending/caught-in-a-fashion-furore-20160521)** South Africa, 2016: a promoter with no fashion background charged young designers R3,500 each. Designers publicly warned about "exploitation of young designers who pay to appear".
- **[FACT](https://fashionweekonline.com/fashion-week-modeling-guide)** Industry guides say models should never pay to walk. Pay-to-play shows charge models anywhere from a few hundred to several thousand dollars.
- **[INFERENCE]** The reputational risk is real but asymmetric. FWB is a 20-year nonprofit with Borough President, Chamber of Commerce and DCLA ties, so it sits well above the scam tier. Still, the regional fashion-week category is crowded with pay-to-play operators, and an "ISSUED by a fashion week" credit is only worth the issuer's reputation. A credit system that admits any self-declared "fashion week" as an issuer would launder exactly that tier. **Issuer vetting is part of the product**, and the existing registry-verification code is relevant here.

### How school and NYFW shows publish credits today
- **[FACT](https://models.com/work/parsons-mfa-2024-show)** Parsons MFA shows have **Models.com work pages listing hair, makeup, casting director and the full model roster**, e.g. hair by John Vial, makeup by T. Cooper and casting by Ian William Bauman. **[FACT](https://theimpression.com/parsons-presents-mfa-show/)** Parsons MFA shows run with IMG and Beyond8 Productions, with full hair and makeup credits in press.
- **[FACT](https://www.pratt.edu/news/runway-ready-pratt-fashion-seniors-shine-at-powerhouse-arts/)** Pratt's 124th show (May 16, 2025, Powerhouse Arts) had **30 BFA seniors and about 160 looks**. Pratt's own coverage does not list production or model credits. **[FACT](https://news.fitnyc.edu/2026/05/11/presenting-the-next-generation-of-designers-at-the-bfa-runway-show/)** FIT's 2026 show had **80 graduates and 91 looks**, presented by Macy's with Dior makeup. No model or crew credits appeared in the coverage I found.
- **[FACT](https://help.models.com/article/61/adding-talent-credits-to-a-new-uploaded-work)** **Models.com already is a credit graph for top-tier fashion.** Agencies and individuals upload work and tag photographers, stylists, hair, makeup, casting and models. Only people who *already have a Models.com page* can be tagged, so untagged crew simply go uncredited. Talent verify by Instagram login or photo ID, and Models.com "does not moderate credit disputes" ([help](https://help.models.com/article/24/addingremoving-agencies-from-my-page)).
- **[INFERENCE]** Credit publication is stratified. CFDA, IMG and top schools get credited on Models.com and in press. Regional weeks and school BFA shows mostly do not, apart from Instagram tags. That gap is exactly Pholio's opening. It also means the hirers who matter for the top tier already have Models.com, and Pholio's record would cover the tier those hirers rarely search.

## 4. Other reachable NYC scenes and how they credit today
- **Music:** **[FACT](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-rolls-out-expanded-song-credits-previews-new-songdna-and-about-the-song-features-for-premium-users)** Spotify credits are supplied by labels and distributors. **[FACT](https://www.musicconnection.com/up-close-muso-ai/)** Muso.ai (Nov 2022): more than **5.5M** profiles. Credit edits require an **ID scan**, and changes are validated by artists and **master-rights holders**, which is an issuer-confirmation model. **[FACT](https://webcf.waybackmachine.org/web/20220512102908/https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discogs)** Discogs (May 2022) had 15.1M releases, 8M artists and **628k contributor accounts**, a crowdsourced credit database built by collectors rather than by the people credited. *Music credits are already served by metadata pipelines and Muso, so Pholio would have no edge with Brooklyn musicians except on non-audio work such as music videos, cover art and styling.*
- **Music videos:** **[FACT](https://creative-commission.com/briefs/brief-9959)** On Creative Commission, the music-video brief platform, commissioners shortlist at most 5 directors from **recent work and a showreel**, then request treatments. The reel is the vetting unit, not a credit list.
- **Photography and design:** **[FACT](https://help.behance.net/hc/en-us/articles/204483494-Guide-Co-owning-And-Crediting)** Behance already supports **co-owners**, who must accept the invitation and get the project on their profile, and **credits** for non-users invited by email. That is a working "mutual" tier inside Adobe's network. **[FACT](https://campaigns.at-edge.com/survey-results/)** Behance is "most important" to only 17% of commercial buyers.
- **Instagram:** **[FACT](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/instagram-collab-posts)** Collab posts, introduced in 2021, now allow up to 5 invited co-authors, with the post appearing on every co-author's grid. *Sources disagree on the exact cap.* Instagram's native co-authorship is the closest thing to a "mutual" credit that creatives already use daily.
- **Influencer and UGC:** **[FACT](https://joinbrands.com/blog/ugc-portfolio-guide/)** UGC buyers hire on content quality, niche fit and brief delivery, "never follower count", from 6–10 portfolio videos posted on marketplaces. *This is vendor guidance, not a survey.* Credits play no role. Prior brand work matters as a sample, not as a record.

## 5. How often anyone looks credits up
- **[FACT](https://help.imdb.com/article/imdbpro/new-features/how-can-i-control-my-known-for-credits/GH8XTYSWKU2P96TA)** **IMDbPro subscriber counts are not public.** I searched and found no figure. Reported price is $19.99/mo or $149.99/yr ([snippet](https://static.hlt.bme.hu/semantics/external/pages/James_Blish/en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb.html), undated). IMDb overall has about 83M registered users, which says nothing about Pro usage.
- **[FACT](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/linkedin-business-highlights-from-microsoft-s-q2-fy25-earnings)** LinkedIn Premium passed **$2B in annual revenue** (Q2 FY25, Jan 2025), and subscriber growth was nearly 50% over two years. LinkedIn guided that **Talent Solutions** (recruiter seats) faced headwinds ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/29/linkedin-passes-2b-in-premium-revenues-in-12-months-with-overall-revenues-up-9-on-the-year)). *Paid search seats on a professional graph do work, but they ride on a graph of about 1B people, not tens of thousands.*
- **[FACT](https://www.bu.edu/com/files/2018/10/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf)** TheLadders 2012 eye-tracking study (30 recruiters): about **6 seconds** per résumé, and **19%** of time on a LinkedIn profile went to the photo. *The study is small and commercially motivated.* **[FACT](https://elementapp.ai/blog/3-seconds-to-screen-candidate-profile-biometric-research-results)** A single-recruiter vendor test found about 3s on a thin profile and up to about 20s on a strong one.
- **[FACT](https://www.musicconnection.com/up-close-muso-ai/)** Muso Pro surfaces daily credit stats from 11 platforms, but **Muso discloses no paying-user count**. **[FACT](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2021/04/27/muso-ai-music-story/)** In 2021 Muso reported 20% month-over-month growth in users, with no absolute number.
- **[INFERENCE]** I found **no public data** on how often hirers in any creative field look up a credit database before booking. The only lookup behavior with real evidence is (a) actor casting checking IMDb, which is anecdotal, and (b) recruiter use of LinkedIn. Both serve fields where a credit is a **gate**: unions, guild minimums and IMDb's STARmeter culture. Fashion, photography and UGC have no such gate.

## Evidence for and against hirers ever using or confirming a credit record

### For
1. **Hirers do check claims when the tool is cheap.** Casting directors cross-check actor résumés against IMDb and catch padding ([Backstage](https://www.backstage.com/advice-for-actors/backstage-experts/why-actors-shouldnt-lie-their-resumes/)). IMDb's verified-versus-self-verified split shows that a tiered-confirmation model is a recognized industry pattern ([IMDb](https://help.imdb.com/article/contribution/filmography-credits/adding-filmography-credits/GAB5B33MBLEG73CT)).
2. **Institutions already confirm work when a formal process asks for it.** O-1B petitions run on industry testimonial letters and tear sheets, and USCIS revokes visas when a letter is faked ([USCIS AAO](https://meritdraft.com/decisions/o-1b-dismissed-fashion-model-2018-03-30.html)). The demand for verifiable creative work history therefore already exists, and fakes get caught.
3. **Issuer-confirmed credits work where the issuer's job is publishing.** Music labels and distributors feed Spotify credits ([MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-rolls-out-expanded-song-credits-previews-new-songdna-and-about-the-song-features-for-premium-users)). Muso validates through rights holders ([Music Connection](https://www.musicconnection.com/up-close-muso-ai/)). Agencies and editors upload credits to Models.com ([help](https://help.models.com/article/61/adding-talent-credits-to-a-new-uploaded-work)). Kavyar makes team credits mandatory at magazine submission ([Kavyar](https://help.kavyar.com/article/p03wnxoixc)).
4. **FWB already holds the roster.** Its registration package staffs hair, makeup, styling, dressers and casting ([FWBK](https://www.fashionweekbrooklyn.com/registration-process)). Davy frames the event as "lending credibility" ([BKReader](https://www.bkreader.com/featured-news/where-brooklyn-at-fashion-week-brooklyn-highlights-hip-hop-culture-in-bk-the-metaverse-and-beyond-6831994)). Unpaid participants are compensated in "exposure" ([BuzzFeed](https://www.buzzfeed.com/hannahmarder/are-fashion-week-models-paid); [Brooklyn Made](https://brooklynmadestore.com/pages/brooklyn-fashion-show-2026)), so a formal credit is a cheap, tangible addition to that exchange. **[INFERENCE]** Event and school issuers have the motive and the data. Brands do not.
5. **Brands vet past work.** 61% of influencer marketers check "prior brand affinities" ([MarketingProfs/ACTIVATE](https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2019/40956/how-marketers-vet-social-media-influencers)), and brands are warned against trusting self-reported metrics. There is latent demand for third-party-confirmed history, at least for creator hiring.

### Against
1. **Hiring runs on people, not records.** 75–82% of fashion and film jobs come through contacts ([Bectu](https://bectu.org.uk/news/three-quarters-of-creative-industries-jobs-go-to-friends-and-connections-reveals-bectus-bectu-survey)). Commercial buyers shortlist from curated portfolio platforms (44%) and their network (24%), with only 7% using open search ([AtEdge](https://campaigns.at-edge.com/survey-results/)). Casting directors ask agents for referrals ([WA AG](https://atg.wa.gov/modeling-agencies-teens)). A referral already carries the verification, so the record adds little.
2. **The vetting unit is the work and the reach, not the credit line.** Commercial buyers look at reels and images (AtEdge; [Creative Commission](https://creative-commission.com/briefs/brief-9959)). Fashion weighs Instagram following, which "affects day rates" ([Glossy](https://www.glossy.co/fashion/you-need-instagram-to-make-it-as-a-model-even-if-youre-kendall-jenner/)). UGC buyers look at sample videos ([JoinBrands](https://joinbrands.com/blog/ugc-portfolio-guide/)). **No source found shows a fashion or photo hirer asking "is this credit verified?"**
3. **Corporate issuers do not confirm voluntarily.** About half of game studios have no credits policy even with an industry standard in place ([IGDA/VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/less-than-half-of-developers-say-their-studio-has-a-credits-policy-new-survey-finds)). Neutral-reference policies cap HR at dates and titles ([SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/in/topics-tools/employment-law-compliance/job-references-can-misleading)). NDAs and embargoes can bar a freelancer from even naming the client ([Freelancermap](https://www.freelancermap.com/blog/can-i-share-nda-protected-work-on-my-portfolio-tips-and-advice/); [iPhotography](https://www.iphotography.com/blog/business-photography-industry-legal-terms-explained/)). **[INFERENCE]** The highest-value credits, brand campaigns, are the least likely to be issued, and they are often the most legally restricted.
4. **Lookup demand is unproven and unpublished.** Neither IMDbPro nor Muso discloses paying users. LinkedIn's $2B Premium business rides on about 1B profiles, and its recruiter seats are under pressure ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/29/linkedin-passes-2b-in-premium-revenues-in-12-months-with-overall-revenues-up-9-on-the-year)). Recruiters spend seconds per profile ([TheLadders](https://www.bu.edu/com/files/2018/10/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf)). IMDb itself treats modeling and commercial credits as self-verified, Pro-only extras ([IMDb](https://help.imdb.com/article/contribution/filmography-credits/adding-filmography-credits/GAB5B33MBLEG73CT)). Even the leading credit database considers this tier marginal.
5. **The top tier is already covered and the bottom tier carries reputational risk.** Models.com credits CFDA, IMG and Parsons-level work ([Models.com](https://models.com/work/parsons-mfa-2024-show)). Hirers who pay for search are mostly hiring from that tier. The uncovered tier of regional weeks and BFA shows is where pay-to-play operators live ([BuzzFeed](https://www.buzzfeed.com/hannahmarder/are-fashion-week-models-paid); [City Press](https://www.news24.com/citypress/trending/caught-in-a-fashion-furore-20160521)), and an "ISSUED" badge from them may read as a negative signal to a serious hirer. **[INFERENCE]**
6. **Credit already has a free, native channel.** Instagram Collab posts give multi-party co-authorship on the grid ([Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/instagram-collab-posts)). Behance has accepted co-ownership ([Behance](https://help.behance.net/hc/en-us/articles/204483494-Guide-Co-owning-And-Crediting)). The outrage creatives voice about credit is about **circulation and licensing** (Quavo, Marie Claire, Tahari), not about proof. A tag also does not substitute for a license ([TFL/Tahari](https://thefashionlaw.com/elie-taharis-reposting-of-photo-on-social-media-is-not-trivial-or-fair-use)).

**Net [INFERENCE]:** Evidence that creatives *want* credit is strong. Evidence that organizers like FWB, schools and indie magazines *can and might* issue credits is moderate, because they hold the rosters and their identity is publishing. Evidence that hirers *would search or pay for* a verified record in fashion, photo or UGC is **essentially absent**. Where verification demand does exist (IMDb in acting, O-1 petitions, influencer fraud vetting), it is tied to a gate or a fraud problem, not to curiosity. If the thesis survives, the buyer is more plausibly the **issuer**, as an organizer tool for rosters, consent, call sheets and published credit pages, or the **creative** facing a gate such as an O-1 visa, an agency application or a grant. The IMDbPro-style search seat is the least supported part of the plan.

### Gaps (not found; do not cite as fact)
- IMDbPro subscriber count; Muso paying users; any survey of fashion or photo hirers on credit-checking frequency.
- FWB per-season participant headcount and designer fee amounts. The brief's "designers pay to show" is not publicly priced.
- Whether FWB currently publishes any participant credits. I found none beyond designer listings and Instagram.
- Any documented case of a brand formally confirming a freelancer's credit on request, outside O-1 testimonial letters.
