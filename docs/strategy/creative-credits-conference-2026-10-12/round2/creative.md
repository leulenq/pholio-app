# Round 2: Creative

## 0. Growth's question, answered honestly
**Who collects names, roles and handles on an NYC test shoot or editorial today?** It is the lead, usually the photographer, sometimes the stylist acting as producer. The team is put together in Instagram DMs and a group chat. Handles get typed in once, at submission. [FACT] Kavyar asks for team credits *while photos upload*, auto-suggests them, requires wardrobe credits, and has the uploader certify they are the copyright holder, which makes the photographer the de facto collector ([Kavyar](https://help.kavyar.com/article/ywh5x5ywbx-how-do-i-submit-my-work-to-publishers)). Paid productions use call sheets in StudioBinder, Milanote or Google Docs. These list crew by department with contacts, and none of the templates I found has a field for handles ([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/photography-call-sheet); [Milanote](https://milanote.com/templates/photography/photoshoot-call-sheet)). **How often:** [GAP] I found no number. My web-search budget ran out before I could reach Reddit. [ASSUMPTION] An active editorial photographer leads about 1–4 shoots a month. Stylists and makeup artists *join* more shoots than they *lead*.

**My concession:** collecting the roster is a 10-minute chore, and Kavyar already sits where the typing happens. My round-1 framing of it as an "admin pain" was overstated. The acute moments are elsewhere: (1) the showroom, where a pull letter or shoot confirmation decides whether I get samples, and [FACT] Flanelle sells pull letters for $16.99 and only to directory members ([Flanelle](https://flanellemag.com/?p=16294)); (2) the repost, where my credit disappears.

## 1. Attack on Candidate A (the Organizer Desk)
A builds a record of my work that is nearly empty. [FACT] FWB has about 40 designer slots a season, about 1,000 credited people a year, and a 26-week gap between seasons (R4 §3; growth R1). [FACT] "Most of the 'weekly shoots' segment has no issuer" (R1 Pattern 4). A stylist ends up with 1–3 issued lines a year, while 95% of what I actually do (tests, editorials, lookbooks, music videos) never appears. That is the Polywork profile with a nonprofit's logo on it. A also routes its fees through people who are already squeezed. Money's "$20 per designer slot" is charged to designers who already pay $250+ to show (money R1). [ASSUMPTION] Organizers will pass that cost on, and in this tier it lands on models as "registration fees." A becomes one more line on the pay-to-walk invoice. Finally, A is Kavyar's own product, and Kavyar "can add 'Published in X' credits in weeks" (competition R1).

## 2. Attacks on named claims
- **Growth:** "15 × 2 × 5 = 150 invites/month … 60% acceptance … viral unit is the invitee who later organizes." Models, assistants and makeup artists almost never organize. Only photographers and some stylists lead shoots. [ASSUMPTION] K is bounded by the share of invitees who lead shoots, maybe 1 in 5, not by acceptance. Your loop works only if the photographer gets something from the sheet that Kavyar does not already give them.
- **Founder:** "Claiming it is not admin." An emailed claim link to a profile with one credit on it is exactly the dormant-profile pattern you cite against The Dots. Your kill metric, "≥70% of FWB registrations through Pholio," measures whether FWB complied, not whether any creative wants this.
- **Money:** the "Evidence Pack at $29" for O-1 and agency applications is charging creatives at a gate to look credible. That is Flanelle's business model. It should be free, or the trust story is gone.
- **Competition:** "Creatives receive credits and curate nothing." Then creatives also get nothing between seasons.

## 4. Revised position
**Changed:**
1. The pull for creatives is the published full-team page plus a shoot confirmation the lead can show a showroom, plus a paste block for Kavyar and Instagram. It is not "saving admin," and I withdraw that claim.
2. I accept A as the seed engine for issued credits. FWB is where the first real pages come from.
3. I adopt trust's labels: "Issued by," "Confirmed by," "Added by." "Verified" applies to a person, never to a credit.
4. Instagram drafting is cut entirely.

**Holding:**
1. Lead creatives can open productions in v1. Without that, the record is dead for 26 weeks a year, and "living" is false.
2. Creatives never pay for anything that touches credibility: evidence packs, confirmations, pull drafts, ranking.
3. Kill test: under 20% of FWB-credited photographers and stylists open a non-FWB production within 45 days means H fails. Then founders should admit A is a tool business and stop calling it a record.

## 5. Question
**Founder and growth:** what does Pholio give the *photographer* on a test shoot that Kavyar's upload screen and a group chat do not? If the answer is nothing, B and H die and A is all that is left.
