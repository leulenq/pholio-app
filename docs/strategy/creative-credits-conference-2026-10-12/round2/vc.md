# Round 2: VC

## 1. Attacking Candidate A (which I helped propose)

The moderator is right. A fixed the buyer problem by shrinking the company until the buyer fit.

**The arithmetic is fatal.** Money priced it honestly at "$20 per designer slot per event with a $250 minimum, so FWB's ~40 slots come to about $800 a season." At that price, $1M ARR takes about 625 FWB-sized events every season. I found no count of recurring regional fashion weeks or BFA shows that comes close [ASSUMPTION].

**A also points at the wrong payer.** My own Round 1 analogy defeats it. The scaled casting-software business, Talent Systems, ran 50,000+ projects and 2M+ auditions in 2021 and earns from subscriptions and service fees [FACT](https://www.castingnetworks.com/redbird-capital-partners-to-acquire-talent-systems-the-industrys-premier-tech-driven-global-casting-marketplace/). Spotlight's estimated ~£10M revenue (unconfirmed) comes from roughly 60k performers paying about £150 a year [FACT, estimate](https://flashesandflames.com/?p=134401). Breakdown Services keeps casting directors free (R2). In casting, the organizer's attention is the scarce side and gets subsidized, while the supply side pays. A charges the scarce side.

**A abandons "living."** FWB's roster excludes photographers (R4 §3), and growth showed that records update twice a year with 26-week gaps in between. That makes A a yearbook. It does not grow between seasons, and Kavyar, which already owns the submission rail, can add a "published in" credit page in weeks (competition).

## 2. Attacks on named delegates

- **Competition:** "Kavyar removed free publisher accounts … [BET] Small issuers will adopt a cheap tool." I read the price hike the other way. Publishers pay $99–499 a month because Kavyar sends them *inbound submissions*. Its site gets an estimated ~329k visits a month (Semrush third-party estimate, [FACT that estimate exists](https://ko.semrush.com/website/magcloud.com/competitors)). The form is free to copy, so Kavyar's moat is the network of submitters. A Pholio intake form with no submitters gives a publisher nothing to switch for.
- **Founder:** "≥1 organizer agrees to pay by month 6." That bar is far too low. One payer in six months is a favor, not a market. I will only accept a test that requires organizers *outside the founders' network*.
- **Creative:** "invited people confirm with one tap and no account." That is good UX and a dead network. If accepting a team sheet creates no persistent identity, nothing compounds: no graph, no profile, no second-use trigger.
- **Hirer / devil (C):** "Money comes later from agency and casting workspace seats." Identity is a commodity. LinkedIn has 100M+ verified members through CLEAR and Persona, and Persona costs about $1 a check (R1 §8, R3). The only durable part of C is capture-dated digitals, a model-only feature that Meta Verified or Casting Networks could ship in one quarter.

## 4. Revised position

**Changed:** A is no longer the business. It is the trust anchor and the launch event. FWB-issued show pages give B its first credible records and its first few hundred accounts. **Kept:** search seats stay dead.

**Revenue moves to the supply side, at a gate, never as a fee to be seen.** The precedents that work are Spotlight at £205/yr, Kavyar PRO/ELITE at $14 and $32, and Flanelle's $16.99 pull letters (creative R1). Pholio Pro sells the *outputs* of a team sheet: Kavyar-ready submission packages, pull-letter drafts, embargo vault, issuer-attested evidence packs for agency, O-1B and grant applications [BET]. Pro never sells ranking, badges or reach (money's Rule zero). A small organizer fee stays as a secondary line.

**Accounts must persist.** The first accept is one tap, and the credit is held in escrow until the person claims a profile. The claim is the conversion event.

**Venture kill test, 90 days after FWB [BET]:**
1. Out of 30 seeded NYC photographers and stylists, at least 12 create a second team sheet without being prompted.
2. At least 15% of invitees organize their own sheet within 60 days.
3. At least 3% of claimed creatives pay for Pro.

If test 1 fails, none of A, B or C is venture-scale. The honest move then is to run A as a small, profitable tool for organizers and not raise money.
