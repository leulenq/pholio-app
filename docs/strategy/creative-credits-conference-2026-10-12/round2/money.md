## 1. Attacking Candidate A, starting with my own Show Desk

I proposed "$20 per designer slot per event, $250 minimum." Here is that price against the realistic NYC organizer market.

**Organizers named in the research**
- FWB: about 40 slots a season, twice a year [FACT](https://www.fashionweekbrooklyn.com/schedule).
- Fashion Week Queens: 6–7 designers, same operator [FACT](https://qns.com/2026/07/queens-center-launch-fashion-week-queens/).
- Harlem FW [FACT](https://edgexpo.com/2017/02/21/edge-fast-fact-fashion-week-designer-costs).
- Runway 7 and Art Hearts [FACT](https://www.buzzfeed.com/hannahmarder/are-fashion-week-models-paid).
- School shows: Pratt (30 seniors) and FIT (80 graduates), once a year each [FACT](https://www.pratt.edu/news/runway-ready-pratt-fashion-seniors-shine-at-powerhouse-arts/), [FACT](https://news.fitnyc.edu/2026/05/11/presenting-the-next-generation-of-designers-at-the-bfa-runway-show/).
- Parsons runs through IMG, which already publishes credits on Models.com, so it is not reachable.

Indie magazines are the generous case. Kavyar now charges publishers $99–499/mo [FACT](https://help.kavyar.com/article/a6pzp4ugix-publisher-plans-are-changing-what-you-need-to-know). If 40 NYC magazines paid $99/mo [ASSUMPTION on count], that adds $47k.

**The honest ceiling for Candidate A in NYC is under $100k ARR. Nationally, at maybe 200 organizers × $1,500, it is about $300k [ASSUMPTION].** That is a tool business, and it is seasonal too: an organizer that buys twice a year has twice a year to churn. I said Show Desk is where the money comes first. I was wrong: it is where the *credibility* comes first, and its revenue is a rounding error.

A also fails the money test on frequency. In my round 1 I attacked the Pro tier with Linktree, and the same reasoning applies to A's buyer: the organizer's spend is tiny because the event is tiny. FWB designers get "exposure" and no stipend [FACT](https://brooklynmadestore.com/pages/brooklyn-fashion-show-2026). An ecosystem that pays its own labor in exposure will not pay software rent at a venture scale.

## 2. Attacking named delegates

**competition: "5–10 indie magazines pushed off Kavyar's free tier."** This is a selection trap. The magazines that left when Kavyar went from free to $99/mo are the ones who chose not to pay $99. Kavyar's price rise didn't create a market of payers for us. It sorted the payers into Kavyar and handed us the rest.

**vc: "At least 5 organizers outside the founders' network pay at least $1,500/season."** Passing that test proves $15k ARR. The VC's own kill criterion can't tell a venture apart from a hobby, because both outcomes would be "a tool business." There is a second problem with the VC's "per-designer casting room add-on that the organizer bundles into the show fees designers already pay." It ties Pholio's price to the pay-to-show fee, the most scam-adjacent number in this industry (R3 §7). When a designer gets fleeced by an organizer, our line item sits inside the invoice.

**hirer: "Hirer side (free, always)."** If Candidate C is right, the hirer is the only party with both a budget and the pain. Brands already pay HypeAuditor and Modash $99–399/mo per seat to verify authenticity [FACT](https://modash.io/modash-vs-hypeauditor). "Free always" throws away the one revenue line with evidence behind it. Hirers should never pay to *search*, but agencies drowning in applications should pay for a *filtered intake*.

## 4. Revised position (changed)

**What I dropped:** per-slot Show Desk pricing as revenue line #1. What survives is a free organizer lane that serves as the ISSUED source and acquisition pulse.

**Hybrid: one object, the Production.**
- Anyone who leads work creates one: a photographer, stylist or show organizer.
- Members accept roles by link.
- Organizers who pass vetting publish ISSUED credits. Everyone else publishes "Confirmed by the team."

**Who pays, ranked by frequency:**
1. **The lead creative, from month 3, at $12/mo** [BET]. This buys unlimited productions, Kavyar-ready submission packs, embargo vault, shoot-confirmation and pull-letter *drafts* (unsigned and unsent by Pholio, carrying no obligation), and the archive. The benchmark is Kavyar PRO at $14. Members, credits and the record are free forever.
   - Ceiling arithmetic [ASSUMPTION]: 2,000 paying NYC leads × $144 = $288k. That is already 20× the organizer line, and the product is not tied to one city's event calendar.
2. **Agencies, from month 9, at $49–99/mo per workspace** [BET]. This buys a filtered intake: ID-matched, dated digitals plus submission snapshots, Candidate C's strongest piece. They pay by card, with no procurement. Models.com agency listings prove agencies pay for presence [FACT](https://models.com/about/listings/modeling/).
3. **Organizers: free for the first 2 seasons, then a flat $300/season** [BET]. The point of this line is retention, not revenue.

**Never:** paid ranking, paid verification, or paid issuer status. Search seats stay dead.

**The gate on all of this:** creative's kill test, at least 20% of FWB leads starting a non-FWB production within 45 days. If it fails, B's frequency was never real, and the money line falls back to agency intake (C).
