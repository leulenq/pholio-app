# Who is reading, and what state they are in

> **Shared layer.** An identical copy lives at `pholio-site/.claude/skills/pholio-site-language/references/audience.md`.
> Edit both together; divergence between the copies is a defect.
Copy decisions here start from evidence about real readers, not personas
invented in a workshop. Sources: r/modeling archive (upvote counts noted),
Model Alliance, FTC guidance, agency FAQ pages, scout interviews, working
models' blogs, trade press (research pass, August 2026).

---

## 1. The aspirant (primary talent-facing audience)

**Who.** Skews 18 to 27 on application surfaces (the industry's editorial
entry window is younger, which is exactly why parents are a co-audience).
Plus returners (post-maternity), 50+ hopefuls, and low-income applicants for
whom every fee is a wall. Dominant posture: "I've been told my whole life I
should model, and I'm finally letting myself try", usually with pre-emptive
apology.

**Their entire mental map is four questions** (an r/modeling moderator's own
summary of the sub): can I make it / do I have the look; what agencies are
in my city; how do I get started; is this a scam.

**The questions, verbatim:**
- "Hope for me in modeling? I'm 22 so I feel like my time has already
  passed... Bust-32 Waist-22 Hips-34" (1,466 upvotes)
- "i'm 5'10 trying to get into modeling, am i cooked?"
- "Who would want me? Am I too old to start?"
- "How much can i get away with rounding up my height?"
- "what is a model video" (asked by someone Wilhelmina had already replied
  to; the vocabulary gap is real even mid-funnel)
- "i'm from a lower income family so i can't afford professional
  photographers"
- "I'm unsure in what agencies I should submit my pictures to due to the
  many scams."

Note the learned behavior: aspirants volunteer their measurements unprompted
in post titles. They have absorbed that the body is the resume before anyone
asks. Write near stats with plain restraint.

**Where they get answers today:** r/modeling (highest-trust genre by far:
a successful peer showing the exact materials that worked: "Digitals that
just got me signed (NYC)", 517 upvotes), TikTok scout content, YouTube
coaches, $1k "bootcamps", agency FAQ pages. The peer-template register
(exact materials, exact specs, real numbers) is the voice they already
trust.

**Implications.**
- Answer the measurable questions with real numbers, divisions, and
  exceptions. "5'8" to 5'11" is the editorial norm; commercial and e-comm
  care less" beats both hype and evasion.
- The single most relieving sentence available to a low-income aspirant is
  the industry's own: professional photos are not necessary; agencies
  prefer plain phone digitals. Say it wherever digitals are asked for.
- Coach inside the form at the moment of anxiety (what to wear, how to
  measure, what a walk video is), not in a seminar voice on a landing page.
- Never flatter. Respect reads as assuming the reader is serious, informed,
  and able to handle real odds.

## 2. The rejection economy (the emotional physics of the funnel)

- Silence is the system's default output and often published policy ("we
  will only contact those who have been shortlisted", Storm; "we cannot
  respond to everyone" in five unrelated agencies' FAQs). Roughly 1 in 100
  applications gets a callback. Anxiety spikes within days, not weeks.
- Rejection continues after signing ("Signed to an agency but not getting
  booked", 645 upvotes). The signed-but-idle model is a real, unserved
  audience state.
- "Look" feedback arrives in centimeters and lands as identity ("her hips
  were two centimeters too wide"). The measuring tape is the single most
  emotionally loaded object in the industry.
- The veteran reframe that works is **fit, not verdict**: "Every agent is
  looking for something specific... it has nothing to do with you not being
  beautiful enough." The industry's own folklore: Gisele was rejected 42
  times.

**Implications.**
- A platform in the middle may promise only inputs (materials right,
  correctly targeted) and information (what silence means, when to
  re-apply). Naming the silence, with the industry's real numbers and the
  6-month re-apply convention, is the largest authority move available.
- Every rejection and status surface reframes structurally (board space,
  market, timing), never reads into silence, never cheerleads. "Don't give
  up on your dreams!" is the coach-sell voice; a decision is a sentence,
  not a data readout and not a pep talk.
- What the shipped email family gets right and copy should preserve as
  posture (not as sacred strings): "That's not a no." for kept-on-file;
  "They don't give a reason, and there isn't one to read into it." for a
  pass; "A real agency will wait." near contracts.

## 3. Parents and guardians (co-audience for minors, vetters for teens)

- Their anxieties: predators, cost, legitimacy, photos of the child online,
  contracts. The scouting story that read as legitimate to a parent: the
  IMG scout "came up to me instead of her, gave a business card and left it
  at that."
- Reputable child-agency communication is structured entirely around
  **process**: who is present, who pays whom, what happens to photos, how
  school is handled, chaperones, licences, deletion rights.
- What reassures: named humans with verifiable emails, process detail,
  explicit "no fees, ever, and here's why", "take your time / take it to a
  lawyer". What raises flags: flattery about the child, urgency, "exposure"
  and "opportunity" as nouns, fees reframed as investment, NDA-gated names.

**Implications.** Address the parent directly and first on any minor-adjacent
surface, in process language. Pholio's real mechanisms are the copy: minor
profiles never publicly exposable, submissions redacted to an age band,
contact routed through Pholio, guardian authorization recorded before
anything is shown or sent. Say "guardian" in product copy.

## 4. The working model

- Freelancer reality: multiple agencies, chasing payments ("most agencies
  do not pay their models unless the models ask", Model Alliance founder),
  rate chaos, TFP pressure, guarantee debt abroad, agent opacity ("My MA is
  ignoring me"), aging across divisions.
- They have seen Uber-for-models before and watched it fail or turn
  predatory (Swipecast, Ubooker, Snapcast at 2.1 stars, Model Mayhem's
  safety reputation). They vet even fashion weeks through the BBB.
- What they demonstrably value: speed and certainty of payment, verifiable
  vetting, control, no one taking a cut of nothing. What triggers cynicism:
  "revolutionize", "democratize", any implication the platform replaces the
  agent relationship they still want.

**Implications.** For working models, lead with money, time, control, and
record-keeping (receipts, usage terms, what was sent where, a book that
stays current without cost). No inspiration register. Pholio does not
handle payments; never imply it does.

## 5. The agency side (bookers, scouts, owners)

- Evaluation is nearly instantaneous and mechanical: height and photos
  first, then Instagram. ~1% callback rates; hundreds of applications a
  week; intake is a cost center they have publicly surrendered on.
- Instant disqualifiers they publish: filters or visible editing, heavy
  makeup in digitals, hats/sunglasses, missing stats, missing full-length.
  They prefer unedited phone digitals over retouched professional photos.
- What earns a third-party platform their respect: submissions in exactly
  the format they ask for, pre-verified stats, compliance handled, zero
  promises made to talent on the agency's behalf, a clean exit ramp (CSV)
  so it never becomes a second system of record. What gets it dismissed:
  hype language to talent, retouched imagery, unfiltered volume.
- Their self-interested reason to cooperate: impersonation defense. Fake
  domains actively spoof major agencies; "the only real way to apply to us"
  is a link an agency wants to publish.

**Implications.** Agency-facing copy is operational, dense, unglossed, and
promise-free. The strongest agency-facing sentences are about what Pholio
will not do: never charge them, never rank talent by payment, never make
promises to applicants in their name, never trap their data.

## 6. Trust mechanics for this population specifically

They are pre-warned and actively verifying: they crowd-vet agency names on
Reddit, check BBB, reverse-check DM scouts, inspect email domains for
one-letter-off spoofs, check whether roster models claim the agency back.

- The one universal heuristic, taught by every safety source: **a
  legitimate agency makes money when you make money; paying up front to get
  work is always the scam signature.**
- Instant scam pattern-matches: "you have the look" (unsolicited flattery),
  guarantees, urgency, implausible pay, fees as investment, NDA-gated
  clients, recruitment via DM.
- **"Free" is itself scrutinized**: predatory funnels start free and invoice
  at $4,000 later. State where the money is ("Pholio's only revenue is one
  optional subscription that changes nothing an agency sees") or the warned
  reader supplies a sinister answer.
- Invite the check instead of resisting it: named team, real mechanisms,
  agencies listed by name with dated, sourced requirement entries, "their
  own page is the authority if the two disagree."

## 7. Reading order for a mixed surface

Most marketing surfaces are read by all five audiences at once. Priority:
never say to talent what would embarrass Pholio in front of a booker, and
never say to agencies what would frighten a parent. The register that
satisfies all five is the same one: mechanism-true, deflationary, specific,
promise-free. When a surface cannot satisfy them all, split the surface
(that is an IA decision, not a wording one; see judgment.md).
