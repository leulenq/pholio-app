# Round 1, Growth/Distribution: opening position

## 1. Verdict
A credit record is the residue of a loop, not the loop: as written the thesis has no reason for anyone to come back next week, so FWB gives us one spike of a few hundred dormant profiles and then nothing.

## 2. Three strongest arguments

**A. Records that people have to curate never spread. Records that come out of a job people already do sometimes win.** [FACT] R1 Pattern 1: IMDb rides on contractual screen credits and music credits on royalty registration. Polywork, Clowdy, Credictive and The Dots asked people to curate a record for its own sake, and failed. R1 §7: Dots members "did not need to return." [INFERENCE] The thesis has no workflow underneath it, only a profile of drafted credits. That is The Dots with a nicer tier label.

**B. FWB is a pulse, not a channel.** [FACT] R4 §3: about 40 designer slots a season, two seasons a year plus a 6–7-designer Queens week. R4 estimates a ceiling of about 1,000 credited people a year and calls it "a seed, not a network." [INFERENCE] There are about 26 weeks between seasons, so a model who walked in October gets nothing from Pholio until April. We would mistake a launch spike for traction. FWB is good for putting a credible issuer and a few hundred real profiles live at once. It cannot carry retention.

**C. The automatic-drafting engine mostly doesn't exist for the people we want.** [FACT] Meta retired the Instagram Basic Display API on Dec 4, 2024. The remaining API covers only Business and Creator accounts and has no methods for personal accounts ([Meta](https://developers.secure.facebook.com/docs/instagram-basic-display-api/overview), [Zapier](https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/32429170578317-Instagram-app-deprecation-on-Dec-4-2024)). The `tags` edge returns only media where *the connected professional account* was tagged, and never private media ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/tags)). [ASSUMPTION] Many early-career models and HMU artists run personal accounts, and caption-only credits are invisible to the API. Drafting covers some professionals, behind an OAuth step that costs conversion.

## 3. The claim most wrong
**"Credits are drafted from work creatives already post, so the record stays current without the creative maintaining it."**
Mechanically it fails (see C). Strategically it is anti-retention by design: a product that needs nothing from the user gives the user no reason to open it. Maintenance-free plus no hirer traffic (R4: no source shows a fashion or photo hirer asking "is this credit verified?") equals a profile nobody visits or updates, the shape of every dead identity network in R1 §10. Growth needs the opposite: Pholio sits *before* the work, where the team gets assembled. Credits fall out at the end.

## 4. Proposed revision: Pholio is the team sheet for a creative production; the credit is what it prints.
**Core object.** A *production* (show, shoot, editorial, video). The organizer creates it and adds the team by role. Members accept by link and confirm name, role, handle and consent. After the work runs, the organizer links it and taps "Publish credits." Every member gets a credit page and a share card naming the whole team by role, with no 6-person collab cap ([FACT] R2 §3). There is also a one-tap **caption and submission credit block** for Kavyar ([FACT] Kavyar requires every photo to be "properly credited at the time of submission" with the whole team's handles, R4 §2). The existing events module, consent records and snapshots cover most of this.

**Why it loops.** Every production is an invite blast with a built-in reason to accept: you are on this shoot. [BET] The viral unit is the invitee who later *organizes* a production; stylists and photographers do that repeatedly.

**Second-use trigger.** For organizers it is the next shoot, weekly or monthly. For members it is (a) getting added to someone else's next production, and (b) "your credit is live," when the organizer publishes the work. The trigger is not search traffic and not Pro.

**Cold start and the first 100 users (numbers to attack).**
- *Weeks 0–4, seed producers:* hand-recruit 15 NYC photographers and stylists who submit to indie magazines. Source them from Kavyar-published NYC editorials and the founders' network. [ASSUMPTION] Each runs about 2 shoots a month with about 5 other people. 15 × 2 × 5 = 150 invites a month. At 60% acceptance [ASSUMPTION] that is about 90 accounts a month, made of real teams rather than random signups. Founders do concierge entry of the first productions.
- *FWB, next season:* Pholio replaces the model and crew Google Forms for free, and designers pick from the existing two-stage selection. After each show FWB publishes ISSUED credits: a public show page for designers' press kits and a story card for every participant. Expect a few hundred registrations ([ASSUMPTION]; no headcount is published).
- *Bridge:* recruit the FWB cohort's HMU leads, stylists and photographers, who shoot between seasons, as producers 16–30.

**Kill criteria.** North star: productions per week created by non-founders. Under 5 of 15 seed producers creating a *second* production unprompted within 30 days kills the wedge. Under 10% of invitees organizing their own within 60 days means K is too low: Pholio is an event tool, not a network. The public record gets built only after both pass.

**Cut:** search seats as a growth driver (R4: "the least supported part of the plan"), IG auto-drafting as the engine (it becomes optional enrichment), and the school, magazine and brand issuer sequence until the producer loop is proven.

## 5. My question for the room
**Creative and hirer:** on an NYC test shoot or editorial, who collects the team's names, roles, handles and releases today, with what tool, and how often each month? If nobody does it repeatedly and finds it painful, my loop has no engine.
