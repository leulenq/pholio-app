# Round 4 — Founder/product: choose opportunity intake, provisionally

**[BET] My one core thesis:** Pholio helps creative organizers select and confirm people from applications made with reusable portfolios. Credits are an optional output of completed participation. They are not the acquisition promise, the safety promise, or the business being tested. I withdraw my Production Record thesis as the next thing to build.

## The fight

**VC: your opportunity paywall manufactures revenue before proving opportunity supply.** Your proposed $12 offer sells applications to “the next 10 calls” that do not yet exist. [ASSUMPTION] Applicants might pay because they trust the founders or believe paid access improves their prospects, even if it changes neither selection nor repeat use. Your trial cannot separate those effects. Incumbents charging performers does not establish that FWB plus two test shoots can charge anyone. [BET] Applications stay free during validation; organizers must demonstrate willingness to buy intake. If nobody pays, I will not rescue the business by charging hopeful applicants for a scarce feed.

**Money: your proposed release collection violates the brief.** [FACT: conference constraint] Pholio must not own contracts or contracting. A model release is a rights agreement; adding release capture to manufacture lead willingness to pay crosses that boundary. [BET] Retain product permissions such as agreement to display a name; leave production releases, employment terms, invoicing and disputes entirely in the organizer's existing systems. Also withdraw your “$1–3M ARR good small business” verdict: assumed customers multiplied by assumed prices is a scenario, not a forecast. “Month 9” agency billing is similarly invented sequencing.

**Creative: a collaborator list does not demonstrate a safe photographer.** [ASSUMPTION] A model might contact previous collaborators using it. That is a useful experiment, but collusive or abusive photographers can have real productions and consenting references. [BET] Do not sell this as safety verification or infer good conduct from participation. Ask models to demonstrate their last actual vetting decision, rather than accepting “I would use this” answers.

**Hirer: your private proof link does not dissolve an NDA.** [ASSUMPTION] Role, month and teammates may identify a project despite suppressing the client. [BET] No embargoed-work sharing feature until an actual user shows a permitted use case; private storage is not permission to disclose. Your claim that an expiring link proves someone attended is also too strong: it records assertions, not attendance.

**Growth, and my own earlier position: “once a month” is not a measured base rate.** [FACT: evidentiary correction] A photographer's advice or example is not a representative frequency study. Replacing leads with members removes one bottleneck but adds requests, confirmation and disputes. [ASSUMPTION] The motivated assistant may want recognition without wanting another publishing workflow. We cannot multiply five presumed writers by a presumed cadence and call that a network.

## Two decisive primary-source checks, plus a competitive correction

[FACT] FWB describes application review, approval, registration, coordinated models, beauty teams and backstage staffing on its own [registration page](https://www.fashionweekbrooklyn.com/registration). That establishes an existing selection workflow. It does **not** establish dissatisfaction, purchasing intent, complete individual rosters, or attendance verification. My Round 1 inference that FWB already holds every credit was unjustified.

[FACT] Kavyar requires team credits and explicitly says editing a credit updates the collaborator's profile; it describes shared portfolios in its [credit documentation](https://help.kavyar.com/article/jzdbs3f6zg-how-to-add-team-credits-to-a-photo). My Round 3 claim that Kavyar never accumulates work on a person's page was false. Cross-production identity alone is not differentiation.

[FACT] Kavyar also already supports jobs, including non-premium posting in NYC, LA and Chicago, and advertises paid applicant-management features in its [jobs documentation](https://help.kavyar.com/article/2mywaf3zfk-how-to-post-a-job). Therefore a creative opportunity board is not an unexplored escape hatch. [BET] Test FWB's actual coordination needs, rather than build a general board.

## Trigger, user, reader, payer, falsifier

[BET] Trigger: a real organizer opens a dated casting and must review applicants, share selections with designers, request acceptance and reconcile the final roster. User/reader: the casting coordinator and authorized designer reviewing the submitted portfolio snapshot. Creative: applies once, receives an explicit outcome, reuses the portfolio for another actual call. Payer: the organizer buying review and coordination tools, through Pholio's own subscription; adoption requires no changes to procurement or production contracts.

[BET] Run one real casting by concierge; observe the current workflow first. Measure coordinator effort and errors, not profile claims. Offer a stated price before operating the pilot. Strongest falsifier: after successfully completing it, FWB declines paid reuse **and** two independent organizers decline the same priced next-casting offer because Forms plus existing tools suffice. Then reject this thesis rather than bolt on releases, paywalls or a credit network. Success warrants another intake experiment; it does not establish venture scale or a universal creative identity graph.
