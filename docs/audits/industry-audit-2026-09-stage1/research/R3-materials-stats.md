Scope: comp cards, digitals/polaroids, the book, tearsheets, tests, measurements/stats, and
"digital model ID"/wallet-pass precedents. Evidence gathered 2026-09-03 via WebSearch/WebFetch.
No Pholio code, docs or skill files were opened.

- **[P-agency]** live agency submission / board / profile page

## 1. Summary of the mental model

A working model does not have "a portfolio" in the software sense. They have **four distinct
objects with four different jobs**, and practitioners keep them rigorously separate. Confusing
any two of them is the fastest way for a product to read as built by someone who has never sat
in an agency office.

**1. Digitals (a.k.a. polaroids / polas / digis).** The *truth document*. Unretouched, un-styled,
no makeup, plain wall, natural light, fitted clothes, neutral face, hair back. Their entire
purpose is to show what the person actually looks like *right now*, stripped of photography.
They are a **dated, perishable, disposable set** — replaced every few months and whenever the
look changes. They are the primary artefact of the *submission* and of the *ongoing agency
relationship* (a booker asks for "fresh digitals" before a client package). They are explicitly
**not portfolio images** and never appear in the book. Agencies ask for 3–6 of them and name the
frames.

**2. The book (portfolio).** The *persuasion document*. Curated, professional, printed and bound
(and now also a web board). Contains tests, editorials/tearsheets, campaigns. It is small on
purpose — roughly 6–20 images, most guidance clustering at 10–15 — and it is **edited down**,
not accumulated. A model "builds a book"; an agency "puts a book together" for a new face. Putting
digitals or selfies or runway snaps in the book is a named amateur error.

**3. The comp card (composite / zed card / Z card / sedcard / setcard).** The *leave-behind*.
A single physical card, 5.5×8.5 in (US) or A5 (Europe), one hero image + name on the front,
3–5 images plus a **stats block** and an **agency contact block** on the back. It is a
*derivative* of the book, not a container: the agency selects from the book to make the card.
It is the artefact that carries the *representation relationship* on its face — the agency logo
and the agency's phone number are what make it a comp card rather than a flyer.

**4. The stats / measurements.** The *specification*. A short, ordered, conventional list —
height first, always — used by clients to decide whether the person fits the sample. Stats are
displayed with the model's images everywhere (board profile, comp card back, package PDF) and
are expected to be *true today*. They are dual-unit at the international level, single-unit in
whichever unit the local market thinks in, and they conspicuously **omit weight and (for adult
fashion) age**.

Two cross-cutting rules that a product must encode:

- **Represented ≠ freelance.** A represented model's card, book and profile carry the *agency's*
  contact details, never the model's own phone or email. A freelance/commercial actor's card
  carries their own. Printers who serve freelancers say "include your cell phone number"; agency
  wikis say the opposite in as many words. Getting this wrong is not a styling error, it is a
  safety and business-model error.
- **Truthfulness is the point of digitals and stats.** Everything in the industry's language
  about these two objects is about *not being misled*: no filters, no retouching, no old photos,
  no estimated measurements. AI-generated or AI-altered imagery lands directly on this nerve, and
  as of 2025–26 it is now also a legal question in New York.

## 3. Workflow / state model for materials

How practitioners describe the lifecycle. Actions, reversibility, obligation.

**Stage 0 — Submission (unrepresented → agency).**
The applicant produces digitals themselves, on a phone, and supplies stats. Universal across the
9 agency application pages sampled: no professional photos required, no fee. Heffner: "Phone
photos are accepted" **[P-agency]**. RULE: "You don't need a portfolio or any modeling experience
to apply… Applying is completely free" **[P-agency]**. IMM: "There are no restrictions on
measurements, including height and clothing size" **[P-agency]** (that is a commercial/diverse
agency; fashion boards do gate on height — see §6).
Obligates nothing. Silence is the normal outcome: PRIM signs "no more than six new talent per
year"; IMM tells applicants "If you do not hear back from us, you are welcome to reapply in
6 months' time"; Heffner "only respond to those they are considering" **[P-agency ×3]**.

**Stage 1 — Meeting / open call.** Agency takes its *own* digitals in-house. Premier: polaroid
images taken by in-house photographers *after* being invited in **[P-agency]**. This is the
moment stated measurements are checked against reality — hence the repeated warning that
"misrepresenting your physical attributes can result in not being signed after an in-person
meeting" **[S]**. BFMA constrains it: measuring is done "with privacy, discretion and respect",
and it is "inappropriate to measure any young person under the age 18 except for their height"
**[P-body]**.

**Stage 2 — Development / tests.** Agency arranges test shoots to build the book. The economics
are a known sore point: the agency commonly fronts the cost and recoups from future earnings
**[S ×2]**. Distinct from TFP, which the model arranges alone and the agency is not party to.
Reversible in the sense that weak test images simply never enter the book.

**Stage 3 — The book is assembled and edited.** Curation, not accumulation. New faces start with
6–10 images; developed books run 10–20 **[S ×3]**. Tearsheets enter as they are earned, and even
then conditionally — "only include them in your book if they make you look amazing, not just to
brag about work you have done" **[S]**. Images leave the book as better ones arrive. This is a
*replace* operation, not an *append* operation.

**Stage 4 — Comp card is cut from the book.** "When working for a modeling agency, usually the
comp card is made by the agency itself, and the agency selects the best photos" **[S]**. The card
is a *published version* of the model: it is reprinted when the look or the book changes
materially, and old cards go stale in casting directors' drawers. The stats block on the card and
the stats on the agency board should agree.

**Stage 5 — Ongoing: digitals refresh.** Signed models re-shoot digitals "every few months, or if
there are major changes to their look such as a change in hair colour/style, tattoos, weight
loss/gain" **[S ×2]**. Common recency guidance is **within ~3 months**, with 3–6 months treated
as stale **[S ×3]**. I found **no agency page in the primary sample that states a numeric
recency rule**; agency pages say "current"/"natural state" and leave the number implicit. That is
an honest gap: the 3-month figure is a coaching-source convention, not a published agency rule.

**Stage 6 — Packaging out to clients.** The agent assembles a *package* (selected talent, images,
stats) rather than sending the whole board. Syngency: "an intuitive, drag-and-drop interface lets
them create the perfect selection of talent" **[P-vendor]**. The book/card/digitals all feed this;
the package is ephemeral and per-brief.

**Reversibility notes practitioners assume:** digitals are freely deleted and replaced; book
images are removed without ceremony; a printed comp card is *not* revisable — it is reprinted,
which is why "digital comp card" (a PDF/link) is attractive and why agencies keep the card
generation inside their own system.

### 4.2 Comp card layout

- **Front:** one image + the model's name. "One full-bleed hero image + name" **[P-print]**;
  "The front features a single headshot with the model/actor's name" **[P-print]**.
  Name typeface guidance is deliberately plain (Arial/Times, 12–14 pt) — the card is not a
  branding exercise **[P-print]**.
- **Back:** **3–5 images** is the dominant convention (4 modal). "three to five images that show
  variety" — Fairway **[P-print]**; "3–5 images showing range (commercial, editorial, full-length,
  profile)" — compcard.com **[P-print]**; "Between 3-5 photographs (4 is standard)" — Joe Edelman
  **[S]**. German practice runs higher: sedcard24's template allows "up to nine additional photos"
  and recommends "at least 3-4 pictures, as multifaceted as possible" **[P-print]**; casting.de says
  "mindestens 4-8 Fotografien" **[S/P-agency]**. So: **US/UK 3–5 on the back; DACH 4–8** is a real
  regional split, weakly evidenced (2 sources per side).
- **Back also carries:** the stats block and the contact block.
- Some printers put 3–4 images on the *front* too (NextDayFlyers describes front as "3 or 4
  images") — a minority/print-shop framing, not the agency convention.

### 4.3 Contact block — the represented/freelance fork

This is the highest-signal convention I found and it is stated most bluntly by agencies:

> "die Kontakte über die Modelagentur unterhalten und entsprechend die Kontaktdaten der
> Modelagentur auf der Sedcard vermerkt werden" — all enquiries route through the agency, and
> accordingly the **agency's** contact data is what is printed on the sedcard.
> — i-models.de **[P-agency]**

Against that, printers serving freelancers and actors say the opposite:

> "include your cell phone number, email address, and possibly your website"
> — Fairway Printing **[P-print]**

And the difference is named explicitly in secondary guidance: "Agency Comp Cards often include
the agency's logo and standardized format, while Freelance Comp Cards offer more flexibility"
**[S]**.

Safety carve-out, stated by a printer: "**Never a home address or a child's personal phone
number**" **[P-print]**.

**Product implication:** a comp card object needs a representation-aware contact block with three
states — agency block (logo + agency phone/email, model's own contact suppressed), freelance block
(model's own contact), and minor (guardian/agency only, never the child's direct line).

### 4.4 Stats block — canonical order

Two independent agency board pages give the cleanest evidence, because these are the labels a
booker actually reads.

**Women — Wilhelmina New York, Women/Main [P-agency]** (verbatim label order):
`Height · Bust · Bra · Waist · Hip · Shoe · Hair · Eyes` (+ `Dress` on some profiles)
Example rendering: `Height 5' 9''`, `bust 30''`, `bra 30A`, `waist 24''`, `hip 34''`, `shoe 10`.
Note: **inches only**, US market. **No weight. No age.**

**Men — Wilhelmina New York, Men/Main [P-agency]**:
`Height · Chest · Waist · Suit · Collar · Inseam · Shoe · Hair · Eyes`
Example: `Height 6' 2''`, `Chest 38''`, `Waist 32''`, `Suit 40L`, `Collar 15½''`,
`Inseam 34''`, `Shoe 12`. **No weight.**

**Women — Models 1, London [P-agency]** (verbatim, dual unit, **cm leads**):
`Height 177.5 CM/5' 10'' · Bust 71 CM/28'' · Waist 57 CM/22½'' · Hips 84 CM/33'' · Shoe 6 UK / 39 EU`
Plus eye colour. **No weight, no dress size, no bra/cup.**

Corroborating conventions from printers and guides:
- Women (comp card): "hair color, eye color, bust, waist, hips, dress size, and shoe size" — Fairway
  **[P-print]**; "height, bust/chest, waist, hips, shoe, hair, eyes" — compcard.com **[P-print]**.
- Men (comp card): "hair color, eye color, height, suit or chest size, waist, inseam, and shoe size"
  — Fairway **[P-print]**; same order corroborated **[S ×2]**.
- Germany (Sedcard): `Name · Größe · Maße (Brust/Taille/Hüfte) · Konfektionsgröße · Schuhgröße ·
  Haarfarbe · Augenfarbe`, plus **Cup-Größe** for women and **Kragenweite** for men —
  i-models.de **[P-agency]** + casting.de **[S/P-agency]**.

**Synthesis of the canonical order:**
- Women: **Height → Bust (→ Bra/Cup) → Waist → Hips (→ Dress) → Shoe → Hair → Eyes**
- Men: **Height → Chest (→ Suit) → Waist (→ Collar) → Inseam → Shoe → Hair → Eyes**
- Height is *always first*. Hair/eyes are *always last*. B–W–H are *always contiguous and in that
  order* — "will always be interpreted in the bust-waist-hip order" **[S]**.

### 4.5 Formatting patterns actually seen

```
Height 5' 9''            (US board, inches, no cm)
Height 177.5 CM/5' 10''  (UK board, cm first, imperial second)
bust 30''  waist 24''  hip 34''
34-26-36                 (hyphenated triple, B-W-H; used in prose/compact contexts)
bra 30A                  (band+cup as one token)
Suit 40L                 (numeric + R/L/S drop)
Collar 15½''             (vulgar fractions, not decimals)
Shoe 6 UK / 39 EU        (UK board, dual localisation)
Shoes 8.5 US / 39 EU     (US-first form, attested in guidance rather than on a board)
Shoe 10                  (US board, bare number — locale implied by the board)
```
Notes on the details a product will get wrong:
- Inch marks on boards are typed as **double apostrophes** (`5' 9''`), not the `"` glyph, on both
  Wilhelmina and Models 1. Half-inches render as `½''`, not `.5`.
- Shoe size on a US board is a **bare integer/half** with no unit. On a UK board it is
  **explicitly labelled UK and EU**. There is no universal shoe number; a single unlabelled shoe
  field is a localisation bug the moment the profile crosses a border.
- Dress/suit sizing is not globally comparable either (US 2 ≈ UK 6 ≈ EU 34 ≈ IT 38 ≈ FR 36).
  I did **not** find an agency board that displayed multi-locale dress sizes — boards display the
  size in their own market's system and expect the reader to convert. Evidence thin; flag as
  unresolved (§6).

### 4.6 What is deliberately withheld

- **Weight.** Absent from every adult fashion board profile I could read (Wilhelmina women,
  Wilhelmina men, Models 1) and absent from the modern comp-card stat lists at Fairway and
  compcard.com. It *is* present in older/generic accounts — Wikipedia's comp card entry still lists
  "height, weight, three sizes…" **[S]** — and in **child** modelling and **actor/commercial**
  resume advice ("name, age, height, weight, hair color, eye color and clothing and shoe sizes")
  **[S ×2]**, and in German consumer-facing sedcard advice ("Alter, Größe, Gewicht…" — casting.de
  **[S]**). **Contested** — see §6. The safe reading: weight is a *legacy/adjacent-market* field
  that mainstream fashion boards have dropped, and putting it on a fashion stat card in 2026 reads
  as either out-of-date or body-surveillance-y.
- **Age / date of birth.** Collected on every application form (Storm, Society, MiLK, Nomad, Want,
  Rule, Elite **[P-agency ×7]**) but **not displayed** in the stats block on adult boards. Kids
  boards are the exception in principle, though the one kids board I could read (Docherty, Kids/Girls)
  displayed **only Height** **[P-agency]**.
- **Bra/cup.** On US boards (`bra 30A`), collected by Elite and Heffner applications, and standard
  on German sedcards for women — but **absent from the Models 1 UK profile**. Regional/agency
  variation, not universal.

### 4.7 Kids and teens

Distinctly different, and the constraint is ethical, not aesthetic:

> "We believe it is inappropriate to measure any young person under the age 18 except for their
> height." — BFMA Best Practice Guide **[P-body]**
> "It is unacceptable to take, send or receive body, bikini or lingerie digitals of any young
> person under the age of 18." — BFMA Code of Practice **[P-body]**

Corroborated on the product side: MiLK's application says "Don't submit any lingerie, swimwear,
or bikini images" **[P-agency]**; Elite's shot list for a division open from age 15 nonetheless
specifies bikini for measuring accuracy — an inconsistency worth noting, not resolving here.
Docherty's Kids board shows **height only** **[P-agency]**. Printer guidance for children's cards
substitutes "height, clothing size, shoe size, hair, eyes, age range" for the B/W/H triple
**[P-print]** — i.e. **age range replaces measurements, and the triple disappears entirely**.

**Product implication:** a stats model must be able to *structurally omit* B/W/H for under-18s,
not merely leave the fields blank, and must never surface a bust/waist/hip capture UI to a minor's
account.

**Are digitals dated?** Not visibly, in any source I found. They are *described* as current
("natural state", "current appearance") and the recency expectation lives in the ask, not in a
stamp on the image. **No agency page in the sample displays a capture date on digitals.** This is
a genuine gap in the evidence, and it matters: a product that stamps "taken 14 Feb" on a digital
is *inventing* a convention, whereas a product that tracks "digitals last updated" as metadata on
the set is consistent with how bookers talk ("send me fresh digitals").

**Do stats accompany digitals?** Yes, always, in the same message. Heffner: if the form fails,
"email your images, measurements, and contact information" **[P-agency]**. Digitals + stats +
a short intro is the atomic unit of a submission **[S ×2]**.

### 4.9 Stats recency

- "check your measurements at least every three months" **[S]**
- "If your comp card is going out today, measure today." **[S]**
- "take current measurements using the method requested by the agency and not estimate or reuse
  old figures" **[S]**
- Agencies verify by measuring in person at the meeting **[P-body BFMA + S]**.

No agency page in the primary sample states a numeric re-measure interval. The 3-month figure is a
coaching convention. Label it as such in any product copy.

## 5. Digital identity precedents — is a "model ID" or Wallet pass a real artefact?

**Short answer: a Wallet-pass "model ID" is not a recognised industry artefact. The nearest real
precedents are (a) a union membership card, and (b) a QR that points at a comp card or portfolio.
An agency-issued digital identity for a model does not appear in any source I found.**

Evidence *for* adjacent precedents:

1. **SAG-AFTRA digital membership card in Apple Wallet.** Real, official, current. "A current
   digital membership card is available to you at all times on your SAG-AFTRA membership app…
   can be used in all circumstances where the physical card can be used", and the member app has
   a button to "add it to your wallet"; the card carries a QR code.
   https://www.sagaftra.org/add-your-membership-card-your-wallet **[P-body]**
   *But note what this is:* a **union membership credential** for performers, proving dues-paid
   status. It is an identity/eligibility document. It is not a portfolio, not a comp card, and
   modelling has no equivalent union card in the US or UK.

2. **QR on/for a comp card.** Attested, but as a *DIY / coaching / QR-vendor* practice, not an
   agency one: "Models, performers, and actors can add QR codes to comp cards, posters, casting
   profiles, and event material to share reels, photos, and booking details" **[S / vendor]**;
   step-by-step DIY guidance to upload a comp card PDF to Dropbox/Drive and generate a QR
   **[S]**. I found **no agency-produced comp card in the sample that carries a QR**, and no
   agency page describing one.

3. **"Digital comp card" as a PDF/link — this one is fully normal.** Not invented at all.
   - Agency software generates them: Syngency's customisable templates cover "the emails,
     invoices, contracts, **comp cards**, and packages sent to your talent and clients"
     **[P-vendor]**.
   - German agency wiki: the A5 sedcard "wird aber bei der Modelagentur inzwischen zunehmend durch
     eine **digitale Version** ergänzt oder ganz ersetzt" — increasingly supplemented or wholly
     replaced by a digital version **[P-agency]**.
   - Printer framing: "a print-ready comp card file used digitally" is standard practice
     **[P-print]**; sedcard24 sells the object as "a double-sided DIN A5 document" **[P-print]**.
   - Secondary: digital comp cards "are easy to share and update", cheaper, and models "send links
     to their modeling portfolio sites" **[S]**.
   So: **PDF/link comp card = universal and expected. Wallet pass = no precedent.**

4. **Casting check-in QR.** I looked for this specifically at Casting Networks and Casting
   Frontier and **found no evidence** of QR/barcode check-in at physical castings. Casting Networks'
   app gives talent "access to their profile, headshots and reels" and handles audition/callback
   requests **[S]**; nothing about on-site scanning. Absence of evidence here, but the absence is
   consistent across two searches.

5. **models.com profile QR.** Could not verify — models.com returns HTTP 403 to this fetcher on
   `/models`, `/newfaces` and individual profiles. Unverified.

**Verdict for product design.** A "digital comp card" (PDF + shareable link) is *the* expected
modern deliverable and naming it that is correct. A **Wallet pass containing a comp card would
read as invented** — the comp card's job is to be looked *at* (images + stats), and a Wallet pass
is a strip of text with a barcode; the affordances don't match, and nobody in the industry does it.
A Wallet pass *could* be defensible as a **credential** ("represented by X, verified") because
SAG-AFTRA establishes that shape for performers — but it would be a new category for modelling,
should never be called a comp card, and should never be called an "ID" unless something is
actually being verified. A QR that resolves to the model's book/comp card is the low-risk,
already-attested form.

## 6. AI and retouching disclosure norms

**The industry's rule for digitals has always been an anti-manipulation rule**, which is why AI
lands hard here. Five of the nine agency pages sampled ban retouching and filters *in the
submission itself* (§4.8) — Wilhelmina names the tools: "FaceTune, Instagram, Snapchat, or other
photo filters will not be considered" **[P-agency]**. Note the asymmetry a product must respect:
**digitals must be unaltered; book images are professionally retouched and that is fine.** A
single global "no retouching" flag across all media would be wrong.

**Statute and industry-body position, 2025–26:**

- **NY Fashion Workers Act** (NY Labor Law Art. 11 amendments; signed 21 Dec 2024, **effective
  19 June 2025**). Requires clear **written consent** for the creation or use of a model's
  **digital replica**, and that consent must state "the scope, purpose, rate of pay, and length of
  time the replica will be used"; for agencies, that consent must be **separate from the
  representation agreement**. **[P-body / legal commentary ×3]**
  https://www.modelalliance.org/fwa
- **NY Synthetic Performer Disclosure Law** — signed 11 Dec 2025, **effective 9 June 2026**:
  conspicuous disclosure required when an advertisement contains a "synthetic performer".
  **[S — legal commentary]** This is the most 2026-current fact in this section.
- **BFMA (UK) Code of Practice** already carries a digital-model-rights clause: "**My digital
  version can only be used within the terms of the booking confirmation agreed by my agency.**"
  **[P-body]** https://bfma.fashion/bfma-code-of-practice/
- **Getty Images contributor policy (May 2025)**: "does not accept photo-realistic, AI-generated
  depictions of people"; retouching tolerated only if "no more than 10% of the pixels" are
  changed, "not adding new elements", and "not retouching models". **[P-vendor]**
  https://contributors.gettyimages.com/article/10847
- **Norm-forming event:** the Guess × Vogue August 2025 AI-model campaign, disclosed only as tiny
  print "Produced by Seraphinne Vallora on AI", drew sustained backlash. The lesson the trade drew
  was explicitly about *disclosure prominence*, not about AI per se. **[S ×4: CNN, Forbes,
  FashionNetwork, DesignRush]**

**What I could not find:** a modelling agency's *published* policy on AI-generated or AI-altered
images in a model's own submission or book. Agencies ban filters and retouching in digitals, which
functionally covers AI beautification, but none of the eleven agency pages I read mentions AI.
Likewise I found **no "unretouched" certification claim** used as a portfolio label anywhere.
Treat "unretouched badge" as an invented affordance until evidenced.

## 7. What would read as wrong in software

Ranked by how loudly a booker would react.

1. **Digitals mixed into the book, or one undifferentiated "photos" bucket.** These are opposite
   objects with opposite rules (unretouched vs retouched; disposable vs curated; agency-eyes vs
   client-eyes). A single gallery collapses the entire mental model.
2. **A comp card with no agency block.** The card *is* the representation relationship. A
   represented model's card showing their personal mobile number is worse than wrong — it routes
   client contact around the agency and around the safeguarding chain.
3. **Exposing a model's personal phone/email on any client-facing artefact while represented.**
   Same issue; printers only recommend personal contact for freelancers, and explicitly never a
   home address or a child's phone.
4. **Bust/waist/hip capture offered to an under-18 account.** BFMA says measuring under-18s beyond
   height is inappropriate; body/bikini/lingerie digitals of minors are "unacceptable". This must be
   a structural omission, not a blank field.
5. **Weight on an adult fashion stat card.** Absent from every board I read. Reads as 1990s, or as
   a commercial/actor resume, or as body policing.
6. **Single-unit stats with no locale.** `Shoe: 8` is meaningless across a border; `Height: 178`
   without cm is ambiguous. Models 1 shows the correct shape: `177.5 CM/5' 10''`, `6 UK / 39 EU`.
   US boards may show inches alone *because the board declares its market* — a global product has
   no such excuse.
7. **Mis-ordered stats.** Height not first; hair/eyes not last; B–W–H split or reordered; men's
   list borrowing bust/hips instead of chest/inseam/collar/suit. The order is the tell.
8. **"Digital business card."** Wrong object, wrong power relationship. Say "comp card" (or
   sedcard/zed card by locale) and, for the shareable version, "digital comp card" — which *is*
   attested.
9. **"Upload a selfie" as an instruction.** "Selfie" appears in agency copy only as a prohibition.
10. **Unlimited book size / append-only portfolio.** The book is 6–20 images and is *edited down*.
    An interface that rewards accumulation teaches the wrong behaviour.
11. **Calling every professional image a "tearsheet".** A tearsheet is *published* work. A test is
    not a tearsheet. Sources are consistent and models are touchy about the distinction.
12. **A "test shoot" feature that behaves like TFP** (model finds photographer, no agency in the
    loop) while using the word "test". Agencies arrange tests; TFP is the freelance thing.
13. **A global "unretouched" toggle.** Correct for digitals, wrong for the book.
14. **Wallet pass presented as a comp card, or an agency-issued "model ID".** No precedent; the
    only comparable credential is a union card. A QR to the book is the attested form.
15. **A capture-date stamp printed on digitals.** No source shows this. Recency belongs in
    metadata and in an "update your digitals" nudge, not burned into the image.
16. **Stats that can drift out of date silently.** Every source frames stats as a claim that will
    be physically verified at the meeting; the product should treat stale stats as a liability,
    with a re-measure prompt (quarterly is the coaching convention — label it as guidance, not law).
