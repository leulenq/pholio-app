The Architecture of
Composure.

Pholio serves people whose work is judged visually, emotionally, and contextually. Every surface we build is in a quiet conversation with that fact — designed not to perform, but to clear space for the person on the page.

Chapter I — The Premise

We do not
design for users.

Pholio's users are not users in the typical sense. They are models, performers, and creatives whose careers turn on how they are seen. Every interface we draw is, in effect, a frame around a person — and the wrong frame can ruin even the right portrait.

Our medium is therefore restraint. We design the way an editor lays out a magazine spread for a model they admire: with deference, with rhythm, with a great deal of white space, and with one or two moments of declared taste. Everything else gets out of the way.

This document is the source of truth. When in doubt, return to it before returning to a screenshot or a Figma frame.

We believe your career should be defined by your face,
not your formatting.

The brand sentence. All other copy descends from this.
Chapter II — Seven Principles

The underlying
logic.

These are the principles we extracted from the parts of the site that already work — the Manifesto, the /talent narrative, the legal pages, the preloader, the footer. They are the rules that produced those moments and the rules that should govern everything that follows.

01
Composure

Composure over
composition.

A model's composure — their stillness, their command of a frame — is the thing the platform exists to showcase. The interface must possess the same quality. It should not bounce, throb, or beg for attention. It should hold the room.

"A confident page does not raise its voice."

Hover
Color shift, not lift. Gold-up, not scale-up.
Motion
Single ease (0.22, 1, 0.36, 1). No bounces.
Density
Spacious. py-32 to py-72 on hero sections.
Borders
1px hairlines. Never thicker. Rarely visible.
02
Earned Emphasis

One italic word, at a time.

Every Pholio headline carries one emphasised word — set in italic Noto Serif Display, coloured gold. Never two. Never three. The italic word is the headline's verdict: the moment the sentence stops describing and starts deciding.

This is the single most distinctive grammar we have. It is also the easiest one to break. Resist the urge to gild a second word. Restraint is what makes the first one land.

Emphasis
Italic serif + gold #C9A55A. Always together.
Frequency
One italic word per heading. No exceptions.
Body emphasis
Inline italic-serif spans. Same gold, same rule.
Never
Bolding for emphasis. Underlines. All-caps shouts.
03
Two Worlds

Ink and cream are moods, not themes.

The site moves between two grounds: velvet ink (#050505) and cream (#FAF7F2). They are not light-and-dark modes; they are two emotional registers, and switching between them is a deliberate dramatic act.

Ink is for conviction — manifestos, beliefs, the preloader, the final close. Cream is for composure — long-form reading, product surfaces, the talent narrative, the agency floor. Gold is the bridge. It belongs to neither and works in both.

Ink #050505
Belief, identity, the dark close. Hero only.
Cream #FAF7F2
Reading, work, the body of the product.
Transition
A hard cut between sections. Not a gradient.
Cadence
Roughly 2 cream : 1 ink across a page.
04
Editorial Rhythm

Pages are scored, not stacked.

The /talent page is built as a narrative score: Arrival, Tension, Release, Proof, Conversion, Myth, Spine, Close. The About page is built as chapters. Sections are movements, not modules — each one has a job in the larger arc and earns its presence by doing it.

This means: no feature parades. No three-up icon grids that say "fast / secure / friendly". When a section can't name its movement, it shouldn't be on the page.

Cadence
Arrival → Tension → Release → Proof → Close.
Section label
Chapter / movement name in gold mono kicker.
Spacing
8rem default. 5rem at small. Never less.
Forbidden
Generic three-up "features" grids.
05
Margin Furniture

The page knows it is printed.

Pholio borrows from print, not from apps. Vertical gold rules, large faint number indices, ghost letterforms, thin top hairlines, uppercase tracked kickers, marginal location captions — these are the page's furniture. They give the eye places to rest and quiet anchors to read against.

The footer's thin gold hairline along the top edge is not decoration. It is the same line that closes a printed page: a final breath before the colophon.

Ghost type
35–80vw, opacity 0.03–0.05, behind content.
Vertical rules
1px gold, 80–120px, scale-in from top.
Index numbers
Serif 84–144px, opacity 0.06–0.10.
Kickers
10–11px, 0.18–0.4em tracking, gold, uppercase.
06
Slow Motion

Motion is arrival, not animation.

Pholio's only motion is the act of arriving — content fading and lifting into place once, on first sight. Word-stagger reveals. A gold underline sweeping in 800ms after the wordmark. A vertical rule scaling down from the top. After arrival, the page is still.

There are no looping animations, no shimmer effects, no carousels that move on their own. The single permitted easing is cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1). Durations live between 0.6s and 1.4s.

Easing
Out-quint only. Every transition.
Duration
0.6s small · 1.0s default · 1.4s hero.
Trigger
In-view, once. Never loop. Never on scroll-tied.
Reduced motion
Honoured. Always. Preloader = 100ms.
07
Plain Specifics

Copy is specific, never sold.

Pholio's voice is editorial and unembellished. The legal pages are the best evidence: they name real vendors (Firebase, Stripe, Groq, Neon, Netlify), real retention windows (30 days, 90 days, 7 years), real legal bases. The marketing voice carries the same posture — declarative, specific, slightly cool, and never enthusiastic.

Tone is quietly authoritative. Not "we built the best portfolio tool." It is "the industry has always had talent. Now it has a standard." If a sentence can be said in fewer words, say it in fewer words. If it can be said without an exclamation point, it must be.

Voice
Editorial, declarative, slightly cool.
Person
"We" + "you". Never "users". Never "folks".
No
Emoji. Exclamations. Marketing superlatives.
Yes
Real numbers. Real names. Specific verbs.

Chapter V — The Score

A page is a movement.

When laying out a Pholio page, score it. Name each beat before drawing it. If a section can't be named — Arrival, Tension, Release, Proof, Myth, Spine, Close — it should not exist. This is how /talent and /about-us were built, and why they hold together.

I
Arrival
A hero on ink. Ghost type at the back. One italic gold word in the headline. The reader meets the page.
II
Tension
The room before. The inbox, the hallway, the friction we exist to remove. Stated plainly.
III
Release
The frictions struck through. A negative reel. Cream after ink, or ink after cream — the page exhales.
IV
Proof
A contact sheet of work. Real names. Real numbers. Not a logo wall — a sheet of evidence.
V
Myth
The lexicon. Words we use the way the industry doesn't. A glossary that earns the rest.
VI
Spine
Three editorial beats — marginalia, not matrices. The product, in language, in order.
VII
Signal
Analytics, depth, social proof. Who leaned in, and what that means.
VIII
Close
A final ink section. The vertical gold rule. Established 2024. The reader is released, but not abandoned.
Chapter VI — What Would Break It

The shapes Pholio
refuses.

Pholio is a platform for people whose careers are judged on surfaces. We owe them an interface that is not embarrassing to be next to. The list below is what we will not do — not because the patterns are wrong elsewhere, but because they are wrong here.

✕ We do not
  • Use purple-blue SaaS gradients. No "AI vibes" backgrounds. Gold radial glows only, and only at <10% opacity.
  • Stack feature cards with coloured left borders. No three-up "fast/secure/easy" grids. If it can't be a chapter, it isn't one.
  • Use emoji. Anywhere. Not in copy, not in icons, not in empty states. The wordmark and the gold rule are our character set.
  • Animate on a loop. No shimmer, no breathing buttons, no auto-rotating carousels. Motion fires once on arrival and dies.
  • Bold for emphasis in headlines. Italic-serif-gold is the only stress. Bold is a body-copy concession, never a brand voice.
  • Use rounded buttons larger than 999px. Editorial buttons are 2px corners or pill — never both, never in the same view.
  • Photograph imagery in saturated colour. Grayscale, or grayscale that resolves to colour on hover. Warm grain over cool sharpness.
  • Write "users". They are talent and agencies. They have names.
✓ We do
  • Cut hard between ink and cream. A section break is a stage direction. Treat it like one.
  • Earn each italic word. Only one per headline. The italic word is the sentence's verdict.
  • Set type at editorial scale. Display sizes are big enough to read across a room. Body copy is generous, 300-weight, with breath.
  • Use mono for timestamps and labels. Document dates, established years, chapter numbers, micro-eyebrows — all JetBrains Mono.
  • Write the way our legal pages are written. Named. Numbered. Plain. Specific. Trustworthy because exact.
  • Frame imagery, not crop it. 4:5 portraits in 2px-radius rectangles, with grain, with hairline. The image is the subject.
  • Let pages breathe. 8rem section padding is the floor, not the ceiling. White space is composure made visible.
  • Honour the brief from the reader's side. If a model wouldn't put it in their portfolio, we shouldn't put it on theirs.
Chapter VII — Curated for Talent

The reader is
the subject.

Pholio serves people whose work is judged the moment they walk in the room. They are professionals at being looked at, and they have strong, specific taste about how their image is framed. An interface that treats them like generic SaaS users — confetti, gradients, cheerful emoji empty states — will lose them in the first scroll.

Our reader has seen the inside of an agency office, a contact sheet, a tear sheet, a comp card, a printed casting list. The visual vocabulary that earns their trust is editorial, not app-y. We borrow from Vogue Italia, from CAA's letterhead, from a well-laid casting brief — not from Slack, not from Notion, not from a B2B onboarding wizard.

When in doubt: would a model put this on their own portfolio? If the answer is no, we should not put it on ours.

This is also why the legal pages matter aesthetically. A model who reads the privacy policy and finds it specific, well-set, and unhurried trusts the rest of the platform a little more.

Closing — The Brief Going Forward

What this document
does.

This is the source of truth for Pholio design. The previous design system pass should be considered a draft against this document — anywhere the two disagree, this document wins.

Future work — the app, the agency portal, sales materials, comp cards, email templates, error states, settings screens, even the favicon — should be derived from these principles before any reference is taken from a screenshot or a competitor.

The order of operations is:

  1. 01 Name the movement the section is playing.
  2. 02 Choose ink or cream. The mood is the choice.
  3. 03 Write the headline. Earn the one italic word.
  4. 04 Add furniture: a kicker, a ghost letter, a vertical rule.
  5. 05 Set body copy at 300-weight, with breath, with specifics.
  6. 06 Animate the arrival once. Leave it alone after.
